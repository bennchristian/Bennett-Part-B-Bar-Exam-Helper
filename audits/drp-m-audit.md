# drp-m audit — Arbitration and Mediation Advocacy

Read-only audit, 18 Nov 2026. Nothing under `study/`, `tools/` or `Bar Exam/` was changed. Known gaps being filled by other sessions are not re-reported.

**Method.** The Examiner's Arbitration Study Notes (11 Aug 2026), both Contact Session handouts, the Learning Plans and the five Mediation Advocacy slide decks were extracted (`pdftotext -layout`) and split between four subagents; each listed every discrete item (statute section, rule, named case, limb of a test, handout point) and searched `cheatsheets.md` and `coverage.md` for it. **Core/non-core:** the Study Notes carry no non-core labels (blue text in the PDFs is only hyperlinks). Passages marked "by way of background", footnoted comparative material, textbook/Learning-Plan optional reading and the soft-law instruments (read by the Learning Plan as background) are treated as non-core. The Mediation decks are one uniform colour with nothing marked FYI or optional, so all Mediation items are core. Counts are the subagents' and approximate (item granularity differs between them). I did not independently re-verify each hit.

### (b) THIN and MISSING items (core first)

### CORE

1. MISSING. SN para 214(a)-(e), p67-68, the approach for deciding whether a tribunal resolved an issue finally and conclusively (*ONGC Petri v DL E&C* [2023] SGHC 197 at [35]).
   - Substance over form.
   - The tribunal's own description is relevant but not conclusive.
   - The test is how a reasonable recipient would view the decision.
   - Objective attributes: description, formality of language, level of reasoning detail.
   - Background and context matter.
   - Searched "ONGC", "reasonable recipient", "Petri": 0 hits in cheatsheets.md and coverage.md. Block 0-131 (finality) and 0-129 (award definition) give only s 19B and functus officio. A "which are accurate" MCQ on how to tell if a decision is final cannot be answered from the pack.
   - Suggested home: 0-131 or 0-129.

2. MISSING. SN para 245, p77, 2nd bullet: the court hearing a challenge may "vary parts of the award".
   - Block 0-151 lists only set aside in whole or part, refuse, and suspend/remit. "Vary" appears nowhere, and there is no source note explaining its omission.
   - Searched "vary parts", "may vary", "vary the award".
   - The omission may be deliberate, because Art 34 gives no power to vary. If so, the pack should say so in a Conflict note, so a limb saying "the court may vary" is not wrongly rejected or accepted.

3. THIN. SN paras 289-290, p89, the three ways a public policy challenge is raised.
   - (1) The remedies ordered are contrary to public policy.
   - (2) The award gives effect to an illegal or tainted underlying contract.
   - (3) How the award was procured is contrary to public policy.
   - Para 290 adds that category 2 is the commonest, and the reviewing court must first decide whether to reopen the illegality issue.
   - 0-165 covers category 2 (the AJU/CBX matrix) and category 3 (GNC v ONI, deliberate falsity). It never states the three-way classification, and category 1 (remedies) is absent.

4. THIN. SN para 236, p75, the practical distinction between ultra petita and infra petita.
   - Ultra petita needs the court to scrutinise the pleadings, list of issues, openings, evidence and closings.
   - Infra petita is a sui generis natural justice breach and needs far less enquiry (Palm Grove [27]; CDM v CDP; Maritima v Pacific Richfield).
   - 0-144 says only "looking mainly at whether the issue was pleaded". Searched "sui generis", "list of issues": 0 hits. The cases are missing.

5. THIN. SN para 195, p58, the definition of an ASI.
   - It is an order by a court with personal jurisdiction over a party that the party must not file, or must not proceed with, a foreign claim.
   - The concern is frustration of the forum's proceedings, or bad-faith harassment.
   - Searched "personal jurisdiction". Blocks 0-122 and 0-124 give the grant test and comity, but not the definition.

6. THIN. SN para 269 first bullet, p83, incapacity under s 31(2)(a) / Art V(1)(a) / Art 36(1)(a)(i).
   - The SN says incapacity "at the time the agreement was made". 0-159 omits the timing.
   - Searched "at the time the agreement".
   - A limb such as "incapacity arising after the agreement" would not be answerable.

7. THIN. SN para 256, p80, the third strand of *Hulley v Russia* [2025] SGHC(I) 19.
   - A state's consent to arbitrate is a private act, so no consideration "trumps" the estoppel.
   - 0-164 gives the binding-authority point and the de novo point, but not this one.
   - Searched "private act".

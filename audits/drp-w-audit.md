# drp-w audit — Civil Litigation and Written Advocacy

Read-only audit, 18 Nov 2026. Nothing under `study/`, `tools/` or `Bar Exam/` was changed. Known gaps being filled by other sessions (the 8 background-only CL rows in `drp-w-cl`) are not re-reported.

**Method.** Civil Litigation: the 2026 Detailed Syllabus and Learning Plan were extracted with `pdftotext -layout` and every named case, statute section and ROC Order/Rule outside the "Background information only" paragraphs was searched for in `prewrites.md` and `outline.md` (scripted, then spot-read; counts are approximate). Written Advocacy: the five Study Notes, the Worked Examples and the Learning Plan were read in full by a subagent, item by item, and each item searched in `templates.md` and `coverage.md`. The Syllabus marks non-core content with the words "Background information only"; the WA notes carry no formal core/non-core label, so the Learning Plan's optional reading and any passage the notes call optional are treated as non-core.

## Most important gaps

1. **WA: 5 of the 17 fallacies in SN5 have no row in template 1-3** (distinction without a difference, negative proof, ad populum, gambler's fallacy, false cause); composition, division and denying the antecedent are named only. Q1-3 tests just 4 fallacies.
2. **WA: O 6 r 7(6)** (a defence that only disputes jurisdiction is not a submission to it) is never stated, though SN4 Annex 3 ¶6 turns on it. Belongs in 1-27.
3. **CL: SCJA ss 17B–17C and ss 20–22 are not cited anywhere.** Coverage row 0.I.6 says appellate jurisdiction is "covered with appeals in 0.XII", but ss 20–22 never appear. Syllabus [3(a)(ii)], [3(b)].
4. **CL: State Courts Act ss 3, 25, 26, 28, 29 (syllabus [4]) and Order 20 r 1 (syllabus [157(b)]) are missing**; the pack cites only ss 2, 19, 22, 23, 31, 52 SCA.
5. **WA: the SN2 analysis tools are thin** — Good/Bad/Neutral fact is one clause (no rule that a Bad Fact must be addressed), "case theory" is used but never defined, and the fact / opinion / assumption / conjecture / inference distinction (SN2 ¶22) is absent.

### Question (30 marks, 36 minutes)

Priya is a legal practitioner of 12 years' standing and the sole proprietor of Priya Law,
a general practice.

**Part A.** Mr Goh, 81, a widower and retired hawker, has been Priya's client for years.
His estate is worth about $900,000, mostly his HDB flat, and Priya is acting for him on its
sale. At a meeting about the sale, Mr Goh says that Priya's daughter Mei, a law student,
has visited him every week since his wife died, and that he wants to make a new will
leaving Mei $300,000. He asks Priya to draft it "since you already know all my affairs".
Priya replies: "That's very kind of you. I can draft it for you — you may want to see
another lawyer, but honestly it's not necessary." She drafts the will, and Mr Goh signs it
at Priya Law the next day.

A week later, Mr Goh's nephew Kelvin, the main beneficiary under Mr Goh's previous will,
telephones Priya. Kelvin has no lawyer. He has heard that his uncle changed his will and
asks whether he is still getting anything. Priya replies: "Don't worry, I'm looking after
the whole family's interests. The new will is fair to everyone and your share is only a
bit smaller."

**(a) Discuss and evaluate Priya's conduct in Part A. (15 marks)**

**Part B.** Priya also acts for Arun, who is charged with cheating his employer of
$40,000. Priya has known Arun's family for many years. On a prison visit before trial,
Arun tells her: "Yes, I took the money, but they'll never prove it. At trial I'll testify
that I never touched it." Arun cannot raise bail, so Priya offers to stand as his surety
herself.

**(b) Advise Priya on (i) the bail offer, (ii) whether she may continue to act for Arun,
and (iii) how she must conduct the trial, including if Arun insists on testifying that he
never took the money. (15 marks)**

Please give detailed reasons, with reference to relevant rules, practice directions,
principles and/or case law.

### Drill 2 (H1) — Marcus Lee and the Orchid suit (50 marks, 60 minutes)

1. Marcus Lee is a litigation partner of Lee & Partners LLC (LPL), with 20 years' standing.
LPL acts for Orchid Holdings Pte Ltd (Orchid), the claimant in a High Court suit against
Vantage Pte Ltd (Vantage) for $5 million. Vantage is represented by Grace Tan of Tan Chambers
LLC.

2. Orchid's discovery application was fixed for hearing in chambers before an Assistant
Registrar (AR) at 10am, in a list of six matters. Marcus arrived at 10.40am without warning,
after the AR had stood the matter down twice. When it was called, Marcus did not tell the AR
that he expected to argue for about two hours, and began at once, while three other
practitioners waited with short applications to adjourn their matters by consent. Marcus had
brought Orchid's CEO, Mr Wong, into chambers with him without telling Grace beforehand; when
Grace objected, the AR asked Mr Wong to leave. When the AR dismissed the application, Marcus
said: "With respect, this is the kind of ruling one gets when the court has not read the
papers," turned his back on the AR and began packing his files while the AR was still
speaking.

3. At a later hearing of Vantage's application for an interim injunction against Orchid, the
Judge indicated that she was minded to grant it. To avoid the injunction, Marcus said:
"I give my personal undertaking to the court that my firm holds the original title deeds to
Orchid's Jurong warehouse and will not release them without the court's permission." The Judge
accepted the undertaking and dismissed the application. Marcus had not checked. He assumed LPL
still held the deeds from Orchid's purchase in 2019. A week later his secretary told him that
LPL had released them to Orchid's bank in 2024 when the warehouse was mortgaged. Marcus did
nothing.

4. That evening, Marcus emailed the Judge's personal assistant, asking that the email be put
before the Judge: "Further to today's hearing, the Court may wish to know that Vantage's
director was investigated for fraud in 2021. I also attach Tan Chambers' letter of 3 October
offering $2 million to settle, which shows that Vantage knows it is liable." The letter was
headed "Without Prejudice". Marcus did not copy the email to Grace.

5. The next day Marcus posted on LinkedIn: "Vantage's defence in the Orchid suit is a sham,
and any judge who reads the documents will see it. Trial next month."

6. Marcus's former associate, Nadia, is now a Deputy Public Prosecutor. She is prosecuting
Raj, Vantage's former finance manager, for criminal breach of trust of $80,000 in the State
Courts. Raj is unrepresented. Nadia holds a statement from a warehouse clerk, Siti, saying that
Raj had his manager's permission to move the money; she decides not to call Siti and does not
tell Raj about her. A prosecution witness, Mr Ho, testifies that Raj never returned any money,
although Mr Ho's earlier statement to the police says Raj returned $30,000; Nadia says
nothing. Raj is convicted. At sentencing, Nadia tells the court that Raj "also faces two
pending charges of a similar nature", and does not mention that Raj made full restitution
last month, which she knows. She has also noticed that the charge gives the wrong year for the
offence, and decides to let it pass because "the defence has not raised it".

**1.1 Discuss and evaluate Marcus's conduct at paragraph 2. (15 marks)**

**1.2 Discuss and evaluate Marcus's conduct at paragraphs 3 to 5, and advise him what he must
now do. (20 marks)**

**1.3 Discuss and evaluate Nadia's conduct at paragraph 6. (15 marks)**

Please give detailed reasons, with reference to relevant rules, practice directions,
principles and/or case law.

### Drill 3 (H2) — Daniel Koh, Rachel Ong and Crest Law (50 marks, 60 minutes)

1. Daniel Koh is a partner of Hart Law LLP (Hart Law). In 2024, before joining Hart Law,
Daniel acted as the mediator, appointed through a mediation centre, in a mediation between
Sunrise Builders Pte Ltd (Sunrise) and Ming Fabrication Pte Ltd (Ming) over late payments
on Sunrise's Tampines project. During the mediation Ming's director disclosed, in confidence,
that Ming had serious cash-flow problems and was relying on one major customer. The mediation
failed.

2. In 2026 Sunrise instructed Hart Law to sue Ming for $800,000 for defective steelwork on the
same Tampines project. Daniel took conduct of the suit. He told Sunrise's CFO that "Ming can't
afford a long fight; I know that from the mediation, so we should press hard for early
judgment."

3. Rachel Ong of Ong & Co wrote to Daniel confirming that she acted for Ming and would file
its defence. The time for filing the defence ran out on Thursday 15 October. At 6pm on
Friday 16 October Daniel emailed Rachel: "We intend to enter default judgment." At 10am on
Monday 19 October he entered default judgment.

4. Daniel then wrote to Rachel: "Please produce Ming's warrant to act. We are not satisfied
that you are instructed." Rachel replied in writing that she was authorised to act for Ming.
Daniel insisted on seeing the warrant and gave no reason.

5. Daniel suspected that Crest Law LLC (Crest Law), which acts for several of Sunrise's
competitors, paid contractors for referrals. He engaged a private investigator to pose as a
contractor and ask Crest Law's partner, Tan Wei Ling, whether Crest Law would pay him 10% of
the fees on any client he brought. Wei Ling said no. The investigator returned twice, offering
five clients, and on the third visit Wei Ling agreed. Daniel plans to use the recording in a
complaint to the Law Society.

6. Ming applied to set aside the default judgment. Its supporting affidavit, drafted by Rachel
and sworn by Ming's director, alleges that Daniel "deliberately ambushed Ming" and "falsified
the time stamp on his notice email". Rachel sent Daniel a one-line letter enclosing the
affidavit at 9am on the day she filed it at 4pm.

7. Meanwhile Sunrise's CFO, unhappy with Daniel, consulted Tan Wei Ling for a second opinion.
Wei Ling told the CFO: "Daniel's handling has been amateurish. Switch to us and we'll do it for
30% less — just sign here." The next day, before Sunrise had told Hart Law anything, Wei Ling
wrote to the court as "solicitors for Sunrise" asking for the setting-aside hearing to be
vacated. Hart Law was still on the record.

**2.1 Discuss and evaluate Daniel's conduct at paragraphs 1 to 5. (30 marks)**

**2.2 Discuss and evaluate the conduct of Rachel at paragraph 6 and of Wei Ling at
paragraph 7. (20 marks)**

Please give detailed reasons, with reference to relevant rules, practice directions,
principles and/or case law.

### Drill 4 (H3) — Benjamin Chua and Madam Lim (50 marks, 60 minutes)

1. Benjamin Chua is the sole proprietor of Chua & Co, a small private-client practice. He
forgot to renew his practising certificate, which expired on 31 March 2026. He went on seeing
clients and renewed it on 15 June 2026.

2. Madam Lim, 72, a wealthy widow, has been Benjamin's client for ten years on her property and
estate matters. In May 2026 Benjamin told her that BC Interiors Pte Ltd (BCI), a company in
which his wife holds 60% of the shares, needed a short-term loan. Madam Lim agreed to lend BCI
$250,000 at 3% interest. Benjamin drafted the loan agreement and Madam Lim signed it at his
office. He told her: "There's no need for another lawyer; it's a family arrangement." BCI has
repaid $50,000.

3. Also in May, Benjamin borrowed $30,000 personally from another client, Mr Ravi, for whom he
was acting on a lease. Mr Ravi is the managing director and majority owner of a licensed
moneylending company, but lent the money from his own savings. Benjamin gave him an IOU.

4. In July, an officer of the Commercial Affairs Department (CAD) telephoned Benjamin about an
investigation into the company of Madam Lim's late husband. The officer asked him to email
"all your files on Madam Lim and the company for the last ten years" by the end of the week.
Benjamin, wanting to be cooperative, emailed the officer the complete files, including his
advice letters to Madam Lim on her husband's estate.

5. In August the Comptroller of Income Tax sent Benjamin a written notice under s 65B of the
Income Tax Act 1947 requiring him to provide the advice he gave Madam Lim in 2023 on the
transfer of her husband's shares into a family trust. Benjamin sent IRAS the advice letters
the next day. He did not tell Madam Lim about either request.

6. Madam Lim's daughter, Grace, who is not a client, then asked Benjamin for a copy of her
mother's will "so the family can plan ahead". Benjamin replied that he would send it once
the tax matter was over.

**3.1 Discuss and evaluate Benjamin's conduct at paragraphs 1 to 3. (25 marks)**

**3.2 Discuss and evaluate Benjamin's conduct at paragraphs 4 to 6, and advise him how he
should have dealt with each request. (25 marks)**

Please give detailed reasons, with reference to relevant rules, practice directions,
principles and/or case law.

### Drill 5 (H4) — Wendy Goh, Alvin and Homesmart (50 marks, 60 minutes)

1. Wendy Goh, of five years' standing, is the sole proprietor of Faith Law, a conveyancing
and landlord-and-tenant practice. Its only other lawyer is Sam, an associate of two years'
standing. Alvin is a practice trainee who has passed Part B and is waiting to be called.
Alvin's training contract named a senior consultant as his supervising solicitor, but the
consultant left Faith Law in March 2026 and nobody else was appointed.

2. Since March, Alvin has run most of the firm's HDB resale files alone. He takes buyers'
instructions, advises them on their options to purchase, and signs letters to other firms
and to HDB as "Alvin Tan, Solicitor, Faith Law". He also wrote a letter of demand to a
tenant, Mr Kok, on the firm's letterhead, signed the same way. Wendy reads none of his
letters, saying she trusts him.

3. Wendy agreed orally with Homesmart Realty Pte Ltd (Homesmart) that its agents would
recommend Faith Law to buyers. Faith Law pays Homesmart "$300 marketing support" for each
completed file. Homesmart's flyer says: "Buy with Homesmart — FREE legal fees with our partner
law firm!" Its agents tell buyers that the 0.5% agency-commission discount is available only
if they use Faith Law.

4. Faith Law acted for a landlord, Mr Tan, against Mr Kok for rent arrears. Mr Kok paid the
$18,000 settlement sum to Faith Law. Alvin paid it into the office account, "because Mr Tan
owes us fees anyway". There was no fee agreement in writing and no bill had been delivered.
Three weeks later Wendy noticed and moved the $18,000 to the client account. The same day
Sam alone signed a cheque drawing $6,000 from the client account for Faith Law's fees, and
Wendy emailed Mr Tan: "We have received the settlement and taken our fees." Faith Law has no
approved book-keeper.

5. Zenith Holdings Ltd (Zenith), a BVI company, instructed Faith Law to buy a $6 million
condominium unit. Zenith's director, who gave instructions by email only, refused to name
Zenith's owner, saying only that it was held "for a family trust". The option fee came from
an unconnected company in a jurisdiction on the FATF's list of jurisdictions under increased
monitoring. Alvin opened the file and banked the option fee. Wendy told him: "Conveyancing is
low risk. Carry on."

6. Alvin is about to apply for admission. He asks Wendy whether he needs to mention that he
signed letters as "Solicitor". Wendy says: "No, nobody checks those things."

**4.1 Discuss and evaluate the conduct of Wendy and Alvin at paragraphs 1, 2 and 6, and advise
Alvin on his admission application. (15 marks)**

**4.2 Discuss and evaluate Faith Law's arrangement with Homesmart at paragraph 3. (15 marks)**

**4.3 Discuss and evaluate Faith Law's conduct at paragraphs 4 and 5. (20 marks)**

Please give detailed reasons, with reference to relevant rules, practice directions,
principles and/or case law.

### Drill 6 question (30 marks, 36 minutes)

Lim & Partners is a 15-lawyer firm. Grace is an equity partner of 20 years' standing; Ravi is
a litigation associate of 4 years' standing who works under her.

**Part A.** Ravi acts for Orion Pte Ltd, which is suing its supplier, Kestrel Pte Ltd, for
delivering machine parts that did not meet the agreed specification. The court orders both
sides to produce all documents relevant to whether the specification was varied. Ravi emails
the order and the relevant Rules of Court to Orion's general manager, Mr Tan, and asks him to
"send whatever you have". Mr Tan sends 40 emails and says: "That's everything." Several of
them refer to "my email of 3 March agreeing the revised spec", but no email of 3 March is
among them. Ravi does not ask about it.

A week before the list of documents is due, Mr Tan calls Ravi. He admits that on 3 March he
emailed Kestrel agreeing to a looser specification, and that he deleted the chain from his
inbox because "it kills our case". He says: "Leave it out of the list. Kestrel's director
left the company last year, so they can't prove it exists." Ravi files the list without the
3 March email.

While going through Orion's documents, Ravi also found emails suggesting that Kestrel's
director paid Orion's procurement officer a bribe to win the original contract. Mr Tan asks
Ravi whether he now has to report this to the police.

**(a) Discuss and evaluate Ravi's conduct in Part A, and advise him on what he must do now,
including on the bribery emails. (15 marks)**

**Part B.** Grace has acted for Mrs Chua, 76, since Mrs Chua's husband died two years ago,
including on the grant of probate and the administration of his estate. Mrs Chua's assets are
about $600,000. Siti, Grace's legal secretary, has helped Mrs Chua with forms and
appointments throughout. Mrs Chua now wants to give Siti her late husband's Rolex, worth about
$45,000, and $20,000 in cash "as a thank-you, while I'm still around to see her enjoy it", and
asks Grace to prepare a deed of gift. Grace replies: "That's very generous, and Siti deserves
it. No need to involve another lawyer for something this simple." Grace prepares the deed and
Mrs Chua signs it at the firm, with Siti as one of the witnesses.

At Chinese New Year Mrs Chua also sends Grace a hamper worth about $150.

**(b) Discuss and evaluate Grace's conduct in Part B, and advise whether she may keep the
hamper. (15 marks)**

Please give detailed reasons, with reference to relevant rules, practice directions,
principles and/or case law.

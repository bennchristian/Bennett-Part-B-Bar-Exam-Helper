## 0. How the paper is tested

**Format.** Long fact-pattern questions split into sub-questions with marks (e.g. 50 + 50,
PYP23 p2–4; 40 + 30 + 30, PYP21 p2–5). 2022 also had a 20-MCQ Section A (PYP22 p1), but the 2026
paper is "1 Written Paper" (HB p8, §7.14) with no MCQ section (PRD §3, citing Opening
Brief slide 45).

**What earns marks** — more than issue-spotting (INTRO2 p25):
1. Identify the issue / concern.
2. Cite the PCR rule(s), PD(s), GN(s).
3. Cite and discuss case law.
4. Explain the competing ethical considerations and any exceptions.
5. Apply to the facts.
6. **Conclude whether there is a breach** — and, for conflicts, whether the lawyer may act
   and what safeguards are needed (INTRO2 p26).

For conflicts specifically: explain *why* a conflict exists on the facts, weigh the
competing interests, distinguish a PCR conflict from a mere commercial conflict, check the
exceptions, then advise whether the lawyer may act and on what safeguards (INTRO2 p26).

**The examiners' own answer** (SQ p109–113, model answer to PYP23 Q1) is the template:
quote the rule's limbs → map each limb to a fact → run the exceptions in order → cite 2–4
cases with one-line ratios → practical safeguards → one-line conclusion. Extra marks go to
the non-obvious step (there: Betty must get M's consent before Archie can even tell L that M
is bidding, because of confidentiality — SQ p111).

## 1. Regulation and the client relationship

### 1.1 Who is a client; implied retainer
- "Client" (LPA s 2(1)) covers anyone who retains or is *about to* retain a solicitor, and
  anyone liable to pay the costs (L1 p5).
- A retainer can be **implied** from conduct: *LSS v Ahmad Khalis* [2006] 4 SLR(R) 308 at
  [64]–[73]; *Anwar Patrick Adrian v Ng Chong & Hue LLC* [2014] 3 SLR 761 at [49]–[61];
  cf *LSS v Lee Suet Fern* [2020] SGHC 255 at [57]–[69] (L1 p6; SYL p2).
- Even without a retainer, a duty of care may be owed to third parties at common law —
  *AEL v Cheoh Yeoh & Associates* [2014] 3 SLR 1231; *Anwar Patrick Adrian* (L1 p7).
- *Lee Suet Fern*: no solicitor–client relationship was found, but her conduct over her
  father-in-law's will was still misconduct unbefitting an advocate and solicitor — she
  represented a critical document when in no position to, knowing her husband benefited
  (L3 p24–25).
- **Exam use:** a former prospective client who only sought advice may still be protected —
  *Harsha Rajkumar Mirpuri* [2018] SGHC 155 (L2 p21) — see §2.2.

### 1.2 Authority and the warrant to act
- If instructions purport to come from someone on the client's behalf: ensure that person
  has authority, or get the client's confirmation within a reasonable time (r 5(5), PCR
  p11–12). Agent instructions: *Fong Maun Yee v Yoong Weng Ho Robert* [1997] 1 SLR(R) 751
  (SYL p2).
- Warrant to act / letter of engagement: PD 7.4.3; *Tung Hui Mannequin v Tenet Insurance*
  [2005] 3 SLR(R) 184 (L1 p11). Acting without authority → breach of warranty of
  authority, wasted costs, discipline (L1 p12).
- Extra caution when the client may lack mental capacity (wills) — *Low Ah Cheow v Ng
  Hock Guan* [2009] 3 SLR(R) 1079; *Chee Mu Lin Muriel* [2010] 4 SLR 373 (L1 p9). Duty to
  keep acting in the client's best interests after capacity is impaired (r 5(4), PCR p11).

### 1.3 Limiting the retainer and liability
- **Limited retainer**: same professional standard as a full retainer; r 6, rr 20–22,
  r 17 and r 26 all still apply (GN 7.4.1 p1, paras 5–6).
- **Limiting liability**: the Council does not object to limiting liability, but not below
  the minimum professional indemnity cover ($1m law firm; $1m–$2m law corporation or LLP
  depending on structure); liability for **fraud or reckless disregard** of professional
  obligations cannot be limited; courts won't enforce an unfair agreement (PD 7.1.2 p1).
- In a contentious-business costs agreement, a term that the solicitor is not liable for
  negligence, or is relieved of professional responsibility, is **wholly void**
  (LPA s 112(5), LPA p271).
- **Tested:** PYP21 Q3.4 — cap of $50,000 covering "negligence, fraud …" → fails on all
  three counts above.

### 1.4 Honesty, competence, diligence (r 5)
- Principles: honesty; requisite knowledge, skill and experience; diligence (r 5(1),
  PCR p10).
- Duties (r 5(2), PCR p10–11): (a) honest in all dealings; (b) tell the client all
  information that may reasonably affect their interests (except where confidentiality to
  someone else overrides, or the client agreed in writing); (c) reasonable diligence and
  competence; (d) have the skills for each matter; (e)–(h) keep informed, respond, keep
  appointments, timely advice; (i) follow lawful, proper, reasonable instructions;
  (j) use all legal means to advance the client's interests; (k) **keep contemporaneous
  records** of instructions and advice.
- The terms of engagement are taken into account (r 5(3), PCR p11).
- Cases (L1 p21–25): honesty — *LSS v Ng Bock Hoh Dixon* [2012] 1 SLR 348; *LSS v Ng Chee
  Sing* [2000] 1 SLR(R) 466. Disclosure — *Su Ah Tee v Allister Lim* [2014] SGHC 159.
  Competence — *LSS v K Jayakumar Naidu* [2012] 4 SLR 1232. Communication — *LSS v Chiong
  Chin May Selena* [2013] SGHC 5; *LSS v Udeh Kumar* [2013] 3 SLR 875. Records — *Lie
  Hendri Rusli* [2004] 4 SLR(R) 594 at [36], [63]–[64].
- Hiding your own negligence from the client and carrying on acting: *LSS v Govindan Balan
  Nair* [2020] 5 SLR 988 (L3 p13) — also r 22.
- **Tested:** PYP21 Q1.3 — getting the client to sign a waiver of claims on a false
  pretext ("insurer reporting") → r 5(2)(a) dishonesty, r 22 (own interest vs client),
  r 8(3)(b)/deceit; also r 6(3)(d) only lets you disclose *to* your insurer, it creates no
  reporting duty to the client. No official answer exists; checked 18 Nov 2026 against the
  text of r 5(2)(a) and r 6(3)(d) PCR (p10, p12–13), which bear this out (0-3, 0-14;
  2021 Q1.3 in `model-answers.md`).

### 1.5 Confidentiality (r 6)
- Must not knowingly disclose information confidential to the client and acquired (from
  anyone) in the course of the engagement (r 6(2), PCR p12).
- **Five exceptions** (r 6(3), PCR p12–13): (a) client authorises; (b) required/permitted
  by law, court or tribunal; (c) in confidence, to get advice on your own legal/ethical
  obligations; (d) in confidence to your PI insurer/broker about a claim or complaint;
  (e) to defend a charge or complaint about your conduct.
- Duty attaches only if the information is confidential in nature — *LSS v Ravi s/o
  Madasamy* [2015] 3 SLR 1187 at [33] (L1 p28).
- Privilege covers all communications to the lawyer in professional capacity, even with
  no case in contemplation (PD 9.1.3, summarised L1 p29).
- **Confidentiality drives conflicts:** you may not even tell client A that B has
  approached you without B's consent (SQ p111).

### 1.6 Ending the retainer (r 26)
- Duties of honesty, competence, diligence last until the retainer ends; must complete
  agreed work (r 26(1)–(2), PCR p48).
- Dissolution of the law practice ends the relationship (r 26(3), PCR p48).
- **Must** withdraw (r 26(4), PCR p48): (a) client discharges you; (b) you read another
  party's document that came by improper channels and would be embarrassed by knowing it.
- **May** withdraw (r 26(5), PCR p48–50): (a) reasonable notice; (b) no significant harm +
  client fully informed and voluntarily assents; (c) serious risk to your health;
  (d) unpaid invoice / breach of fee or conduct agreement; (e) material misrepresentation
  by client; (f) needed to avoid breaching LPA, PCR, PDs; (g) serious loss of confidence;
  (h) other good cause.
- On withdrawal (r 26(6), PCR p50): take reasonable care to avoid foreseeable harm —
  reasonable notice, time to find a new lawyer, cooperate with the new lawyer; abide by
  the client's choice of replacement.
- **Lien** (r 26(7), PCR p50): if a new lawyer B acts, A must release the documents to B if
  B undertakes to hold them subject to the lien and gives reasonable security, or B offers
  to pay/arrange payment of A's costs on completion and A accepts; otherwise release to
  the client on reasonable security.
- Cases (SYL p4): *Chew Kim Kee v Kertar & Co* [2004] SGHC 95; *Alfons Tanumihardja v Thio
  Su Mien* [2005] 2 SLR(R) 445; *Chiong Chin May Selena* [2013] SGHC 5.
- **Tested:** PYP21 Q3.3 — "may terminate with immediate effect for any reason" → clashes
  with r 26(5)(a) reasonable notice and r 26(6)(a) duty to avoid harm. PYP22 (c) — Luna
  received documents under an undertaking to protect Top Law's lien, then handed them to
  the next firm without protecting it → breach of r 7(6) (§5.2); she should have obtained
  Dire Law's matching undertaking/security (r 26(7)(a)) or kept the documents.

## 2. Conflicts of interest

**First step in any conflict answer: which rule?**

| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Acting for 2+ parties in one matter/transaction | r 20 |
| New client adverse to a former client, and you hold the former client's confidential info | r 21 |
| Your (or your family's, or the firm's) own interest vs the client's | r 22 (+ r 23–25) |
| Your relationship with client or court; you'll be a material witness | r 11 |
| You prepared a document for 2+ parties and they now dispute it | r 20(8) — absolute bar |

### 2.1 Two or more clients (r 20)
- Principle: loyalty and confidentiality owed to each client; act prudently to avoid
  compromising the relationship (r 20(1), PCR p35).
- **Trigger** (r 20(2), PCR p35): intend to act for 2+ different parties to a matter or
  transaction **and** a diversity of interests exists or may reasonably be expected to.
- **Before accepting instructions** (r 20(3), PCR p35–37), communicate *directly* with each
  party to:
  - (a)(i) explain (A) how interests diverge, (B) how you may be unable to pass on
    information from one party to another, (C) how you may be unable to give advice
    prejudicial to another party;
  - (a)(ii) tell them you must stop acting if you can't advise "competently, evenly and
    consistently";
  - (a)(iii) answer their queries; (a)(iv) ascertain their intentions precisely;
  - (b) if the transaction is ostensibly disadvantageous to one party: explain the
    detriment, verify the instructions are really theirs, rule out undue influence;
  - (c) advise independent legal advice; (d) if declined, written confirmation or a
    written record; (e) **informed consent in writing** from each.
- **During** (r 20(4)–(6), PCR p37–38): stay vigilant, tell each party of conflicts, and
  **cease to act** if you can't act competently, evenly and consistently. r 20(5)–(6) apply
  the same where a diversity arises mid-retainer.
- **Exception** (r 20(7), PCR p38): you may continue for one party if you cease to act for
  all the others whose interests diverge **and** they all give informed written consent.
- **Absolute bar** (r 20(8), PCR p38): you prepared a document for 2+ parties about their
  rights and obligations, and they fall into dispute over it → you must not act for
  **any** disputing party.
- Cases:
  - *Lie Hendri Rusli v Wong Tan & Molly Lim* [2004] 4 SLR(R) 594 at [48]–[50]: the standard
    of care for each of several clients is at least that owed to a single client; you
    cannot prefer one; at the "crossroads" you must discharge yourself (L2 p9).
  - *LSS v Uthayasurian Sidambaram* [2009] 4 SLR(R) 674 at [34]–[53]: may act for both
    sides of a commercial deal but must disclose conflicts, get informed consent, stay
    impartial, define the retainer, keep records, not be a mere messenger (L2 p10–11).
    Risk: disqualified from acting for **all** clients (SQ p111).
  - *Mahidon Nichiar v Dawood Sultan Kamaldin* [2015] SGCA 36 at [63]–[80]; *Kuek Siang
    Wei v Kuek Siew Chew* [2015] 5 SLR 357 — steps needed for informed consent (SQ p111;
    SYL p3).
  - *LSS v Ezekiel Peter Latimer* [2019] SGHC 92 (SYL p3; SQ p111). Read and summarised
    in eth-w-0-7 (and the 2024 decision in eth-w-0-38).
  - *LSS v Ahmad Khalis* at [74]: even under an implied retainer, a lawyer acting for
    several parties must keep each informed and must not be forced to choose between them
    (L2 p12).
- Practice directions (L2 p14–16): PD 7.2.2 — undesirable to act for applicant creditors
  and the provisional liquidator; PD 7.2.3 — having acted for the complainant (even just
  obtaining the police report) you cannot defend the accused in the same case; PD 7.2.4 —
  debenture holder + its receiver is fine unless a conflict arises; PD 7.2.6 — don't act
  for both mortgagor and mortgagee; PD 7.2.1 — caution acting against a public authority
  you previously worked for. PD 7.2.5 — having acted for both mortgagor and mortgagee,
  don't act for either in later litigation on that transaction (PD 7.2.5 p1).
- **Information barriers (Chinese walls)**: physical separation; instructions and
  acknowledgements from lawyers/staff; monitoring (SQ p112).
- **When multiple representation is unworkable** even with safeguards: where one client
  (e.g. a regulator with a veto) will predictably take positions against the others, the
  lawyers can't advise "competently, evenly and consistently" → decline (SQ p112–113,
  Cheryl/DSA).
- **Tested:** PYP23 Q1 (bidders L and M in the same firm → r 20 with consent + walls,
  acceptable; regulator DSA → decline). PYP21 Q1.2 (firm can't keep Vega Bank and Xavier
  on opposite sides of the Delta Tech default).

### 2.2 Current vs former client (r 21)
- Principle: loyalty and confidentiality survive the end of the retainer (r 21(1),
  PCR p39).
- **Must decline/withdraw** if all three (r 21(2), PCR p39): (a) you/the firm hold the
  former client's confidential information protected by r 6; (b) the current client's
  interest is or may reasonably be adverse to the former client's; (c) the information
  may reasonably be material to the current representation.
- **Exceptions, in order:**
  1. r 21(3) (PCR p39): former client adequately advised to get independent advice **and**
     gives informed written consent (conditions may be agreed).
  2. r 21(4) (PCR p39–40): if (3) fails despite reasonable efforts — adequate safeguards
     to protect the information **and** reasonable efforts to notify the former client of
     the safeguards and that you will act.
  3. r 21(5) (PCR p40): if meeting (3)(a)/(4)(b) would be illegal — adequate safeguards and
     no access/use/disclosure without written consent.
- **Safeguards are hard when the tainted lawyer is the one acting** (SQ p110, Archie).
  Adequacy factors from *Harsha Rajkumar Mirpuri* [2018] 5 SLR 894 at [68]: nature of the
  work for the former client; when the barrier was set up; size of firm; physical
  locations; number and seniority of tainted lawyers (SQ p110).
- *Harsha*: a former **prospective** client (advice only, no retainer) can restrain the
  firm if (a) it holds his confidential, unconsented information; (b) it is or may be
  relevant to the new adverse matter; (c) there is a risk it reaches those now acting
  (L2 p21).
- *Lim Oon Kuin v Rajah & Tann* [2022] SGCA 29: the court's supervisory jurisdiction can
  restrain lawyers where a fair-minded, reasonably informed member of the public would
  conclude the proper administration of justice requires it; a long history with the
  former client is relevant (L2 p22).
- *LSS v Mahtani Bhagwandas* [2021] SGHC 170 (SYL p3): the new client already knowing the
  confidential information is no defence under r 21(2) (at [45]–[51]); read from the judgment,
  now in eth-w-0-11.
- **Tested:** PYP23 Q1.1 / SQ p109–110 (Archie knows RPL's minimum price → r 21(2) met;
  needs (3) or (4); walls doubtful as Archie himself holds it). PS1 Q2 (Howwie drafted for
  Emily, the beneficial owner behind the opposing party; also Emily's negligence threat
  creates an r 22 own-interest issue for the firm).

### 2.3 Lawyer/firm vs client (r 22) and the specific prohibitions
- Must not act where there is or may reasonably be a conflict between the duty to serve
  the client and your or the firm's interests, except as the rule permits (r 22(2),
  PCR p41).
- Interest of you, your **immediate family**, or your firm in the matter (r 22(3),
  PCR p41–42):
  - **adverse** interest → withdraw unless (i) full and frank disclosure, (ii) advise
    independent advice, (iii) if not taken, ensure the client doesn't think you're
    protecting them, (iv) informed written consent;
  - **non-adverse** interest → withdraw unless full and frank disclosure + informed written
    consent. Same for the law practice (r 22(4), PCR p42).
- Cases (L3 p7–14):
  - *LSS v Tan Phuay Khiang* [2007] 3 SLR(R) 477 — favoured referrers' interests over the
    client's; 2 years' suspension.
  - *LSS v Udeh Kumar* [2013] 3 SLR 875 — moneylender-referral facts; acquitted on the
    conflict charge.
  - *Then Khek Khoon v Arjun Permanand Samtani* [2012] 2 SLR 451 — the rule protects the
    client; only the client is the aggrieved party, so the opponent couldn't use it to
    attack the firm's fees.
  - *Ho Kon Kim v Betsy Lim* [2001] SGCA 64 at [63] — firm whose handling caused the
    dispute (and whose partner is a material witness) should not act; advise independent
    advice.
  - *LSS v Singham Dennis Mahendran* [2001] 1 SLR(R) 1 — sexual relationships with clients
    clearly wrong.
  - *LSS v Tan Chun Chuen Malcolm* [2020] 5 SLR 946 — steered the client into his own
    company's investment scheme.
  - *LSS v Govindan Balan Nair* [2020] 5 SLR 988 — concealed his own negligence and kept
    acting.
- GN 7.4.2 — lending money to clients (foreign workers) for living costs creates a
  creditor/debtor conflict; refer them to welfare organisations (L3 p14).
- **Prohibited borrowing (r 23, PCR p42–43)**: must not borrow from a client (or let an
  associated party do so) unless the client had independent advice + full disclosure, or
  the client is separately represented, or the Council allows it. A client in the
  business of lending to the public is outside the definition (L3 p15). Cases: *Yap Bock
  Heng Christopher* [2014] 4 SLR 877 (position of influence; 2 years); *Thirumurthy*
  [2015] SGDT 2 (even a moneylender client, no independent advice → cause of sufficient
  gravity); *Chia Chwee Imm Helen* [2022] SGHC 214 (PCR applies even without a practising
  certificate) (L3 p16–18).
- **Purchases (r 24, PCR p46–47)**: only at market or reasonable price, subject to
  fiduciary law; independent valuation where practicable.
- **Gifts (r 25, PCR p47)**: client intends a **significant** gift (by will or inter
  vivos) to you, anyone in the firm, their immediate family, or the firm → you must
  **not act** on the gift **and** must advise independent advice.
  - *LSS v Wan Hui Hong James* [2013] 3 SLR 221: "significant" is relative to the client's
    means and other beneficiaries' expectations. Suggested approach: explain why
    independent advice is needed; client nominates the lawyer; written confirmation of
    the advice; give that lawyer full information; insist; **decline the gift** if no
    independent advice (L3 p21–22).
  - *LSS v Manjit Singh* [2015] 3 SLR 829 — $1.8m "gift", no independent advice (L3 p23).

### 2.4 Conflicts in proceedings (r 11)
- Principle: duty to advance the client's case is subject to duties as officer of the
  court (r 11(1), PCR p21).
- Must not appear where (r 11(2), PCR p21): (a) a commercial, family, personal or other
  relationship with the client makes independence difficult; (b) your relationship with
  the court or a member of it might make justice appear prejudiced.
- **Material witness** (r 11(3), PCR p22): if it's known or it appears you'll give
  evidence material to a contested issue → (a)(i) don't accept instructions; (a)(ii) if
  already acting, discharge yourself / apply to be discharged; **but** (b) your firm or a
  colleague may act unless that would prejudice the administration of justice.
- Rationale: a witness must tell the whole truth, an advocate must present the client's
  best case — don't let the two collide (L4 p34).
- PD 1.7.1: don't act where you have reason to believe you'll be a witness on a material,
  disputed fact; discharge yourself without jeopardising the client; affidavits on formal
  or undisputed facts are fine (PD 1.7.1 p1).
- *Ho Kon Kim v Betsy Lim* [2001] SLR(R) 220 at [63]: conveyancing partner a material
  witness **and** the firm had an interest because its own work was in issue → neither
  partner should act (L4 p35).
- *Then Khek Khoon* — no disqualification where the firm's "interest" was only the
  reasonableness of its fees (L4 p36).
- Criminal defence: must not stand surety or bail for the accused — personal financial
  interest; incarcerated client can't give real consent (r 14(3)(a), PCR p25; L4 p37).
- **Tested:** PYP22 (a)–(b): Luna and Marci (drafted the settlement, attended, typed the
  attendance notes) are material witnesses on the disputed "Representations" → they can't
  act (r 11(3)(a)); can Chen in the same firm act? r 11(3)(b) allows it **unless** it would
  prejudice the administration of justice — and r 22 bites because the firm's own
  conduct (Luna's alleged representations to an unrepresented party, r 8(2)) is in issue
  (*Ho Kon Kim*). PYP23 Q2.1–2.2 is the same structure (Y, attendance notes, partner A).

## 3. Administration of justice — duties to the court

### 3.1 Officer of the court, paramount duty
- Every advocate and solicitor is an officer of the Supreme Court (LPA s 82(1), LPA p207;
  L4 p3).
- *Public Trustee v By Products Traders* [2005] 3 SLR(R) 449 at [26], [35]: absolute and
  overriding duty to the court; never mislead actively or passively; never knowingly give
  erroneous or incomplete information (L4 p3, p5).
- *Zhou Tong v PP* [2010] 4 SLR 534 at [17]–[19]: duty to assess merits before suing — owed
  to both client and court (L4 p6, p11). *Chung Ting Fai* [2006] 4 SLR(R) 587 at [48]:
  don't put words in the client's mouth in affidavits (L4 p7). *BOI v BOJ* [2018] 2 SLR
  1156: counsel are not mouthpieces; don't take pointless points (L4 p8).
- Proper risk–benefit evaluation: *Lock Han Chng Jonathan v Goh Jessiline* [2008] 2 SLR(R)
  455 at [45]–[46] ($60 fight); *Miya Manik v PP* [2021] 2 SLR 1169 at [82] (unmeritorious
  applications can be misconduct even in capital cases) (L4 p10, p12); *Singapore Shooting
  Association v Singapore Rifle Association* [2019] SGCA 83 — risk–benefit at each stage
  (L10 p34).

### 3.2 Conduct of proceedings (r 9)
- Principles (r 9(1), PCR p17): assist the administration of justice; truthful and accurate
  with everyone involved; never present evidence known to be false; fairness, integrity,
  efficiency; comply with laws and PDs.
- Must not (r 9(2), PCR p17–19): (a) knowingly mislead the court, its staff, another
  lawyer or a witness; (b) fabricate facts or evidence; (c) put a statement of fact known
  to be false in a document; (d)–(e) express or be swayed by personal opinion of the
  client's conduct; (f) knowingly/recklessly cite law out of context or advance a
  proposition known to be wrong; (g) concoct evidence; (h) draft pleadings/affidavits with
  unsupported facts, unarguable contentions, **fraud allegations without clear
  instructions + reasonably credible prima facie material**, or affidavit content beyond
  what the witness would say orally.
- Must (r 9(3), PCR p19): (a) cite **every relevant authority**, for or against; (b)
  disclose what the law requires disclosed, and any procedural irregularity.
- Can't hold back a procedural irregularity for appeal (r 9(4), PCR p19). If you
  unknowingly breach r 9, disclose and remedy once aware (r 9(5), PCR p19).
- Mental element: false statement made knowing it false, without belief in its truth, or
  recklessly — *LSS v Seah Zhen Wei Paul* [2024] 5 SLR 915 at [69]; there, agreeing not to
  volunteer a settlement unless asked misled the Court of Appeal (L4 p21); settled case →
  tell the court well before the hearing (L7 p8).
- Misleading the court — *Re Ram Goswami* [1988] 2 SLR(R) 183 (bail money "15 years'
  savings" when lawyer knew it wasn't the bailor's: 6 months) (L4 p19); *Loh Der Ming
  Andrew v Koh Tien Hua* [2022] 3 SLR 1417 ("unable" to get instructions implied an attempt
  that was never made: dishonest, 3 years) (L4 p20).
- False attestation: *LSS v Thirumurthy* [2022] 4 SLR 462 — attesting a POA not signed in
  his presence: 9 months; PD 1.6.1 (L4 p22).
- Fictitious authorities / generative AI: *Tajudin bin Gulam Rasul* [2025] SGHCR 33; *Tan
  Hai Peng Micheal* [2026] SGHC 49; Registrar's Circular No 1 of 2024 (SYL p5). Read from the
  judgments and the Guide; in eth-w-0-45.
- **Ex parte applications** (r 30(3), PCR p52): comply with the PDs on without-notice
  applications — Supreme Court PD 2021 para 71: tell the court what attempts were made to
  notify the other side, unless notice would defeat the purpose, in which case explain why
  in the affidavit (L7 p4–7).
- **Tested:** PYP21 Q2 — Fiona: knew disclosure of the UK/HK proceedings was required but
  didn't advise (r 5(2)(b), r 10(2)); filed with no evidence of dissipation risk (r 9(2)(h),
  *Zhou Tong*); purpose to pressure ICPL (r 10(5), abuse); answer to the judge's direct
  question was misleading by omission (r 9(2)(a), *Public Trustee*, *Seah Zhen Wei Paul*);
  let admiration for the client cloud judgment (r 9(2)(e)). Remedy now: r 9(5) / r 10(4)
  — disclose and correct before judgment, or cease to act.

### 3.3 Responsibility for the client's conduct (r 10)
- Principles (r 10(1), PCR p20): duty, commensurate with your control, to stop the client
  misleading the court; exercise professional judgment over advice and drafts; never do
  anything unlawful, unethical or improper for the client.
- Tell the client their duties to be truthful and comply with the rules (r 10(2),
  PCR p20).
- So far as able, prevent, not be party to, not assist the client in suppressing evidence
  or giving false evidence (r 10(3), PCR p20).
- **Client about to give / has given false evidence** (r 10(4), PCR p20–21): cease to act,
  **or** continue without perpetuating the falsehood.
- No advice you know or have reason to believe is for a dishonest, fraudulent or unlawful
  purpose (r 10(5), PCR p21); don't knowingly help the client mislead the court or do
  anything dishonest (r 10(6), PCR p21). *LSS v Leong Pek Gan* [2016] SGHC 165 (SYL p6).
- Cases: *Narindar Singh v PP* [1996] 3 SLR(R) 318 — lawyer joined client in corrupt
  solicitation; struck off (L4 p24). *Bachoo Mohan Singh v PP* [2010] 4 SLR 137 at [137(e)]
  — decline instructions that plainly lack foundation; no duty to verify unless compelling
  evidence (L4 p26). *Teo Wai Cheong v Crédit Industriel et Commercial* [2013] 3 SLR 573 at
  [43]–[45] — lawyers share responsibility for proper discovery; explain relevance; don't
  just accept "no more documents" (L4 p28). *LSS v de Souza Christopher James* [2024] 3 SLR
  1570 — ex parte affidavit disclosed a breach of the search-order undertaking but not its
  extent → breach of candour (L4 p30; L7 p9).
- **Tested:** PS2 Q1 — Zapp: advising a scheme to evade Barbara's undertaking to court
  (r 10(5)–(6)); drafting an affidavit with a pretext he hasn't checked (r 9(2)(h)(i),
  (c)); attestation without the Commissioner present (PD 1.6.1, *Thirumurthy*); telling the
  court "certainly has not disposed of any assets" knowing of the imminent transfer
  (r 9(2)(a), *Loh Der Ming*, *Seah Zhen Wei Paul*).

### 3.4 Witnesses (r 12)
- Don't discuss evidence with your own witness during their cross-examination without
  leave (r 12(2), PCR p22).
- Put allegations to the witness in cross-examination before asserting them in
  submissions (r 12(3), PCR p23).
- Don't accuse a witness or others of an offence unless material and on reasonable
  grounds (r 12(4)); no scandalous or vilifying questions (r 12(5)) (PCR p23).
- You may interview any witness (r 12(6)); but if you know the other side has called or
  summoned them, tell that side's lawyer or the prosecution first (r 12(7)) (PCR p23).
  [L5 p9 says "subpoena"; the rule text now says "issued an order to attend court" — use
  the rule text.]
- Pay only lawful expenses; nothing contingent on evidence or outcome (r 12(8), PCR p23).
- Witness coaching: *Ernest Ferdinand Perez De La Sala* [2018] SGCA 16; inappropriate
  cross-examination of victims: *LSS v Wong Sin Yee* [2018] SGHC 196 (SYL p6). Both in eth-w-0-49.

### 3.5 Respect for the court; undertakings (r 13)
- Respectful and courteous to the court and everyone in the proceedings (r 13(2)–(3),
  PCR p24). *LSS v Ravi Madasamy* [2007] 2 SLR(R) 300 at [25]–[26]: turning his back on the
  DJ, walking away, threatening to report the DJ → misconduct; disrespect in open court is
  more serious than mere discourtesy (L4 p15–16). Punctuality: PD 1.4.2.
- **Undertakings to court**: honour every one; only give one you believe necessary and know
  you can honour (r 13(4)–(5), PCR p24). *Re Seow Francis T* [1971–1973] SLR(R) 727
  (undertook to hand over all files, didn't search: 1 year); *Re Marshall David* [1971–1973]
  SLR(R) 554 (assured AG affidavits wouldn't go to the press, sent them two days later:
  6 months) (L5 p15–16).
- Don't publish material that is contempt or calculated to prejudice a fair trial
  (r 13(6), PCR p24) — applies to paralegals too (GN 3.7.1, L8 p7).

### 3.6 Criminal defence (r 14) and prosecution (r 15)
- Defence (PCR p24–26): pursue every reasonable defence (r 14(2)); no surety/bail; no
  personal opinion on guilt; don't be absent without reason, and if absent tell the client
  and arrange cover; keep assisting after conviction (r 14(3)).
- **Confession** (r 14(4), PCR p26): may continue to act, but must not adduce evidence or
  make submissions inconsistent with the confession (so: may test the prosecution's case;
  may not run a positive defence that contradicts it).
- Advise on plea and on giving evidence, then **abide by the accused's decision**
  (r 14(5), PCR p26).
- Absconding client: apply to discharge, or carry on as if present but silent (r 14(6)).
  No scandalous mitigation (r 14(7)). Antecedents: no duty to disclose, but nothing
  inconsistent with them (r 14(8)) (PCR p26).
- *Iskandar bin Rahmat v LSS* [2020] SGHC 40 at [60], [80]–[85] (SYL p7). In eth-w-0-51.
- Prosecution (r 15, PCR p27–28): fair and impartial; disclose non-called witnesses and
  material inconsistencies; tell the court of antecedents, sentencing precedents, and
  mitigation for an unrepresented accused; draw attention to errors. *Re Parti Liyani*
  [2020] 5 SLR 1080 at [38] (SYL p7).

## 4. Other lawyers and other persons

### 4.1 Courtesy and fairness (r 7, r 27)
- Treat other lawyers with courtesy and fairness (r 7(2), PCR p13); relate in a way that
  promotes the administration of justice (r 27, PCR p51). Not inconsistent with fearless
  advocacy — *China Insurance v Liberty Insurance* [2005] 2 SLR(R) 509 at [64]; *Ahmad
  Khalis* at [40] (L6 p8–9). Breaches: *Terence Tan Bian Chye* [2007] SGDSC 10; *Ravi*
  [2015] SGHC 120; *K Jayakumar Naidu* [2012] SGHC 200 at [75] ("bloody-minded") (L6
  p10–11).
- **No contact with a represented person** about the matter (r 7(3), PCR p13–14) unless:
  (a) their lawyer consents; (b) reasonable basis + you notified their lawyer and got no
  reply in reasonable time; (c) delay would severely prejudice your client; (d) authorised
  by law or court order.
- **Second opinions** allowed, with or without the other lawyer's knowledge, but don't
  improperly induce a switch (r 7(4), PCR p14; PD 7.3.3). Don't take over a matter until
  the first retainer is ended (PD 7.3.3 p1). A replaced lawyer must not contact the former
  client to check the new lawyer's authority (PD 8.3.1, L6 p14).
- Appointing another lawyer → you pay their fees unless agreed otherwise (r 7(5),
  PCR p14).
- **Undertakings to other lawyers**: honour every one; give only if necessary and you know
  you can honour it (r 7(6)–(7), PCR p14). An undertaking is a solemn promise the other
  lawyer relies on (L6 p15). *LSS v Naidu Priyalatha* [2022] SGHC 224 (SYL p6, p8).
- **Authority**: may ask; must accept a written representation of authority unless good
  reason to suspect it (r 7(8)–(9), PCR p14); don't treat a request for the warrant as an
  insult — *Tung Hui Mannequin* (L6 p16).
- **No entrapment** to get evidence of another lawyer's misconduct (PD 8.5.5, L6 p17).

### 4.2 Default judgment, allegations, communications (r 28–31)
- **Default judgment** against a represented party: written notice to their lawyer and
  2 working days must pass; notice after 4pm or on a non-working day counts from the next
  working day; doesn't extend court deadlines; no notice needed for failure to comply
  with a court order (r 28, PCR p51–52). Courtesy rule — don't exploit the other side's
  oversight (L6 p19–21).
- **Allegations against another lawyer** in any document filed for your client (incl. the
  client's own affidavits and letters): the lawyer must be given the chance to respond and,
  where practicable, the response disclosed to the court (r 29, PCR p52; L7 p13–17).
  *Tan Beng Hui Carolyn v LSS* [2023] SGCA 7; PD 8.1.1 (SYL p8).
- **Communication with court** in pending proceedings: give the other lawyers a chance to
  be present/reply; copy written communications; if you slip, tell them asap (r 30(1)–(2),
  PCR p52).
- Don't disclose to the court communications between opposing lawyers unless both agree
  (r 31, PCR p53); applies to pre-action talks too (L7 p19).

### 4.3 Other persons (r 8)
- Principles: honest, courteous, befitting your standing towards **anyone**; act in the
  public interest; fair to unrepresented persons (r 8(1), PCR p15).
- **Unrepresented person** you deal with for your client (r 8(2), PCR p15): (a) no legal
  advice (except "get independent advice") if their interests are or may be adverse;
  (b) reasonable steps so they don't think you're protecting them.
  - *LSS v Ganesan Krishnan* [2003] 2 SLR(R) 251: failed to tell non-client donors of a POA
    to get independent advice; must disabuse anyone who may think you protect them (L2 p13).
  - *Ahmad Khalis*: introduced as the client's "friend" there to "help" the family; he
    subordinated the beneficiaries' interests (L7 p24–25).
- No unfair advantage; nothing fraudulent or deceitful (r 8(3), PCR p15). *Wong Juan Swee
  v LSS* [1994] 3 SLR(R) 619 — misled a third party about a bankrupt's creditworthiness
  (L7 p27).
- **Letters of demand**: demand only what's recoverable by due process (r 8(4), PCR p15) —
  e.g. not the cost of the letter for a simple debt (L7 p28). PD 1.8.1.
- **No threats of criminal or disciplinary proceedings** while a civil claim is unsatisfied
  (r 8(5), PCR p16); no offensive letters — PD 9.1.2; *Terence Tan Bian Chye* (27 letters);
  *Chew Kia Heng* [2001] SGDSC 3 ("accept service within 5 minutes") (L7 p29–32).
- Mediator can't later act for a party on that subject (r 8(6), PCR p16).
- **Personal misconduct counts**: *Gopalan Nair* [2011] 4 SLR 607; *Choy Chee Yean* [2010]
  3 SLR 560 (foreign conviction for dishonesty); *Kirpal Singh* [2010] SGDT 5 (L7 p36–38).
- **Tested:** PYP22 / PYP23 Q2 — telling an unrepresented counterparty "you don't need a
  lawyer" and that terms are "fair" breaches r 8(2)(a)–(b) and makes the lawyer a witness.

## 5. Staff, titles, executive appointments

- **Supervision** (r 32, PCR p53): every lawyer, whatever their title, must properly
  supervise staff under them.
- GN 3.7.1 (L8 p4–7): the solicitor stays responsible for paralegals' work; no
  unsupervised court appearances or litigation conduct; no unsupervised dealings with
  police/prosecutors.
- Paralegals are **unauthorised persons** (L8 p8; LPA s 32(2), L8 p9). Unauthorised
  persons acting as or pretending to be advocates and solicitors commit an offence
  (LPA s 33, L8 p10). Lawyer must not act as agent for, lend his name to, or enable an
  unauthorised person (LPA s 77, L8 p15); don't "lend your name" to documents they
  prepared (PD 3.11.2, L8 p17).
- Cases (L8 p18–20): *Yeo Siew Chye Troy* [2019] SGHC 115 (clerk handled payments: 4 years);
  *Tan See Leh Jonathan* [2020] SGHC 102 (paralegal posed as lawyer + fee-sharing:
  3 months); *Lun Yaodong Clarence* [2022] SGHC 269 (not qualified to supervise trainees:
  18 months).
- **Titles** (r 33, PCR p53; L8 p22): only "advocate and solicitor", "lawyer", "legal
  consultant", "Commissioner for Oaths", "Notary Public" (if qualified) or as allowed.
- **Executive appointments** (r 34, PCR p54; L8 p24–28): not in a business that detracts
  from the profession's dignity, materially interferes with practice, or unfairly attracts
  legal work; may hold one in a non-legal business if the Second Schedule conditions are
  met (r 34(4)). Not a housing or estate agent alongside practice (PD 3.8.1, L8 p29). Due
  cause: a business incompatible with the profession (LPA s 83(2)(i), L8 p32).
- **Tested:** PS3 Q2 — paralegal running files alone, signing as "Mega Law", 50/50 fee
  split, 5% to referrer; lawyer as non-executive café director.

## 6. Publicity and touting

- May publicise (r 41) but you're responsible for compliance even by others on your behalf
  (r 42) (L9 p4–5).
- **r 43(1)** (PCR p64): claims of expertise must be justifiable (factors in r 43(2):
  qualifications, experience, time spent, success, complexity, significance, peer
  assessment); no mention of past cases/clients if that breaches confidentiality; **no
  success rates**; **no comparison or criticism** of other lawyers' fees or quality.
- **r 44** (PCR p65–66): nothing likely to diminish confidence in the profession, nothing
  misleading/deceptive/inaccurate/false or **unbefitting the dignity** of the profession.
  Misleading includes: material misrepresentation, omitting a material fact, unverifiable
  information, or **creating unjustified expectations about results** (r 44(2)).
- Good causes (r 45), third-party publicity (r 46), free advice clinics (r 47), overseas
  publicity (r 48) (L9 p12–15).
- PDs: PD 6.2.1 (adverts generally fine), PD 6.2.4 (public appearances), PD 6.1.2
  (hyperlinking — fine absent a financial arrangement), PD 7.4.1, PD 6.2.3, GN 6.1.1
  (L9 p11, p19).
- **Touting** (r 39, PCR p61–62): no touting or anything that reasonably looks like it.
  Referred clients: keep independence, **no commission or consideration to the referrer**,
  advise independently, **communicate directly** with the client. Conveyancing referral
  agreements must be in writing with prescribed terms (r 40, PCR p62–63).
- LPA s 83(2)(d)–(f): due cause includes giving or allowing retention of a fee or
  gratification for procuring legal business (L9 p23). LPA s 78(1)(e) restricts employing
  certain persons (L9 p22).
- Cases (L9 p20–21): *Chong Wai Yen Michael* [2012] 2 SLR 113 (referral fees to property
  agents); *Udeh Kumar* (communicate directly with referred client); *Lillian Bay Puay Joo*
  [2008] 2 SLR(R) 316 (sting operations).
- **Tested:** PYP23 Q2.3 — "best of the best … unlike other lawyers" (comparison, r 43(1)(c);
  unverifiable, r 44(2)(c)); "crush your enemies and recover millions" (unjustified
  expectations, r 44(2)(d); undignified, r 44(1)(b)). PS3 Q3 — "99% success rate" breaches
  r 43(1)(b)(ii) and a small-print disclaimer doesn't cure it. PYP21 Q3.5 — fee discount
  for website shares and a favourable review = consideration for procuring work
  (r 39(2)(b); LPA s 83(2)(d)).

## 7. Fees and client money

### 7.1 Client money (r 16 PCR; Solicitors' Accounts Rules)
- Hold client money/property so as to protect the client; account for it; promptly notify
  receipt; give statements on request (r 16, PCR p30).
- Pay client money into client account **without delay** (r 3(1) SAR); wrong deposit into
  office account is a breach — *Tan Chwee Wan Allan* [2007] 4 SLR(R) 699 (L10 p9). Can't
  contract out — *Dhanwant Singh* [2019] SGHC 290 (L10 p10). Ignorance no defence — *LSS v
  Lim Tean* [2026] SGHC 78 at [55]–[63] (SYL p12): read 18 Nov 2026 —
  strict liability, ignorance aggravates (at [56], [60]–[61]); $30,000 penalty (at [64]); in eth-w-0-68.
- Withdrawals > $5,000 need two solicitors' signatures, unless an approved book-keeper is
  engaged and the sum is ≤ $30,000 (r 8(5)–(6) SAR, L10 p17–18). No transfers between
  clients' ledgers (r 10, L10 p20). Records kept 6 years; reconcile (r 11 SAR, L10 p21).
- AML: client due diligence; suspicious transaction reports; don't tip off — *Chan Chun Hwee
  Allan* [2018] SGHC 21 (unusual transfers without checks) (L10 p27–30).

### 7.2 Fees (r 17)
- Charge fairly (r 17(1), PCR p31). Don't inflate costs; finish promptly; explain offers
  (in a language the client understands); weigh cost vs benefit and ADR; advise on issues
  (r 17(2), PCR p31).
- Tell the client the fee basis, foreseeable payments and (on request) estimates; actuals
  mustn't vary substantially without written notice (r 17(3), PCR p32).
- Contentious matters: explain the client pays own costs regardless of costs orders, may
  pay the other side's if they lose, and may not recover everything if they win (r 17(4),
  PCR p32).
- Bill queried → tell the client in writing of the right to have it assessed (r 17(5),
  PCR p33).
- **No overcharging, even under a fee agreement** (r 17(7)); test: could a reasonable
  lawyer in good faith charge it, considering standing, nature of work, time,
  instructions, other circumstances (r 17(8), PCR p33). Cases: *Andre Ravindran* [2011] 4
  SLR 1184; *Syn Kok Kay* [2023] SGHC 7; *Low Yong Sen* [2009] 1 SLR(R) 802; *Ang Chin Peng*
  [2013] 1 SLR 946 (SYL p12). Ratios in eth-w-0-32 and eth-w-0-70.
- Conflict forces a switch → charge only what the new lawyer needn't duplicate (r 17(9),
  PCR p34).
- PDs (L10 p42–43): equity in lieu of fees OK with care (PD 5.1.1); fee agreements tied to
  party-and-party costs recovered may breach LPA s 107 / r 18 (PD 5.2.1). PD 5.2.2:
  s 111 LPA allows a gross-sum agreement for contentious work, but an unfair one may be
  declared void (s 113(4)), and a "non-refundable" deposit is not always kept regardless of
  work done (PD 5.2.2 p1).

### 7.3 Contingency fees (r 18; LPA s 107)
- Must not negotiate for an interest in the subject matter of contentious proceedings, or
  for pay proportionate to the amount recovered (r 18, PCR p34).
- Must not buy a party's interest, or agree to be paid **only in the event of success** in
  contentious proceedings (LPA s 107(1), LPA p265); maintenance and champerty still apply
  (s 107(3), LPA p266). Conditional fee agreements under Part 8A are the exception
  (s 107(3AA); r 18A) — **non-examinable** (SYL p13).
- *Kurubalan* [2013] 4 SLR 91 — champertous agreement; but acting for an impecunious client
  knowing fees will likely only be recovered if successful is permissible (L10 p46–47).
  *Lau See Jin Jeffrey* [2017] SGHC 30 — alleged oral 20–25% contingency deal (L10 p48).
- **Tested:** PYP21 Q3.1 — $4m fixed fee on a $6m goods claim, payable even on early
  settlement → overcharging analysis under r 17(7)–(8). PYP21 Q3.2 — extra $500k if the
  settlement is under $500k = payment contingent on outcome in contentious proceedings
  (r 18; s 107(1)(b)). Settled 18 Nov 2026: the bonus is the part of the fee payable
  only on success, so s 107(1)(b) LPA catches it; GN 5.6.1 treats a base fee plus success
  uplift as a conditional fee agreement, lawful only in prescribed proceedings (2021 Q3.2 in
  `model-answers.md`; eth-w-0-33). No official answer exists.

### 7.4 Fee sharing (r 19)
- No sharing fees or paying commission to unauthorised persons (r 19, PCR p35); includes a
  lawyer with an expired practising certificate posing as a lawyer — *Tan See Leh Jonathan*
  (L10 p54). No debt collectors for legal fees (PD 5.4.1, L10 p55).

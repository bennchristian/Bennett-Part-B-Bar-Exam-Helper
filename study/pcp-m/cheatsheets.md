### pcp-m-0-1 — Succession planning tools
*Keywords: succession planning, estate planning, will, inter vivos gift, lasting power of attorney, LPA, trust, family office, non-core*

**End point:** The syllabus lists five tools for succession planning: (a) wills; (b) inter vivos gifts; (c) Lasting Powers of Attorney; (d) trusts; (e) family office. The Learning Plan aims at advising on a will and an LPA, applying for a grant and for deputyship, and advising on succession law and the effect of mental incapacity.

*Source note: PR-SYL p1 (Succession planning in Singapore, item 1); PR-LP p1 (Learning Competencies).*

### pcp-m-0-2 — What a will can and cannot dispose of
*Keywords: Wills Act s 3, property disposable by will, CPF, joint tenancy, trust insurance policy, s 132 Insurance Act, inter vivos trust, assets outside estate, non-estate assets*

**End point:** A testator may dispose by will of all real and personal estate he is entitled to at law or in equity at his death (WA s 3). The course says three assets should not be given by will: (a) CPF monies; (b) immovable property held as joint tenants; (c) trust policies under s 132 Insurance Act. Assets settled on an inter vivos trust are not part of the deceased's estate.

> **WA s 3**
> - Subject to the Act, every person may devise, bequeath or dispose of by his will, executed as the Act requires, all real estate and all personal estate which he shall be entitled to either at law or in equity at the time of his death.

*Source note: PR-6a p3 (s 3 WA), p9 (para 3: assets not to be given by will); PR1 s19–20 (CPF, insurance, joint tenancy, trust property); Cyril-PR §1.5. WA: SSO lists the 2020 Revised Edition with amendments up to 1 Dec 2021; the text here follows the course copy.*

### pcp-m-0-3 — Joint tenancy: survivorship, severance
*Keywords: joint tenancy, joint tenants, right of survivorship, jus accrescendi, severance, deed of declaration, instrument of declaration, s 66A(3) CLPA, s 53(5) Land Titles Act, Lau Siew Kim v Yeo Guan Chye, Chan Yuen Lan v See Fong Mun, Ong Chai Soon v Ong Chai Koon, tenancy in common, last survivor*

**End point:** Survivorship applies to joint tenancy property, so it passes to the surviving joint tenant and not under the deceased's will. A will cannot sever a joint tenancy. Without prejudice to the general law on severance, a joint tenant may sever by a deed of declaration (unregistered land, s 66A(3) CLPA) or an instrument of declaration in the approved form (registered land, s 53(5) LTA), served personally or by registered post on the other joint tenants. On registration the severing tenant holds as tenant in common with the others (s 53(6) LTA). The ultimate survivor can dispose of the asset by will, e.g. "If I outlive my husband, I give [property] to my son" (*Lau Siew Kim v Yeo Guan Chye*). Joint tenancy property is not normally listed in the Schedule of Assets.

> **Severance (course summary)**
> - A will cannot sever a joint tenancy.
> - Sever by a notice/deed or instrument of declaration (unregistered land: deed; registered land: instrument), served personally or by registered post on the other joint tenant(s).
> - Effect: tenancy in common, so each share can then pass by will or on intestacy.
> - The syllabus also lists *Chan Yuen Lan v See Fong Mun* [2014] 3 SLR 1048 (CA) and *Ong Chai Soon v Ong Chai Koon* [2022] 2 SLR 457 (CA) on joint tenancies; the Course Materials do not summarise them, and no holding is relied on here.

*Source note: PR1 s20; PR5 s25 (joint assets not in Schedule of Assets); PR-SYL p2 item 6(a); Cyril-PR §1.5.1; CLPA s 66A(3) and LTA s 53(5)–(6) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026). Correction: the draft cited CLPA s 35 for severance; s 35 deals with devolution of land on death, not severance.*

### pcp-m-0-4 — Joint bank accounts
*Keywords: joint bank account, joint account, survivorship, presumption of resulting trust, presumption of advancement, Estate of Yang Chun v Yang Chia-Yin, Lim Chen Yeow Kelvin v Goh Chin Peng, Chye Seng Kait v Chye Seng Fong, gift to survivor*

**End point:** The starting point is survivorship: the surviving holder takes the balance. A presumption of resulting trust (unequal contributions, no clear evidence of a gift) can displace it, so the survivor holds for the deceased's estate (*Estate of Yang Chun*). A presumption of advancement (spouse, parent–child) can in turn displace the resulting trust. Evidence of intention includes who contributed, how the account was run, who declared the interest for tax, and the bank's joint-account documents (*Lim Chen Yeow Kelvin*).

*Source note: PR-SYL p3 item 6(c) (Lim Chen Yeow Kelvin [2008] 4 SLR(R) 783; Estate of Yang Chun [2019] 5 SLR 593; Chye Seng Kait [2021] 2 SLR 1131 CA); Cyril-PR §1.5.3. The Course Materials list the cases without summarising them; the propositions follow Cyril.*

### pcp-m-0-5 — CPF monies: nomination only
*Keywords: CPF, Central Provident Fund, CPF nomination, s 25 CPF Act, Public Trustee, nomination revoked by marriage, divorce does not revoke, will cannot dispose CPF, CPF memorandum*

**End point:** CPF monies can be dealt with only by a CPF nomination, not by will (CPF Act s 25). A member aged 16 or above may nominate (s 25(1)). With no nomination, CPF Board transfers the savings to the Public Trustee's Office, which distributes them to the family under the intestacy laws (or, for a Muslim, per the inheritance certificate). A nomination is revoked by the member's marriage (s 25(5)(a)); divorce is not a statutory ground of revocation. An LPA donee or a deputy cannot make or revoke a CPF nomination for P (see [0-80](#pcp-m-0-80), [0-86](#pcp-m-0-86)).

*Source note: PR1 s19; PR-6a p9; PR-SYL p3 item 6(d); CPF Act s 25(1), (5) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026); CPF Board FAQ "If I have not made a CPF nomination…" (cpf.gov.sg, Nov 2026) for distribution through the Public Trustee's Office. Conflict: Cyril-PR §1.5.4 cites s 25(2)(a) for the Public Trustee rule; s 25(2) was deleted by Act 39 of 2021 wef 18 Jun 2022, so the block relies on the CPF Board's statement of current practice.*

### pcp-m-0-6 — Insurance nominations and payment without grant
*Keywords: insurance nomination, Insurance Act s 132, trust nomination, s 133 revocable nomination, Part 3C Insurance Act, policies after 1 September 2009, s 150(2), Insurance (General Provisions) Regulations reg 7, S$150,000 without grant, proper claimant*

**End point:** For policies after 1 Sep 2009, a policy owner may make a trust nomination (s 132 IA, irrevocable; proceeds held for the nominees, outside the estate) or a revocable nomination (s 133 IA, changeable without the nominee's consent). An insurer may pay up to S$150,000 of policy moneys without a grant (s 150(2) IA read with the Insurance (General Provisions) Regulations 2003, reg 7). An LPA donee or deputy cannot make or revoke an insurance nomination.

*Source note: PR1 s19; PR-SYL p3 item 6(e) and p14; Cyril-PR §1.5.5. IA s 150 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026) (payment of policy moneys without a grant); Insurance (General Provisions) Regulations reg 7(1) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026): the prescribed amount is $150,000 (the regulation still cross-refers to the pre-2020 numbering, s 61).*

### pcp-m-0-7 — Landed property, HDB flats and mortgaged property
*Keywords: Residential Property Act, RPA s 3, s 25(5), s 3(4), foreign person, landed property, Land Dealings Approval Unit, LDAU, proceeds of sale, HDB flat, Housing and Development Act s 59, s 50, s 58, mortgaged property, s 26 Civil Law Act, mortgage debt, exoneration*

**End point:** Before gifting immovable property, check the beneficiary. A foreign person cannot take restricted residential (landed) property under the RPA without approval of the Land Dealings Approval Unit (s 25(5) RPA). Otherwise the executors sell and give the proceeds instead of the property in specie (s 3(4) RPA). An HDB flat needs HDB's consent (s 59 HDA). A beneficiary of mortgaged land takes it subject to the mortgage debt. The debt is not paid from the personal estate unless the will says so expressly; a general direction to pay debts is not enough (s 26 CLA).

> **Course and Cyril details**
> - RPA s 3(3)–(4): no residential property passes to a foreign beneficiary; the PRs must sell to a citizen or approved purchaser within 5 years of death and pay him the proceeds (see [1-31](#pcp-m-1-31)).
> - HDA s 59: a transmission or transfer of the flat by the PRs needs HDB's written consent (s 59(1)). HDB may have the flat vested in itself if (a) no grant is taken out within 12 months of death, (b) the PRs do not apply for consent within 6 months of the grant, or (c) the sale or transfer is not completed within 12 months of consent (s 59(3)); it may also do so if it refuses consent (s 59(2)).
> - CLA s 26: (1) the devisee cannot have the mortgage debt paid out of other property unless the testator showed a contrary intention; (2) the land is primarily liable for its mortgage; (3) a general direction to pay debts out of personal estate does not shift it, unless the will refers expressly or by necessary implication to mortgage debts.

*Source note: PR1 s23; PR-SYL p3 item 6(b); Cyril-PR §1.5.2. Worked example: PR-PP1 Q1 (31 King's Road with S$350,000 mortgage left to Maureen, a US citizen). RPA s 3(3)–(4) (SSO PDF current at 3 Dec 2023, in RE-Statutes); HDA s 59 and CLA s 26(1)–(3) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026). Conflict: Cyril-PR says HDB may "compulsorily acquire" the flat; s 59 provides for vesting in HDB.*

### pcp-m-0-8 — What a will is; ambulatory nature
*Keywords: will, definition, testator, executor, beneficiary, ambulatory, s 19 Wills Act, speaks from death, codicil, s 2 will includes codicil, revocable at any time*

**End point:** A will is a written declaration, made with the statutory formalities, of what the maker wants to happen on or after death. It is ambulatory: it is construed as if executed immediately before death, unless the will shows a contrary intention (WA s 19). The testator can therefore change or revoke it as often as he likes while he has capacity, unless he has made mutual wills. "Will" includes a codicil (s 2 WA).

> **WA s 19**
> - Every will shall be construed, with reference to the real and personal estate comprised in it, to speak and take effect as if executed immediately before the testator's death, unless a contrary intention appears by the will.

*Source note: PR1 s2–3, s6; PR-6a p5 (s 19 quoted), p14 (s 2); PR-6d p1 (Ambulatory).*

### pcp-m-0-9 — Mutual wills, mirror wills, joint wills
*Keywords: mutual wills, mirror wills, joint will, VTL v VTM, Hiroto Watanabe v Law Yen Yen, agreement not to revoke, constructive trust, reciprocal wills, spouses*

**End point:**
- **Mirror wills:** reciprocal terms only, and each party can revoke freely.
- **Mutual wills:** made under a legally binding agreement (intended to bind, supported by consideration) not to revoke without the other's consent. Once the first dies having kept his side, equity imposes a trust on the survivor's property. The survivor can still revoke, but the new dispositions are ineffective against the trust.
- **Joint will:** one document executed by two or more persons, the survivor taking the whole. It is rare because of the problems on the first death or on revocation.

*Source note: PR-SYL p1 item 1 (VTL v VTM [2021] SGHCF 30; joint wills); PR-6a p27–28 (para 15.2); Cyril-PR §1.1 (test from Hiroto Watanabe: prior agreement intended to be irrevocable on the first death, plus making the wills). VTL v VTM is not summarised in the Course Materials.*

### pcp-m-0-10 — Who can make a will: age 21, s 27 exceptions, Muslims
*Keywords: minimum age, 21 years, s 4 Wills Act, s 27, soldier in actual military service, mariner or seaman at sea, nuncupative will, privileged will, Muslim testator, one-third, AMLA, Mohamed Ismail bin Ibrahim v Mohd Taha, domicile, forced heirship*

**End point:** A will made by a person under 21 is invalid (WA s 4). The exception is a soldier in actual military service, or a mariner or seaman at sea (s 27), who may make a will under 21; a nuncupative (oral) will is valid only for them. A Muslim domiciled in Singapore can dispose by will of no more than one-third of his estate. Muslim wills are outside this course. Ask the client's domicile, since a foreign domicile may bring forced heirship.

> **WA s 4 and s 27 (course summary)**
> - s 4: no will made by any person under the age of 21 years shall be valid.
> - s 27: soldiers in actual military service and mariners or seamen at sea may dispose of personal estate as before the Act (i.e. without the s 4 age rule or s 6 formalities).

*Source note: PR1 s4; PR-6a p3–5 (paras 2(2)(a), (f), (g)); PR-6d p2 (nuncupative will); PR-SYL p1 item 2 (Mohamed Ismail bin Ibrahim [2004] 4 SLR(R) 756); Cyril-PR §1.2 (s 60(1) AMLA one-third). Conflict: Cyril adds that s 27 also covers guardianship appointments and some real estate dispositions; the Course Materials state only the soldiers and mariners exception, which is followed.*

### pcp-m-0-11 — Testamentary capacity, knowledge and approval, undue influence
*Keywords: testamentary capacity, sound mind, Banks v Goodfellow, Chee Mu Lin Muriel v Chee Ka Lin Caroline, ULV v ULW, WWI v WWJ, XBO v XBP, dementia, presumption of capacity, knowledge and approval, suspicious circumstances, undue influence, burden of proof, propounder, medical certificate, Mental Capacity Act ss 4 and 5*

**End point:** The propounder must prove capacity and knowledge and approval, on a balance of probabilities. Capacity is presumed if the will looks rational and was duly executed, and knowledge and approval is then presumed too, unless there are suspicious circumstances. To have capacity, the testator must:
- understand the nature and effect of making a will;
- know the extent of his property;
- know who the beneficiaries are and appreciate their claims;
- not be affected by a disorder of mind (e.g. delusions) that influences the dispositions.

Undue influence needs coercion that overpowers free will, which is a high bar (*ULV v ULW*). Dementia alone does not prove incapacity (*XBO v XBP*). The solicitor should call a doctor where the testator has a known mental infirmity (*Chee Mu Lin Muriel*).

> **Course practice points**
> - Ask whether this is a first will; if not, check the testator knows he is revoking the earlier one (Chee Mu Lin Muriel [60]).
> - See the testator alone if a beneficiary attends; probe reasons for leaving a child out (PR-6c sample note).
> - Course links the test to MCA ss 4–5 (PR-6a p4).

*Source note: PR1 s4; PR-6a p4, p26–27 (para 15.1, Chee Mu Lin Muriel [62]); PR-6c p1–7; PR-SYL p2 item 4 (Chee Mu Lin Muriel [2010] 4 SLR 373 at [37]–[62]; ULV v ULW [2019] 3 SLR 1270; WWI v WWJ [2024] SGFC 22; XBO v XBP [2024] SGHCF 36); Cyril-PR §1.2 (limbs, presumptions, burden, XBO). The limbs follow Cyril; the Course Materials cite the cases without setting out the limbs.*

### pcp-m-0-12 — Formal validity of wills made abroad (s 5 WA)
*Keywords: s 5 Wills Act, formal validity, internal law, place of execution, domicile, habitual residence, nationality, vessel, aircraft, immovable property lex situs, revocation, power of appointment, affidavit of foreign law, foreign will*

**End point:** A will is properly executed if it conforms to the internal law of any one of these places:
- where it was executed;
- the testator's domicile, at execution or at death;
- the testator's habitual residence, at execution or at death;
- the State of his nationality, at execution or at death.

If the will was executed outside Singapore, the Court may require an affidavit of foreign law to confirm formal validity.

> **WA s 5 (course summary)**
> - s 5(2): the four connecting factors above.
> - s 5(3): also properly executed if (a) executed on a vessel or aircraft, conforming to the law of the territory most closely connected with it; (b) so far as it disposes of immovable property, conforming to the law where the property is; (c) so far as it revokes an earlier will or provision, conforming to any law under which the earlier will would be valid; (d) so far as it exercises a power of appointment, conforming to the law governing the essential validity of the power.
> - s 5(4): a will exercising a power of appointment is not improperly executed only because it does not follow a form required by the instrument creating the power.

*Source note: PR1 s5; PR3 s9 (affidavit of foreign law); PR-SYL p2 item 3(a); Cyril-PR §1.3.1 (limb detail). WA: SSO lists the 2020 Revised Edition with amendments up to 1 Dec 2021, so no amendment after the course copy.*

### pcp-m-0-13 — Execution under s 6 WA
*Keywords: s 6 Wills Act, in writing, signed at the foot or end, signed by another person at direction, acknowledged signature, two or more witnesses present at the same time, subscribe in presence of testator, no form of attestation necessary, s 6(3), s 6(4), dispositions after signature, Re Beadle, Tan Pwee Eng v Tan Pwee Hwa, holograph will*

**End point:** No will is valid unless it is in writing and executed as s 6(2) requires. The testator (or someone in his presence and by his direction) signs at the foot or end. The signature is made or acknowledged before two or more witnesses present at the same time. Each witness then subscribes in the testator's presence. No form of attestation is needed. A home-made will signed in the top corner, with the witnesses signing the envelope, fails (*Re Beadle*). The formalities are mandatory.

> **WA s 6**
> - (1) No will shall be valid unless it is in writing and executed in the manner mentioned in subsection (2).
> - (2) signed at the foot or end by the testator, or by some other person in his presence and by his direction; signature made or acknowledged by the testator in the presence of two or more witnesses present at the same time; those witnesses subscribe the will in the presence of the testator; no form of attestation necessary.
> - (3) the signature is valid as to position if placed at, after, following, under, beside or opposite the end so that it is apparent on the face of the will that the testator intended to give effect to the writing by it.
> - (4) no signature is operative to give effect to any disposition or direction which is underneath or follows it, or which is inserted after the signature is made.

*Source note: PR1 s5, s17; PR-6a p3–4 (s 6 quoted; Re Beadle [1974] 1 All ER 493), p22–23 (para 9: execution checklist); PR-6d p2 (holograph will: unwitnessed, so invalid under s 6); Cyril-PR §1.3.2. WA current per SSO listing (2020 RevEd, amended to 1 Dec 2021).*

### pcp-m-0-14 — Who can witness a will (ss 9–12 WA)
*Keywords: attesting witness, s 10 Wills Act, beneficiary as witness, spouse of beneficiary witness, gift void, will still valid, supernumerary witness, s 10(3), s 9 incompetent witness, s 11 creditor witness, s 12 executor as witness, blind witness, Ross v Caunters, witness later marries beneficiary, witness age*

**End point:** If a beneficiary, or a beneficiary's spouse, attests, the will stays valid. Only that beneficiary's gift is void (s 10(1)), and the witness can still prove the will (s 10(2)). The gift is saved if the will was duly executed without that attestation, e.g. by two other independent witnesses (s 10(3)). A witness who marries the beneficiary after execution does not void the gift. An executor (s 12) or a creditor (s 11) may witness. Later incompetence of a witness does not invalidate the will (s 9). A witness can be deaf or mute but must be able to see. No minimum age is set; choose credible, traceable witnesses.

> **WA s 10**
> - (1) If a person attests a will under which any beneficial devise, legacy, estate, interest, gift or appointment is given to him or to his wife or husband (other than charges and directions for payment of debts), that gift, so far as it concerns the witness or spouse or anyone claiming under them, is utterly null and void.
> - (2) The witness is still admitted as a witness to prove the execution or the validity or invalidity of the will.
> - (3) The attestation is disregarded for subsection (1) if the will is duly executed without his attestation and without that of any other such person.
>
> **Exam patterns**
> - Executor (no gift) witnesses → will valid, appointment stands (PR-PP1 Q2).
> - Witness's daughter is a beneficiary → will valid; s 10 covers only the witness and spouse (SQ p73 Q5 I).
> - Witness later declared mentally incompetent → will valid (s 9) (SQ p73 Q5 II).
> - Witness's husband named executor → will valid (s 12) (SQ p73 Q5 III).

*Source note: PR1 s5; PR-6a p5–6 (s 10 quoted; Ross v Caunters; later marriage; blind witness), p23 (paras 9(3)–(6)); Cyril-PR §1.3.2 table (ss 9, 11, 12); SQ p73 Q5 (key p128: D, none invalidates); PR-PP1 Q2. WA current per SSO listing.*

### pcp-m-0-15 — Solicitor's duties in will preparation; negligence
*Keywords: Low Ah Cheow v Ng Hock Guan, Chee Mu Lin Muriel, Cheo Yeoh & Associates LLC v AEL, solicitor's duty, attendance notes, adverse inference, Law Society Guidance Note Ethical Considerations Preparing Witnessing Will 2011, Mahidon Nichiar, Wan Hui Hong James, professional negligence, disappointed beneficiary, translation, supervise execution*

**End point:** There must be "no room for even the slightest doubt" that the solicitor understood the testator's intention and expressed it in the will (*Low Ah Cheow*). The solicitor should:
- document his discussions and instructions painstakingly;
- confirm before execution that the draft reflects the testator's intention;
- have any translation done thoroughly;
- supervise the execution, or check the will afterwards;
- keep contemporaneous attendance notes.

Missing notes may lead the court to doubt the solicitor and draw adverse inferences (*Low Ah Cheow* [38]; *Mahidon Nichiar* [105]). A solicitor whose will fails for defective execution is liable in negligence to the disappointed beneficiaries (*Cheo Yeoh & Associates LLC v AEL*).

> **Attendance note contents (PR1 s29; PR-6c p2–3)**
> - how long known and who introduced the testator; date and mode of instructions;
> - medical history; any confusion or memory loss (call a doctor if in doubt);
> - discussion of earlier wills and reasons for departures;
> - how the will was explained; language and the interpreter's competence; whether the other witness understood;
> - who else was present; where, when and how executed.

*Source note: PR1 s26–29; PR-6a p1–2, p13 (Low Ah Cheow [73], Chee Mu Lin Muriel [60]), p24 (taking wills away to execute is perilous), p32 (para 15.6 Cheo Yeoh [2015] 4 SLR 325); PR-6c p1–2; PR-SYL p2 item 3(d).*

### pcp-m-0-16 — Evidence of due execution: affidavits required by the Registrar
*Keywords: affidavit of due execution, P 6 r 6, P 6 r 7, P 6 r 8, P 6 r 9, P 6 r 10, Family Justice (Probate and Other Matters) Rules 2024, FJC PD para 210, attestation clause missing, blind testator, illiterate testator, signed by another, alterations, attempted revocation, undated will, different pens*

**End point:** The Registrar requires an affidavit from an attesting witness, or another person present, in these cases:
- there is no attestation clause, the clause is insufficient, or there is doubt about due execution (P 6 r 6);
- the will was signed by a blind or illiterate testator or by another at his direction, or there is reason to doubt he understood it (P 6 r 7);
- there are alterations not compliant with the WA (P 6 r 8);
- there is an appearance of attempted revocation (P 6 r 9).

P 6 r 10 lets the Registrar call for any such affidavit. If no witness affidavit can be had, the Registrar may accept other evidence raising a presumption of due execution.

*Source note: PR5 s19 (P 6 rr 6–10 FJ(PAOM)R 2024; FJC PD 210(4)); PR1 s18; PR-6a p23–24 (para 10: undated will, different pens, other matters needing explanation), p15 (P 6 r 9 quoted); PR-SYL p2 item 3(d)(iv); Cyril-PR §1.7.1 (no-witness alternative). FJ(PAOM)R 2024 (S 723/2024) P 6 rr 6–10 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026); under r 6(3) the Registrar must refuse probate if satisfied the will was not duly executed, or may refer a doubtful case to the Court.*

## 0.III Construction of wills, failure of gifts, revocation and alteration

### pcp-m-0-17 — Principles of construction; precatory words
*Keywords: construction of wills, testamentary intention, Foo Jee Seng v Foo Jhee Tuang, Zhao Hui Fang v Commissioner of Stamp Duties, Goh Nellie v Goh Lian Teck, a testator does not will in vain, purposive interpretation, armchair, surrounding circumstances, Lau Tyng Tyng v Lau Boon Wee, precatory words, wish and strong desire, evidence of drafting lawyer*

**End point:** The court's overriding aim is to find and give effect to the testator's intention as expressed. It derives that intention mainly from the words of the will, and may consider the circumstances when the will was executed. A literal reading clearly out of step with the will as a whole gives way to a purposive one. Effect is given to every word where possible ("a testator does not will in vain") (*Foo Jee Seng* [17]; *Zhao Hui Fang* [46]–[54]). Precatory words such as "my wish and strong desire" only express a hope. They are not binding and the court will not strain to make them imperative (*Lau Tyng Tyng*). The drafting lawyer's evidence and attendance notes may be relevant.

*Source note: PR-6a p8–9 (para 2(3)(f)–(g), Foo Jee Seng [17] and Lau Tyng Tyng quoted); PR-SYL p3 item 7(a)–(b); Cyril-PR §1.6.1.*

### pcp-m-0-18 — Trust for sale v power of sale; executor's power of sale
*Keywords: trust for sale, power of sale, doctrine of conversion, power to postpone sale, Foo Jee Seng, personalty, realty, executor statutory power of sale, executor v trustee, Trustees Act Part 3, s 35(1) CLPA, implied powers*

**End point:** A trust for sale imposes a duty to sell. It converts land notionally into money (personalty) from the testator's death, even if the trustees have power to postpone sale. A power of sale is a discretion, and the property changes character only on actual sale (*Foo Jee Seng* [22]–[23]). An executor has a statutory power of sale for administration. Once administration ends he holds as trustee, so when selling he should sell "as executor" unless the will gives trustees an express power of sale. The syllabus points to the implied powers in Trustees Act 1967 Part 3 and s 35(1) CLPA (devolution of land to personal representatives, with power to deal with it); draft express powers where more is needed.

*Source note: PR-6a p16–18 (paras 7.4(1)–(12)); PR-SYL p4 item 8(h); CLPA s 35(1) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026).*

### pcp-m-0-19 — Types of gift
*Keywords: specific legacy, general legacy, demonstrative legacy, pecuniary legacy, residuary legacy, residue, devise, bequest, bequeath, legatee, devisee, non-specific gift, gift over, substitute beneficiary, specific gift v residuary gift*

**End point:**
- **Specific:** a particular identified item ("my car to my son"; "12 Maple Hill to Robert").
- **General:** a value from anywhere in the estate ("$100").
- **Demonstrative:** a general gift payable from a named fund ("$100 from my XXX Bank savings").
- **Pecuniary:** money, which may be specific, general or demonstrative.
- **Residuary:** everything not otherwise disposed of. Residue usually bears debts, funeral and testamentary expenses.
- **Gift over:** gives the property to a substitute if the first beneficiary predeceases or the condition fails.

A devise is a gift of land; a bequest is a gift of personalty. Only specific gifts adeem; general and demonstrative gifts do not.

*Source note: PR1 s11–16; PR-6d p1–2 (Legacy types, Ademption, Gift over, Devise, Bequest); PR-6a p11 (para 5(2)(d)).*

### pcp-m-0-20 — What passes: ss 18–25 WA
*Keywords: s 18 Wills Act subsequent conveyance, s 19 speaks from death, s 20 lapsed or void devise falls into residuary devise, s 21 general gift includes property under general power of appointment, s 22 devise without words of limitation passes fee simple, s 23 die without issue, s 24 devise to trustee or executor passes whole estate, s 25 devise to trustee without limitation, what passes under will, after-acquired property*

**End point:** The will catches whatever the testator owns at death (s 19).
- **s 18:** a conveyance or other act after the will (other than a revocation) does not stop the will operating on whatever interest the testator can dispose of at death.
- **s 20:** unless the will shows otherwise, real estate in a devise that fails or is void (e.g. the devisee died first) falls into the residuary devise.
- **s 21:** a general devise of real estate, or a general bequest of personal estate, includes property the testator has a general power to appoint, and operates as an exercise of that power.
- **s 22:** a devise of real estate without words of limitation passes the fee simple or whole interest the testator could dispose of.
- **s 23:** "die without issue" means without issue living at that person's death, not an indefinite failure of issue.
- **s 24:** a devise of real estate to a trustee or executor passes the whole estate, unless a definite term or freehold estate is given to him.
- **s 25:** a devise to a trustee without express limitation vests the whole legal estate, not one ending when the trust purposes are satisfied.

*Source note: PR-SYL p3 item 7(c)–(e); WA ss 18–25 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026). Conflict: Cyril-PR §1.6.2–1.6.5 paraphrases s 21 as "a general gift includes all property the testator could dispose of" and gives the "no words of limitation" rule as s 24 and the trustee rule as s 25; the SSO text (s 21 general power of appointment; s 22 no words of limitation; ss 24–25 trustees) is followed.*

### pcp-m-0-21 — Lapse: beneficiary predeceases; gift over
*Keywords: lapse, beneficiary predeceases testator, s 20 Wills Act, gift over, falls into residue, lapsed residue passes as on intestacy, ADG v ADH, partial intestacy, company dissolved, survivorship clause 30 days*

**End point:** A gift lapses if the beneficiary dies before the testator, unless there is a gift over or s 26 applies.
- **Lapsed specific gift:** falls into residue.
- **Lapsed residue, share of residue or general gift:** passes as on an intestacy (*ADG v ADH* [40]), unless the will directs that a lapsed share accrues to the other residuary beneficiaries.
- **Gift to a dissolved company:** lapses the same way.
- **Prevention:** draft a gift over, and a survivorship clause (e.g. "if she survives me by 30 days").

> **Exam pattern (PR-PP1 Q4)**
> - Residue to boyfriend who predeceased, no gift over → residue lapses → passes on intestacy to the sole relative (sister) under the ISA.
> - Specific gifts that fail fall into that residue.

*Source note: PR1 s21; PR-6a p12 (fn 1, survivorship clause), p20–21 (paras 7.7–8.2, ADG v ADH [2010] 1 SLR 557 at [40]; s 20 WA covers only lapsed devises of real estate, so use a gift over for personalty), p30 (para 15.4); Cyril-PR §1.6.3. Conflict: Cyril states s 20 sends every lapsed gift into residue; the Course Materials distinguish specific gifts (to residue) from residuary gifts (intestacy), which is followed.*

### pcp-m-0-22 — s 26 WA: gifts to children or issue; class gifts (Re Loke Soh Lui)
*Keywords: s 26 Wills Act, gift to child predeceases leaving issue, does not lapse, goes to child's estate, Re Will of Loke Soh Lui, class gift, my children living at my death, named child, contrary intention, grandchildren, gift over, In re Hurd*

**End point:** A gift to a child or other issue of the testator does not lapse if that child dies before the testator leaving issue alive at the testator's death. The gift takes effect as if the child had died immediately after the testator, unless the will shows a contrary intention (s 26). The gift goes into the predeceased child's estate, passing under his will or intestacy, which may benefit his spouse. It does not go directly to the grandchildren (*Re Loke Soh Lui*). s 26 does not apply to:
- a class gift ("my children as are living at my death"): the dead member's share lapses and the survivors share it;
- a gift to named persons who are not the testator's issue: it lapses into residue.

To send the share elsewhere, or to the dead child's issue, say so expressly.

> **WA s 26**
> - Where a child or other issue of the testator, to whom real or personal estate is devised or bequeathed for an interest not determinable at or before his death, dies in the testator's lifetime leaving issue, and any such issue is living at the testator's death, the gift shall not lapse but takes effect as if the death had happened immediately after the testator's death, unless a contrary intention appears by the will.
>
> **Contrast:** ISA s 7 r 3 sends a predeceased child's share to his descendants per stirpes and never to his spouse. s 26 WA instead saves the gift into the child's estate. England changed its s 33 in 1983; Singapore's s 26 is unchanged.

*Source note: PR1 s21–22, s32; PR-6a p28–30 (para 15.3, s 26 quoted, Re Loke Soh Lui [1997] 3 SLR(R) 956 at [20]–[28], [37]); Cyril-PR §1.6.3. Conflict: Cyril says under s 26 the gift "goes to the descendants"; the Course Materials (Re Loke Soh Lui) say it goes to the deceased child's estate, which is followed. WA current per SSO listing.*

### pcp-m-0-23 — Commorientes: s 30 Civil Law Act
*Keywords: commorientes, simultaneous death, same accident, uncertain order of death, s 30 Civil Law Act, presumption of survivorship, younger deemed to survive elder, order of seniority, survivorship clause*

**End point:** Where two or more persons die in circumstances making it uncertain who survived, the deaths are presumed (subject to any court order) to have occurred in order of seniority, so the younger is deemed to have survived the elder (CLA s 30). A gift by the elder to the younger therefore takes effect and passes to the younger's estate. A gift by the younger to the elder lapses.

> **Exam patterns**
> - PR-PP1 Q3: Colin (28) leaves S$200,000 to David (26); both die in a crash, order unknown → David deemed to survive → the gift goes to David's personal representatives.
> - PR-PP1 Q1: Kim Seng (65) and Polly (67) die together → Kim Seng deemed to survive Polly → Polly's estate gives Kim Seng his spouse's share, which then passes under Kim Seng's will; joint tenancy assets accrue to Kim Seng's estate.
> - A 30-day survivorship clause avoids passing gifts through two estates.

*Source note: PR1 s32; PR-6a p12 fn 1 (s 30 CLA quoted, survivorship clause); PR-6d p1 (commorientes); Cyril-PR §1.9.4; applications are the drafter's, applying s 30 to PR-PP1 Q1 and Q3 (no official answers in the Course Materials); CLA s 30 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026).*

### pcp-m-0-24 — Ademption
*Keywords: ademption, adeemed, specific gift no longer exists, sold before death, destroyed, given away, changed character, Low Gim Har v Low Gim Siah, proceeds of sale, replaced car, compulsory acquisition*

**End point:** A specific gift is adeemed (fails) if at death its subject matter no longer belongs to the testator: sold, destroyed, given away, acquired by Government, or changed in character so it no longer fits the description. The beneficiary gets neither the property nor its proceeds unless the will expressly gives the proceeds (*Low Gim Har* [57]–[59]). General and demonstrative gifts do not adeem. Whether ademption has occurred is a question of construction.

> **Ademption occurs when (Low Gim Har, per Cyril)**
> - the subject matter is disposed of or destroyed before the will takes effect;
> - it ceases to conform to the description by which it is given (e.g. shares replaced after a takeover);
> - its nature has changed (e.g. shares under an enforceable but uncompleted sale are treated as money).
>
> **Exam pattern (PR-PP1 Q4):** "my Audi" when the testator now owns a BMW; a necklace already given to a nurse → both adeemed.

*Source note: PR1 s20; PR-6a p22 (para 8.3); PR-6d p1; PR-SYL p4 item 7(g) (Low Gim Har [1992] 1 SLR(R) 970 at [57]–[59]); Cyril-PR §1.6.6.*

### pcp-m-0-25 — Abatement
*Keywords: abatement, insufficient assets, legacies reduced, pari passu, proportionate reduction, insolvent estate, fund insufficient, Second Schedule Probate and Administration Act, order of resort to assets*

**End point:** If the assets (or a named fund) cannot pay all debts and legacies, the legacies abate: they are reduced proportionately and beneficiaries take pari passu. Where a legacy has no named fund, the trustee pays it from available assets in the order in the Second Schedule to the PAA. Under the Second Schedule, assets are applied in this order: (1) property undisposed of by will; (2) residue; (3) property appropriated for debts; (4) property charged with debts; (5) the fund retained for pecuniary legacies; (6) specifically devised or bequeathed property, rateably; (7) property appointed under a general power. So general (pecuniary) legacies are used up before specific gifts. The will may vary the order.

*Source note: PR1 s20; PR-6a p22 (para 8.4); PR-6d p1; PR-SYL p4 item 7(h); Cyril-PR §1.6.7; PAA Second Schedule (s 57(4)) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026).*

### pcp-m-0-26 — Disclaimer and other reasons a gift fails
*Keywords: disclaimer, refuse gift, Townson v Tickell, failure of gifts, public policy, uncertainty, condition not satisfied, no-contest clause, fraud, s 10 witness, cy-pres, charity wrong name, Re Will of Samuel Emily, receipt clause*

**End point:** A testamentary gift may fail through:
- disclaimer by the beneficiary (who cannot be forced to take);
- lapse;
- s 10 WA (beneficiary or spouse witnessed);
- ademption, or abatement or insolvency;
- public policy or illegality, or uncertainty;
- an unmet condition or forfeiture (no-contest clause);
- fraud.

A disclaimed gift goes to a gift over if there is one. Otherwise it follows the specific-or-residuary rules for lapse. Charity gifts must name an existing charity correctly, or a cy-près application may be needed. Add a receipt clause so the trustee need not see to how the gift is applied.

*Source note: PR-6a p21–22 (paras 8.1, 8.4 Disclaimer), p26 (para 14, charities; Re Will of Samuel Emily [2001] 3 SLR(R) 335).*

### pcp-m-0-27 — Revocation: marriage, later will, destruction; revival
*Keywords: revocation of will, s 13 Wills Act marriage revokes will, contemplation of marriage, s 14 no revocation by change of circumstances, divorce, birth of child, s 15 revocation methods, later will, codicil, writing declaring intention to revoke, burning tearing destroying, animus revocandi, s 17 revival, conditional revocation, Cheo Yeoh, revocation clause, foreign wills*

**End point:**
- **Marriage:** revokes a will (s 13(1)), unless the will is expressed to be made in contemplation of that marriage (s 13(2)). This applies to a first or later marriage, including a lawful polygamous one. The power of appointment exception also applies.
- **Change of circumstances:** no revocation by presumption from changed circumstances, so divorce or the birth of a child does not revoke (s 14).
- **Methods (s 15):** revocation is only (a) by s 13 marriage; (b) by a later duly executed will or codicil; (c) by a duly executed writing declaring the intention to revoke; or (d) by burning, tearing or otherwise destroying the will, by the testator or another in his presence and by his direction, with intention to revoke.
- **Revival (s 17):** only by re-execution or a duly executed codicil showing an intention to revive.
- **Revocation clause:** always include one. Without it, an earlier will may be proved alongside. A Singapore will must not unintentionally revoke the client's foreign wills.

> **WA s 13(2) wording tip:** "I declare that I make this Will in contemplation of my intended marriage to [name] and that this Will shall not be revoked by the said marriage" (and that it stays effective if the marriage does not take place). Omitting it risks a negligence claim.
> **Attempted revocation:** must be accounted for to the Registrar (P 6 r 9 FJ(PAOM)R 2024). Doctrine of conditional revocation: Cheo Yeoh & Associates LLC v AEL.

*Source note: PR1 s8; PR-6a p7–8 (ss 13–15, 17 quoted; para 2(3)(e)), p14–15 (para 7.2); PR-SYL p5 item 10(c); Cyril-PR §1.9.2. WA current per SSO listing.*

### pcp-m-0-28 — Alterations after execution (s 16 WA)
*Keywords: s 16 Wills Act, alteration, obliteration, interlineation, handwritten amendment after signing, initials in margin, memorandum, re-execute, apparent, fresh will*

**End point:** An obliteration, interlineation or other alteration made after execution has no effect unless executed like a will. The exception is where it makes the original words no longer apparent, in which case those words are lost. An alteration is duly executed if the testator signs and the witnesses subscribe in the margin or near it, or at the end opposite a memorandum referring to it (s 16(2)). Alterations made before execution should be referred to in the will, or initialled by the testator and witnesses. Best practice is to engross and execute a fresh will.

*Source note: PR-6a p6–7 (s 16 quoted), p24 (para 11); PR5 s19 (P 6 r 8 affidavit for non-compliant changes); Cyril-PR §1.9.3. WA current per SSO listing.*

### pcp-m-0-29 — Codicils
*Keywords: codicil, amendment to will, supplement, republication, confirm will, executed like a will, attach to will, original will before solicitor*

**End point:** A codicil alters or supplements an existing will. It is itself a will and must be executed with the same formalities. Draft one only with the original will and codicils in front of you, identify them precisely, and confirm the will as varied, which republishes it as at the codicil's date. For substantial changes, make a new will.

*Source note: PR-6a p25 (para 13); PR-6d p1 (Codicil); PR1 s8.*

### pcp-m-0-30 — Rectification of a will (s 28 WA)
*Keywords: rectification of will, s 28 Wills Act, clerical error, failure to understand instructions, testator's intentions, 6 months after first grant, permission of court, personal representatives distribute after 6 months*

**End point:** The court may order a will to be rectified to carry out the testator's intentions if it fails to do so because of (a) a clerical error or (b) a failure to understand his instructions (s 28(1)). The application must be made within 6 months after the grant is first made, unless the court permits a later one (s 28(2)). Personal representatives who distribute after those 6 months are not liable merely because a rectification order might later be made (s 28(3)).

*Source note: PR-SYL p5 item 10(b); MU3 s22 (same two grounds); WA s 28 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026; inserted by Act 16 of 2016).*

### pcp-m-0-31 — Civil Law Act provisions on wills (ss 23–27, 30)
*Keywords: Civil Law Act s 23 illusory appointment, s 24 executor trustee of undisposed residue, s 25 chain of executorship, executor of executor, s 26 mortgage debt, s 27 bona vacantia, s 30 commorientes*

**End point:**
- **s 23:** an appointment under a power is not invalid merely because some objects take an unsubstantial or illusory share, or none.
- **s 24:** an executor is trustee of undisposed residue for those entitled on intestacy, unless the will shows he was to take it beneficially.
- **s 25:** the executor of a sole or last surviving executor who has proved the will is executor of the original testator (chain of executorship). The chain breaks on intestacy, failure to appoint an executor, or failure to prove.
- **s 26:** the mortgaged land bears its own mortgage debt (see [0-7](#pcp-m-0-7)).
- **s 27:** bona vacantia, with the Minister's power to release (see [0-45](#pcp-m-0-45)).
- **s 30:** commorientes (see [0-23](#pcp-m-0-23)).

*Source note: PR-SYL p5 item 10(e); PR2 s28–29 (s 24), s32 (s 27); PR3 s12 (s 25); PR-6a p12 (s 30); Cyril-PR §1.9.4; CLA ss 23–27, 30 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026).*

### pcp-m-0-32 — Taking instructions to prepare a will
*Keywords: taking instructions, will instructions, checklist, domicile, testamentary capacity, earlier wills, foreign wills, executor, trustee, guardian, gift over, residuary clause, partial intestacy, attendance note, see the testator alone*

**End point:** Before drafting, establish:
- the testator's domicile (it decides formal and essential validity, s 5 WA, and forced heirship);
- testamentary capacity and freedom from pressure (see [0-11](#pcp-m-0-11));
- the declaration of testamentary intention and the revocation of earlier wills, without revoking foreign wills by accident;
- the executors, trustees and guardians, with substitutes;
- the gifts (specific, general, devises) and gift-overs, noting foreign law for foreign immovables;
- a residuary clause that leaves no partial intestacy;
- powers for executors and trustees beyond Trustees Act Part 3 and s 35(1) CLPA;
- the testimonium and the right attestation clause (blind, cannot sign, does not read English).

Take instructions from the testator, alone if a beneficiary brought him, and keep an attendance note (see [0-15](#pcp-m-0-15)).

*Source note: PR-SYL p4 item 8(a)–(j); PR1 s26–29; PR-6b (checklist of information); PR-6c p1–3; Cyril-PR §1.7.1.*

### pcp-m-0-33 — Structure and clauses of a will
*Keywords: will clauses, commencement, last will and testament, revocation clause, appointment of executors and trustees, guardian clause, just debts funeral and testamentary expenses, specific gift, residuary gift, trust for minor, testimonium, attestation clause, translation attestation, blind testator, signed by another, also known as*

**End point:** A will runs in this order:

| Part | Purpose / wording |
|---|---|
| Commencement | "This is the last will and testament of me [name, NRIC, address]", plus any alias ("also known as") |
| Revocation | "I revoke all former wills, codicils and testamentary dispositions" |
| Executors and trustees | named, with substitutes |
| Guardians | for children under 21 (s 7 GIA) |
| Debts | gifts are subject to just debts, funeral and testamentary expenses |
| Specific and general gifts | described accurately, with gift-overs and survivorship clauses |
| Residue | to named persons or a class, often on trust for minors |
| Powers | express powers for executors and trustees |
| Testimonium | "In witness whereof I have set my hand this [date]" |
| Attestation | testator signs, then two witnesses present at the same time; variants for a testator who cannot read English (translated, by whom), is blind, or signs by another at his direction |

*Source note: PR-6a p10–25 (paras 5–9); PR-SYL p4 item 8(e)–(j); Cyril-PR §1.7.2 (clause table and model wording).*

### pcp-m-0-34 — Who cannot or should not be appointed executor
*Keywords: executor, minor executor, infant, s 21 PAA, lunatic, mentally disordered executor, s 22 PAA, undischarged bankrupt, s 400 IRDA, leave of court, failure of executors, s 13 PAA, s 15 PAA, s 27 PAA, substitute executor, renounce, letters of administration with will annexed*

**End point:**
- **Minor:** a minor cannot take the grant; administration may be granted for his use and benefit until he reaches 21 (s 21 PAA).
- **Person lacking mental capacity** (within the MCA, to manage his property and affairs): no grant to him; administration with or without the will annexed may go to the person lawfully entrusted with the care of his estate, or anyone the court thinks fit, for his use and benefit (s 22 PAA, as amended wef 15 Oct 2024). So a named executor who has lost capacity does not stop a grant (SQ p72 Q3: his LPA donee may apply; key A).
- **Undischarged bankrupt:** disqualified from being appointed or acting as personal representative without the court's permission (s 400(1) IRDA); the bar ends on annulment, rescission or discharge (s 400(2)); acting while disqualified is an offence (s 400(3)). The court is readier to allow a bankrupt named executor than to appoint a bankrupt administrator.
- **Failure of executors:** if no executor is appointed, or none survives, is able, or will act, apply for letters of administration with the will annexed (s 13 PAA; see [0-60](#pcp-m-0-60)).

Always name a substitute executor, choosing someone likely to accept.

*Source note: PR-SYL p2 item 5(a)–(d); PR3 s4–5; Cyril-PR §1.4.1–1.4.2; SQ p72 Q3 (key p128: A). PAA ss 21–22 and IRDA s 400 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026).*

### pcp-m-0-35 — Testamentary guardians
*Keywords: testamentary guardian, guardian clause, s 7 Guardianship of Infants Act, minor children under 21, surviving parent objects, act jointly, s 6 GIA, intestate parent*

**End point:** A testator may appoint a guardian for his children under 21 by will (s 7 GIA). The guardian acts jointly with the surviving parent while that parent lives, unless the parent objects. If the parent objects, the guardian may apply to court (s 7(4) GIA). Without a will, the surviving parent is the sole guardian.

*Source note: PR3 s8; Cyril-PR §1.7.2; GIA s 7(1)–(5) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026) (either parent may appoint by deed or will; on objection the court may refuse, order joint guardianship, or make the guardian sole guardian).*

### pcp-m-0-36 — Conflicts of interest and ethics in will work
*Keywords: conflict of interest, significant gift, rule 25 PCR, Legal Profession (Professional Conduct) Rules 2015, independent legal advice, Lee Suet Fern, Mahidon Nichiar, Kuek Siang Wei, solicitor as executor, acting for several beneficiaries, Law Society Guidance Note*

**End point:** If a client intends a significant gift (by will or otherwise) to the lawyer, the law practice, any of its partners, directors, consultants or employees, or their immediate family, the lawyer must not act on that gift and must advise the client to get independent advice (r 25 PCR; *Law Society v Lee Suet Fern* [2020] SGDT 1: one-third of the estate to the lawyer's husband). A lawyer who drafts the will may be executor if not a beneficiary. Acting for several family members or beneficiaries:
- *Mahidon Nichiar* [2015] 5 SLR 62: explain documents properly to each; beware the one who pays and instructs for the group.
- *Kuek Siang Wei* [2015] 5 SLR 357: a lawyer advising an executor may owe duties to beneficiaries; identify each one's interest, get informed consent if acting for all, or make clear whom he does not act for.

*Source note: PR-SYL p4 item 9(a)–(b); Cyril-PR §1.7.1, §1.8. Case holdings follow Cyril; the Course Materials list the cases only.*

### pcp-m-0-37 — After execution: safekeeping and the Wills Registry
*Keywords: safekeeping of will, Wills Registry, Singapore Academy of Law, register will, locate will, original will, deposit*

**End point:** Keep the original will safe (with the lawyer or in a safe place) and record it with the SAL Wills Registry, which records that a will exists, who prepared it and where the original is kept, but does not store the will. Since 9 Sep 2026 SAL also runs a Wills Repository Service for depositing a digital copy through participating law firms; the original signed will is still needed. After death, the Wills Registry is one place to look for a will.

*Source note: PR-SYL p4–5 item 10(a); PR1 s30; Cyril-PR §1.9.1; PR-SYL p6 item 2(b); SAL notices "Wills Registry To Be Managed By Singapore Academy Of Law From 4 May 2020" and "SAL Wills Registry Enables Secure Deposit of Digital Copies of Wills" (sal.org.sg).*

### pcp-m-0-38 — Scope of the ISA
*Keywords: Intestate Succession Act, ISA s 2, Muslim excluded, s 4 law regulating distribution, movable property lex domicilii, immovable property lex situs, s 5, domiciled outside Singapore, intestate defined, s 3*

**End point:** The ISA does not apply to the estate of any Muslim (s 2; Muslim estates go by faraid under AMLA). Distribution of movables is governed by the law of the deceased's domicile at death (s 4(1)). Distribution of immovable property in Singapore is governed by the ISA whatever the domicile (s 4(2)). "Intestate" includes a person who leaves a will but dies intestate as to some beneficial interest (s 3).

> **ISA ss 3–5**
> - s 3: "child" = a legitimate child, including one adopted by court order under the law of Singapore, Malaysia or Brunei; "issue" includes children and descendants of deceased children.
> - s 4(1): movables → law of the country of domicile at death.
> - s 4(2): immovables → this Act, wherever domiciled.
> - s 5: dies intestate after 2 Jun 1967 (a) domiciled in Singapore, owning property in Singapore, or (b) domiciled outside Singapore, owning immovable property in Singapore → distributed under the Act.

*Source note: PR2 s3–5; PR-SYL p5 Intestacy item 1(a)–(b); ISA s 3 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026).*

### pcp-m-0-39 — Distribution on intestacy: s 7 rules 1–9
*Keywords: ISA s 7, rules of distribution, rule 1, rule 2, rule 3, rule 4, rule 5, rule 6, rule 7, rule 8, rule 9, spouse half, children half, parents, brothers and sisters, nephews and nieces, grandparents, uncles and aunts, intestacy table, who inherits*

**End point:** Go down the table and stop at the first row that fits the survivors.

| Survivors | Distribution | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Spouse only (no issue, no parent) | Spouse 100% | r 1 |
| Spouse + issue | Spouse 50%; issue 50% per stirpes | r 2, r 3 |
| Issue, no spouse | Issue 100% per stirpes | r 3 |
| Spouse + parent(s), no issue | Spouse 50%; parent(s) 50% | r 4 |
| Parent(s) only | Parent(s) 100%, equally if two | r 5 |
| Brothers and sisters (and children of dead siblings) | 100%, equally; a dead sibling's children take his share per stirpes | r 6 |
| Grandparents | 100%, equally | r 7 |
| Uncles and aunts | 100%, equally | r 8 |
| None of the above | Government (bona vacantia) | r 9 |

Note: siblings never share with parents; parents never share with issue. If the intestate leaves more than one widow, they share the spouse's share equally (s 8). An illegitimate intestate's estate under rr 4–5 goes to the mother only (Legitimacy Act s 10(2)).

**Worked example:** estate $900,000; wife, two sons, and a daughter who died before him leaving two children. Wife $450,000 (r 2). Issue's $450,000 is split into three stocks of $150,000: each son $150,000; the dead daughter's two children $75,000 each (r 3 per stirpes). The daughter's husband takes nothing.

*Source note: PR2 s7–14, s31, s33 (summary table); PR-SYL p5 item 1(c)–(d); PR-PP1 Q4 (lapsed residue to sole sister). Worked example is the drafter's application of r 3. ISA ss 7–8 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026).*

### pcp-m-0-40 — Per stirpes and the two provisos to rule 3
*Keywords: per stirpes, by stocks, rule 3 proviso, descendants not next of kin, child-in-law excluded, son-in-law, daughter-in-law, grandchildren take parent's share, remotest degree, predeceased child*

**End point:** Issue take per stirpes. Proviso 1: those who "legally represent" a dead child are his descendants, not his next of kin, so the dead child's spouse takes nothing. Proviso 2: descendants to the remotest degree stand in their ancestor's place and share what he would have taken. If the dead child left no descendants, his share goes to the other children (or their stocks).

*Source note: PR2 s8–10. Contrast s 26 WA for gifts by will, where the gift goes into the dead child's estate ([0-22](#pcp-m-0-22)).*

### pcp-m-0-41 — Who counts as a "child": illegitimate, adopted, step and foster children
*Keywords: illegitimate child, Legitimacy Act s 10, mother's estate, legitimated child, s 2, s 3 Legitimacy Act, adopted child, s 47 Adoption of Children Act 2022, step-child, Low Guang Hong David v Suryono Wino Goei, foster child, Lim Weipin v Lim Boh Chuan, AAG v Estate of AAH, disowned child*

**End point:**
- **Legitimate or legitimated child:** a child under the ISA.
- **Adopted child:** a child of the adopter (s 3 ISA; s 47 Adoption of Children Act 2022).
- **Illegitimate child:** not a "child" under the ISA (*AAG v Estate of AAH* [2010] 1 SLR 769). The exception is that he takes his mother's intestate estate as if legitimate, if she leaves no legitimate children (Legitimacy Act s 10).
- **Step-child:** not a child; it needs a legal and biological link, so a step-parent who wants to provide must make a will (*Low Guang Hong David v Suryono Wino Goei* [2012] 3 SLR 185).
- **Foster child:** not a child (*Lim Weipin v Lim Boh Chuan* [2010] 3 SLR 423).
- **"Disowned" child:** the ISA's definition of "child" (s 3) has no exception for a child the parent has disowned, so he still takes on intestacy. Only a will can exclude him, subject to the IFPA.

*Source note: PR2 s21–25; PR-SYL p5 item 2(a)–(c); SQ p71 Q2 (step-daughter excluded; key p128: B); Legitimacy Act s 10(1)–(2), ACA s 47(1) and ISA s 3 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026).*

### pcp-m-0-42 — Whole blood and half blood; child en ventre sa mere
*Keywords: half blood, whole blood, half-brother, half-sister, s 6 ISA, Chng Heng Tee v Estate & Trust Agencies, paternal and maternal relatives equal, en ventre sa mere, child in the womb, posthumous child*

**End point:** No distinction is drawn between relatives through the father and through the mother. A child conceived before the death and later born alive counts as if born (s 6(a)). Half-blood relatives rank immediately after whole-blood relatives of the same degree (s 6(b)). So a whole-blood aunt excludes half-blood aunts and uncles entirely (*Chng Heng Tee v Estate & Trust Agencies* [2010] 1 SLR 681).

> **ISA s 6**
> - (a) no distinction between those related through the father and through the mother, nor between those born in the deceased's lifetime and those conceived (en ventre sa mere) at death and later born alive;
> - (b) those related by the half blood rank immediately after those of the whole blood related in the same degree.

*Source note: PR2 s6, s15–20; PR-SYL p5 item 3(a)–(b).*

### pcp-m-0-43 — No hotchpot for lifetime gifts (s 9)
*Keywords: hotchpot, advancement, lifetime gift to child, s 9 ISA, not taken into account, settled on child, distributive share*

**End point:** Money or property the intestate gave, paid or settled for a child's advancement in his lifetime is not taken into account in fixing that child's (or his descendants') share (s 9). The rule speaks only of children and their descendants, not of the spouse, parents or siblings.

*Source note: PR2 s26.*

### pcp-m-0-44 — Partial intestacy (s 10 ISA; s 24 CLA)
*Keywords: partial intestacy, s 10 ISA, s 24 Civil Law Act, executor trustee of undisposed residue, lapsed residue, probate not letters of administration, part of estate not disposed of*

**End point:** Where a will disposes of only part of the estate, the ISA applies to the rest, subject to the will (s 10). The personal representative holds the undisposed part as trustee for those entitled under the ISA, unless the will shows he takes it beneficially (s 10 proviso; s 24 CLA is to the same effect for executors). The executor simply applies for probate. No separate letters of administration are needed, even for infant beneficiaries of the intestate part.

*Source note: PR2 s27–30; PR-SYL p5 item 1(d); PR-PP1 Q4.*

### pcp-m-0-45 — Bona vacantia (rule 9; s 27 CLA)
*Keywords: bona vacantia, no next of kin, Government entitled, rule 9 ISA, s 27 Civil Law Act, Minister for Law, disclaim and release, equitable or moral claim, Consolidated Fund*

**End point:** If no one takes under rules 1–8, the Government takes the whole estate (r 9). Non-land property goes to the Consolidated Fund (s 27(1)–(2) CLA). The Minister may disclaim and release the Government's right, in whole or part, to anyone who shows an equitable or moral claim (e.g. a step-child or carer), on such terms as he thinks fit (s 27(3) CLA).

*Source note: PR2 s31–32; PR3 s3.*

### pcp-m-0-46 — Spouse during divorce; step-children (exam pattern)
*Keywords: interim judgment, divorce not final, spouse still entitled, final judgment, step-daughter, intestate wife, husband inherits, SQ*

**End point:** Every judgment of divorce is first an interim judgment, made final no earlier than 3 months later (WC s 99(1)). Until final judgment the parties are still married, so a spouse who dies after only interim judgment leaves the other a "spouse" under r 2. In SQ p71 Q2, Jasmine dies intestate after interim judgment: Joel (husband) takes half and their two children the other half. Joel's daughter by an earlier marriage (Jasmine's step-daughter) takes nothing.

*Source note: SQ p71 Q2 (key p128: B); PR2 s23–24 (step-children). WC s 99(1) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026).*

*Keywords: advantages of a will, dying testate, dying intestate, choice of executor, vesting on death, Public Trustee vesting, guardians, sureties, administration bond, chain of executorship, stamp duty, deed of family arrangement, ABSD, delay 6 months, two administrators minority, testamentary freedom*

### pcp-m-0-48 — Scope of the IFPA
*Keywords: Inheritance (Family Provision) Act, IFPA, s 1(2) Muslims excluded, domiciled in Singapore, s 3(1), family provision, testamentary freedom limited, Family Justice Courts, UK 1975 Act not in pari materia*

**End point:** The IFPA lets a dependant ask the court for reasonable maintenance from the estate where the will, the intestacy rules, or both do not make reasonable provision. It does not apply to Muslim estates (s 1(2)), and it applies only where the deceased died domiciled in Singapore (s 3(1)). The application goes to the Family Justice Courts. English cases on the UK 1975 Act are not in pari materia.

*Source note: PR4 s2–3, s6; PR-SYL p5–6 Other issues item 1(a)–(b); IFPA ss 1(2), 3(1)–(5), 4 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026).*

### pcp-m-0-49 — Who is a dependant
*Keywords: dependant, wife, husband, unmarried daughter, daughter incapable of maintaining herself, infant son, son under disability, adopted child, en ventre sa mere, illegitimate child excluded, AAG v Estate of AAH, adult son not dependant, Jeanne Christine Monteiro, IFPA s 2, s 3(1)*

**End point:** Dependants (s 3(1)) are:
- a wife or husband;
- a daughter who has not been married, of any age (*Jeanne Christine Monteiro*: aged 61), or who cannot maintain herself because of mental or physical disability;
- an infant son (under 21);
- a son who cannot maintain himself because of mental or physical disability.

"Son" and "daughter" include an adopted child and a child en ventre sa mere at the death (s 2). An illegitimate child is not a dependant (*AAG v Estate of AAH*). An adult son who is able-bodied is not, even if studying (*AOS v Estate of AOT*). A divorced daughter (once married) is not, unless disabled.

*Source note: PR4 s4–5, s12, s14; Cyril-PR §3.1.2.*

### pcp-m-0-50 — What the court may order; the two-thirds exception; lump sum
*Keywords: reasonable provision for maintenance, periodical payments, s 3(2) IFPA, termination remarriage, daughter marries, infant son 21, disability ceases, two-thirds of income, s 3(1) proviso, lump sum, net estate not exceeding $50,000, s 3(4), APZ v AQA, s 3(3) annual income cap, Soh Siew Yok*

**End point:** The court may order reasonable provision out of the net estate for the dependant's maintenance (s 3(1)). The claim fails where the surviving spouse gets at least two-thirds of the income of the net estate and the only other dependants are children of that spouse (s 3(1) proviso; *Soh Siew Yok*). The order is by periodical payments (s 3(2)), not exceeding the annual income of the net estate (s 3(3)). A lump sum is possible only if the net estate does not exceed $50,000 (s 3(4); *APZ v AQA* [2011] 3 SLR 1110).

> **IFPA s 3(2): periodical payments must end no later than**
> - (a) spouse: remarriage;
> - (b) unmarried or disabled daughter: marriage or end of disability, whichever is later;
> - (c) infant son: reaching 21;
> - (d) disabled son: end of disability;
> - in any case, the dependant's earlier death.

*Source note: PR4 s6, s8–9; Cyril-PR §3.1.3 (s 3(3) cap). Conflict: Cyril says the spouse exception applies to "more than 2/3"; PR4 s6 says "not less than 2/3rds", which is followed.*

### pcp-m-0-51 — Time limit for an IFPA application
*Keywords: time limit, 6 months, IFPA s 4, representation first taken out, grant, extension of time, discovery of will or codicil, s 4(2), s 6*

**End point:** The application must be made within 6 months from the date representation is first taken out (s 4(1)), not from death. The court may extend time if the limit would operate unfairly because of:
- (a) discovery of a will or codicil making a substantial change;
- (b) an undetermined question whether a person has an interest, or its nature;
- (c) other circumstances affecting administration or distribution (s 4(2); see also s 6).

*Source note: PR4 s7; Cyril-PR §3.1.3.*

### pcp-m-0-52 — Matters the court considers; deceased's reasons; effect of an order
*Keywords: s 3(5) nature of net estate, s 3(6) dependant's means, conduct towards deceased, s 3(7) deceased's reasons, signed and dated statement, letter of wishes, s 5 effect of order, will deemed varied from death, s 5(2)*

**End point:** The court considers:
- the nature of the net estate, avoiding an improvident sale (s 3(5));
- the dependant's past, present or future capital and income, and conduct towards the deceased (s 3(6));
- any other relevant matter (s 3(6));
- the deceased's reasons for the dispositions or omissions, including a signed and dated written statement, weighed for accuracy (s 3(7)).

So advise a testator who leaves out a dependant to record dated reasons. An order takes effect as if the will or intestacy rules had been varied from the death (s 5(1)). Only enough of the net estate to produce the provision's income may be set aside (s 5(2)).

*Source note: PR4 s9–11; Cyril-PR §3.1.3.*

### pcp-m-0-53 — Court of Appeal IFPA cases: AAG and AOS
*Keywords: AAG v Estate of AAH, illegitimate children cannot claim, AOS v Estate of AOT, widow, decree nisi, pending ancillary matters, division of matrimonial assets, Women's Charter just and equitable division, maintenance not legacies, adult son with cerebral palsy must apply himself, inter vivos gifts*

**End point:**
- *AAG v Estate of AAH* [2010] 1 SLR 769 (CA): illegitimate children cannot claim under the IFPA. Allowing it would indirectly give them an intestate share the ISA denies.
- *AOS v Estate of AOT* [2012] 3 SLR 721 (CA): the husband died after decree nisi but before ancillary matters, leaving everything to a grandson. The IFPA gives maintenance, not a WC-style share of matrimonial assets. The widow was already reasonably provided for by properties in her name and income above her expenses, so no order was made. Adult sons studying abroad were not dependants. A disabled adult son had to apply himself; his mother could not apply for him.

*Source note: PR4 s12–18; PR-SYL p6 item 1(c)–(d).*

### pcp-m-0-54 — Maintenance of Parents Act
*Keywords: Maintenance of Parents Act, MPA s 3, parent aged 60, unable to maintain himself, Tribunal for the Maintenance of Parents, s 5 just and equitable, child's ability to pay, s 7 expiry on death, s 11 application on behalf, caregiver*

**End point:** A parent aged 60 or above, domiciled and resident in Singapore and unable to maintain himself adequately, may apply to the Tribunal for an order that one or more children pay a monthly allowance, other periodical payment or lump sum (s 3(1)); a caring organisation may also apply (s 3(2)). The claim must first be referred to the Commissioner (s 3(3)). The Tribunal orders it if just and equitable, where the child can pay after meeting his own family's needs and the parent cannot maintain himself despite efforts (s 5). An unsecured order ends on the death of the parent or the child; a secured one on the parent's death (s 7). Where there are several respondents, one's death does not end the others' liability (s 7(2)).

*Source note: PR-SYL p14 (MPA ss 3, 5, 7, 11, additional reference); Cyril-PR §3.2; MPA ss 3, 5, 7 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026) (as amended by Act 22 of 2023 wef 1 Jul 2024). Cyril's under-60 infirmity limb and the s 11 point were not re-checked and have been left out.*

### pcp-m-0-55 — Probate terms
*Keywords: probate, grant of probate, letters of administration, administration, testate, intestate, personal representative, executor, administrator, trustee, caveator, grant of representation*

**End point:**

- **Administration:** a grant to an administrator where there is no will, or no executor able and willing.

### pcp-m-0-56 — First steps on a death; lost will; will proved abroad
*Keywords: steps when someone dies, locate will, Wills Registry, Law Gazette advertisement, lost will, copy or draft of will, s 9 PAA, contents of will, will deposited in foreign court, s 11 PAA, P 6 r 43, authenticated copy, willing and able executor*

**End point:** Check whether there is a will. Search belongings, ask earlier solicitors, check the Wills Registry, and advertise in the Law Gazette or newspapers. If found, check validity and apply for probate; if none, apply for letters of administration. Check the executors or administrators are willing and able (ss 13, 18 PAA).
- **Original lost:** probate of a copy or draft (s 9(a) PAA), or of the contents if they can be proved (s 9(b)), on affidavit evidence of due execution, survival of the will after death (to rebut presumed revocation) and accuracy.
- **Original proved and deposited in a foreign court:** probate of a duly authenticated copy (s 11 PAA; P 6 r 43).

*Source note: PR-SYL p6 Probate item 2(a)–(f); Cyril-PR §4.2.*

### pcp-m-0-57 — Types of grant and which one to apply for
*Keywords: grant of probate, letters of administration, letters of administration with will annexed, s 13 PAA, s 14 executor absent, resealing, ss 47–49 PAA, s 19 attorney, s 20 pending suit, s 21 during infancy, s 22 person lacking mental capacity, s 23 trust property, s 24 collection and preservation, s 25(2) de bonis non, decision tree*

**End point:**
- **Valid will + an executor able and willing** → grant of probate (s 8 PAA).
- **Valid will, but no executor able and willing** (none named, dead, incapable, renounced, absent) → letters of administration with will annexed (ss 13, 14, 21, 22).
- **No valid will** → letters of administration (s 18), distributed under the ISA.
- **Commonwealth or Hong Kong grant** → resealing (ss 47–49).

> **Other grants (PR5 s6)**
> - s 19: to an attorney of a person entitled who is absent from Singapore;
> - s 20: pending a probate action (no power to distribute);
> - s 21: during infancy; s 22: for a person lacking mental capacity;
> - s 23: limited to trust property; s 24: limited to collecting and preserving property;
> - s 25(2): for estates not fully administered (de bonis non).

*Source note: PR5 s3–7; Cyril-PR §4.3.1.*

### pcp-m-0-58 — Which court hears the application
*Keywords: Family Division of the High Court, Family Courts, Family Justice Courts, estate value exceeds S$5 million, Family Justice (Family Proceedings before Family Division of High Court) Order 2014, s 2(a), assets held as trustee excluded, debts not deducted*

**End point:** If the estate (excluding assets held as trustee, without deducting debts) is believed to exceed S$5 million, the application goes to the Family Division of the High Court. Otherwise it goes to the Family Courts (Family Justice (Family Proceedings before Family Division of High Court) Order 2014, s 2(a)). The procedure is in Part 6 of the Probate Rules (FJ(PAOM)R 2024) and Part 24 of FJC PD 2024.

*Source note: PR5 s11; PR-SYL p7 item 3(b); Cyril-PR §4.3.2 (exclusions).*

### pcp-m-0-59 — Deceased domiciled outside Singapore (P 6 r 22)
*Keywords: domiciled outside Singapore, foreign domicile, P 6 r 22, person entrusted by foreign court, person entitled under foreign law, will in English, immovable property only, affidavit of foreign law*

**End point:** Where the deceased died domiciled outside Singapore, the Registrar may order a grant to:
- (a) the person entrusted with administration by the court of the domicile;
- (b) the person entitled to administer under the law of the domicile;
- (c) if none, whoever the Registrar directs.

(d) where s 6 PAA requires 2 administrators, to someone the Registrar directs jointly with such a person. Without such an application, probate of an admissible will may go to the named executor if the will is in English (or to a person whose described duties make him executor). If the Singapore estate is only immovable property, a grant limited to it may follow Singapore law (r 22(2)). An affidavit of foreign law is usually needed.

*Source note: PR-SYL p7 item 3(c); PR5 s12, s20; FJ(PAOM)R 2024 P 6 r 22 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026); P 6 r 3(5) (the application must state where the deceased died domiciled).*

### pcp-m-0-60 — Letters of administration with will annexed: failure of executors and the s 13(2) order
*Keywords: letters of administration with will annexed, s 13 PAA, failure of executors, no executor appointed, executor predeceased, executor renounces, executor incapable, s 13(2) priority, universal or residuary legatee, personal representative of residuary legatee, legatee, creditor, SQ Terrence Daisy*

**End point:** Where no executor is appointed, or none survives, is capable, is willing, or appears to take probate, the court grants administration with the will annexed to the fittest person (s 13(1)). Priority under s 13(2):
1. universal or residuary legatee;
2. the legal personal representative of a deceased universal or residuary legatee;
3. beneficiaries who would have been entitled to a grant on an intestacy;
4. a legatee with a beneficial interest;
5. a creditor.

In SQ p73 Q6, Terrence's will names no executor and the residuary legatee (sister Daisy) predeceased him, so Daisy's personal representative has the prior right, ahead of the wife and parents (specific legatees) and the creditor (key B).

*Source note: PR5 s4, s35; PR3 s5; Cyril-PR §1.4.2, §4.4; SQ p73 Q6 (key p128: B).*

### pcp-m-0-61 — Letters of administration: who may apply (s 18 PAA)
*Keywords: letters of administration, who can apply, s 18 PAA, next of kin, spouse, Toh Seok Kheng v Huang Huiqun, ISA priority, creditor, same degree, Tan Hui Cheng Lily, renunciation not needed same class, s 18(2), s 18(4), relationship documents, marriage certificate*

**End point:** The court grants administration having regard to the rights of everyone interested (s 18(2)): the spouse, next of kin, or one or more of them, and failing them a creditor (s 18(4)). The most suitable applicant is found by the order of entitlement under s 7 ISA (*Toh Seok Kheng v Huang Huiqun* [2011] 1 SLR 737 at [23]). Persons in the same class need not join or renounce (*Tan Hui Cheng Lily*), so SQ p73 Q7 option A is wrong. The application must give the deceased's nationality, religion and marital status, relationships, and particulars of beneficiaries, minors, persons lacking capacity, dead next of kin and persons with prior or equal rights (Form 162).

*Source note: PR5 s34, s36, s38; Cyril-PR §4.4 (Step 0); SQ p73 Q7 (key p128: B). FJ(PAOM)R 2024 P 6 r 3(3), (7) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026): the application must state how prior rights were cleared off, any minority or life interest, how each person is related and by what law or custom they are entitled. The rules do not themselves require marriage or birth certificates to be produced; the SQ key treats option C as wrong.*

### pcp-m-0-62 — Renunciation and clearing off prior rights
*Keywords: renunciation, renounce, s 3 PAA, s 5 PAA, Form 166, attested by solicitor, oral renunciation at hearing, withdraw renunciation, express renunciation, constructive renunciation, clearing off, citation to accept or refuse, P 6 r 28(5), P 6 r 18*

**End point:** An applicant without priority must clear off everyone above him by express renunciations (Form 166; s 3(2)(b) PAA; P 6 r 28(5)). A renunciation is made orally at a hearing, or in writing signed and attested by a solicitor or commissioner (s 3(2)). A person who has renounced cannot later apply unless the court allows withdrawal (s 5). If a renunciation cannot be had, cite the person to accept or refuse the grant (see [0-70](#pcp-m-0-70)).

*Source note: PR5 s12, s18, s36; Cyril-PR §4.4. Form numbers follow FJC PD 2024 per PR5 (Cyril cites the old Form 53).*

### pcp-m-0-63 — Number of administrators (s 6 PAA)
*Keywords: s 6 PAA, minority interest, life interest, two administrators, trust corporation, not less than 2 individuals, infant beneficiary*

**End point:** Where a minor is a beneficiary or a life interest arises, letters of administration go to a trust corporation (with or without an individual) or to at least two individuals (s 6). This does not apply to probate: executors named in the will take probate however many they are.

*Source note: PR5 s33, s37; PR3 s6 (heading).*

### pcp-m-0-64 — Filing the originating application: documents and timing
*Keywords: originating application without notice, Form 162, certified true copy of will, digital death certificate, death extract, Administration Oath, Form 167, s 28 PAA, translation, court interpreter, Malay words, affidavit of due execution, affidavit of foreign law, P 6 r 13, caveat search, probate search, 15 December 2003, original will 4.30pm next working day, Probate Counter, delay more than 6 months, P 6 r 3(8), aliases, P 6 r 4, electronic checklist, original passport not needed*

**End point:** File an originating application without notice (Form 162) with:
- a certified true copy of the will (certified on every page or by a cover page);
- the digitally verifiable death certificate or extract (no CTC needed), or a CTC of the death certificate;
- the Administration Oath (Form 167; s 28 PAA), now or with the supporting affidavit;
- if needed: renunciations, translations of non-English documents (P 1 r 11 FJ(G)R 2024; FJC PD 218), an affidavit of due execution ([0-16](#pcp-m-0-16)), and an affidavit of foreign law (P 6 r 13).

The application states aliases (P 6 r 4) and the reasons for any delay over 6 months from death (P 6 r 3(8)). Caveat and probate searches are optional where death was on or after 15 Dec 2003, as the system checks. For earlier deaths, state whether any are known. The original will (or foreign grant) must reach the Probate Counter by 4.30pm the next working day.

In SQ p71 Q1 (mother died in Dubai), only the original will is needed: the Arabic death certificate must be filed with its translation, and her passport is not needed (key C).

*Source note: PR5 s10–21; PR-SYL p7 item 4(c), (e)–(f); SQ p71 Q1 (key p128: C); SQ p73 Q7 D (searches not mandatory).*

### pcp-m-0-65 — Supporting affidavit, Schedule of Assets and issue of the grant
*Keywords: supporting affidavit, Form 163, 14 days, Schedule of Assets, Form 177, market value at date of death, joint accounts, zero balance account, undertaking to amend, amend without leave, supplementary affidavit, FJC PD 212, grant issued automatically, digital grant, printed grant*

**End point:** File the supporting affidavit (Form 163) within 14 days of the originating application, exhibiting:
- A: the originating application;
- B: the will and death certificate;
- C: the Schedule of Assets (Form 177), if available.

List assets at market value at the date of death. Joint assets pass by survivorship and are usually left out. Zero-balance accounts need a reason. The affidavit includes the "true and accurate to the best of my knowledge" averment and undertaking to amend (FJC PD 212(5)). If banks will not disclose until the application is sealed, file the Schedule later with a supplementary affidavit. It may be amended without leave by a supplementary affidavit. The grant issues automatically, as a digital copy (a printed grant must be asked for when filing), with no request to extract.

*Source note: PR5 s22–30, s58; PR-SYL p7–8 item 4(d), (h).*

### pcp-m-0-66 — Administration bond and sureties (s 29 PAA)
*Keywords: administration bond, sureties, two sureties, Form 168, s 29 PAA, estate exceeds S$5 million, minority interest, life interest, beneficiary lacking capacity, grantee is a creditor, Public Trustee exempt s 29(4), s 29(5) administrator entitled to whole estate, dispensation of sureties, s 29(3), FJC PD 214, FJC PD 215, consent of adult beneficiaries, probate no bond*

**End point:** No bond is needed for probate. A grantee of letters of administration must give an administration bond (Form 168) with 2 sureties where:
- (a) the gross estate exceeds S$5 million;
- (b) there is a minority interest;
- (c) there is a life interest;
- (d) a beneficiary lacks capacity;
- (e) the grantee is a creditor;
- (f) the Registrar thinks fit (s 29; P 6 r 31; FJC PD 214).

The Public Trustee gives no security (s 29(4)). Sureties may ordinarily be dispensed with where the administrator takes the whole estate (s 29(5)). The court may vary or dispense with sureties (s 29(3)) on summons and affidavit by all administrators, with the written consent of all adult beneficiaries (FJC PD 215). Administrators sign the bond jointly.

*Source note: PR5 s41–46; PR3 s13–14; SQ p73 Q7 (key p128: B).*

### pcp-m-0-67 — Resealing a foreign grant (ss 47–49 PAA)
*Keywords: resealing, reseal, foreign grant, Commonwealth, Hong Kong, s 47 PAA, s 47(2) same effect, s 47(4) domicile, s 49, P 3 r 13, FJC PD 211, WKR v WKQ, memorandum of resealing, sealed certified true copy of foreign grant, non-Commonwealth grant fresh application*

**End point:** A grant by a court of a Commonwealth country or Hong Kong may be resealed in Singapore (ss 47–49). Once resealed it has the same effect as a Singapore grant (s 47(2)). If the deceased was not domiciled within that court's jurisdiction, it is resealed only if it is a grant the High Court would have made (s 47(4)). File the sealed or certified foreign grant (or a digitally verifiable one with a solicitor's certificate) with an originating application. It must give domicile, the grant particulars, whether the deceased lived or did business in Singapore in the last 12 months, Singapore creditors, and Singapore property. The court issues a Memorandum of Resealing. A grant from elsewhere needs a fresh Singapore application. The syllabus also lists *WKR v WKQ* [2023] SGHC(A) 35 on resealing principles; the Course Materials do not summarise it, and no holding is relied on here.

*Source note: PR5 s5, s47–56; PR-SYL p6 item 3(a)(iv)–(v). *

### pcp-m-0-68 — Contentious probate: probate actions
*Keywords: probate action, contentious probate, Part 7 Probate Rules, P 7 r 1, testamentary capacity dispute, undue influence, forgery, revoked by marriage, passing over executor s 8 PAA, revocation of grant s 32 PAA, originating claim P 7 r 2, intervener P 7 r 4, notice of action P 4 r 9, discontinuance P 7 r 16, administration pending trial s 20, P 7 r 21, costs P 16 r 3(5), solemn form*

**End point:** A probate action is for a grant, for revocation of a grant, or for a decree for or against a will's validity. Typical issues are capacity, undue influence, fraud or forgery, revocation by later marriage, passing over a named executor (s 8 PAA) and revocation of a grant (s 32 PAA).
- It is begun by originating claim issued out of the Registry, endorsed with the parties' interests (P 7 r 2). A non-party may apply by summons and affidavit for permission to intervene, then files a notice of intention to contest (P 7 r 4).
- Anyone with an adverse interest may be served with a notice of action (P 4 r 9; P 7 r 5). The court may order the action discontinued or dismissed, and a grant to the person entitled (P 7 r 16).
- Pending the action, the court may grant administration to someone who cannot distribute (s 20 PAA), applied for by summons in the pending action (P 7 r 21).
- Costs: a party who only insists on proof in solemn form and cross-examines the will's witnesses is not ordered to pay the supporter's costs, unless there was no reasonable ground to oppose (P 16 r 3(5)).

*Source note: PR-SYL p8–10 item 5(a)–(e); FJ(PAOM)R 2024 P 7 rr 2, 4, 5, 16, 21 and P 16 r 3(5) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026). Conflict: Cyril-PR §4.5 describes the 2014 procedure (writs, appearances); the 2024 Rules use originating claims and notices of intention to contest.*

### pcp-m-0-69 — Caveats
*Keywords: caveat, s 33 PAA, P 6 r 34, Form 170, enter caveat, warning to caveat, Form 171, notice of intention to contest or not contest, Form 172, 14 days, summons for directions 21 days, caveat expires 6 months, P 6 r 34(3), caveat cease to have effect, withdraw caveat, P 6 r 37*

**End point:** Anyone claiming an interest may enter a caveat after the death and before a grant, so no grant is made without notice to him (s 33 PAA; P 6 r 34). It lasts 6 months from entry (P 6 r 34(3)) unless extended or ended.
- The caveat is filed in Form 170 (r 34(2)). The Registrar may not make a grant knowing of an effective caveat, but a caveat does not stop a grant made on the day it is filed (r 34(5)–(6)).
- An interested person may warn the caveator by a Form 171 warning, served on him (r 34(7)–(8)).
- The caveator files a notice of intention to contest or not contest (Form 172) within 14 days after service of the warning, and serves it (r 34(11)–(12)).
- A caveator who files that notice must file and serve a summons for directions within 21 days after the warning was served (r 34(13)).
- If he does neither, the person warning may file an affidavit of service and apply by summons for the caveat to cease to have effect (r 34(15)). A caveator who does not contest may withdraw (r 34(9)).

*Source note: PR-SYL p9 item 5(c)(i); FJ(PAOM)R 2024 P 6 rr 34–37 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026). Conflict: Cyril-PR §4.5.2 gives the 2014 procedure (appearance in 8 days, summons for directions within 14 days after the time for appearance); the 2024 Rules (14 days to file Form 172; summons within 21 days of service) are followed.*

### pcp-m-0-70 — Citations
*Keywords: citation, citor, citation to accept or refuse a grant, citation to propound a will, caveat before citation, P 6 r 38(4), P 6 r 39, P 6 r 40, served personally, affidavit verifying citation, Form 173, notice of intention to contest Form 172, 14 days, deemed renounced, intermeddling executor, grant as if will invalid*

**End point:** A citation issues from the Registry in Form 173, verified by the citor's affidavit (P 6 r 38(1)–(2)). The citor must enter a caveat first (r 38(4)) and serve the citation personally unless substituted service is ordered (r 38(5)). The person cited may file a notice of intention to contest or not contest (Form 172) within 14 days after service (r 38(7)).
- **To accept or refuse a grant** (r 39(1)): issued by someone who would be entitled if the person cited renounced. If no notice is filed in time, or he says he will not contest, the citor may apply for an order that he is deemed to have renounced, and for permission to take a grant himself (r 39(6)).
- **Executor who has intermeddled:** after 6 months from death, any interested person may cite him to show cause why he should not be ordered to take a grant (r 39(3)).
- **To propound a will** (r 40): against the executors and those interested under a will, by a citor with a contrary interest. If no one contests, or a contesting party does not propound the will with reasonable diligence, the citor may apply for permission to apply for a grant as if the will were invalid (r 40(2)).

*Source note: PR-SYL p9 item 5(c)(ii); PR5 s36; FJ(PAOM)R 2024 P 6 rr 38–40 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026). Conflict: Cyril-PR §4.5.2 gives 8 days to enter an appearance (2014 Rules); the 2024 Rules give 14 days to file Form 172, which is followed.*

### pcp-m-0-71 — When personal representatives can act; acting jointly
*Keywords: executor before grant, Lee Han Tiong v Tay Yok Swee, executor authority from will, administrator only after grant, Teo Gim Tiong v Krishnasamy Pushpavathi, relation back, Wong Moy exception, beneficiary sues to protect estate, Mustaq Ahmad v Ayaz Ahmed, one executor binds estate, administrators act jointly, Tacplas Property Services v Lee Peter Michael, ratified*

**End point:**
- **Executor:** his authority comes from the will, so he may start proceedings before probate. He needs the grant to get judgment or enforce (*Lee Han Tiong v Tay Yok Swee* [1996] 2 SLR(R) 833).
- **Administrator:** has no authority until the grant. A next of kin who settled before the grant could not bind the estate; the right course was a stay pending the grant (*Teo Gim Tiong* [2014] 4 SLR 15).
- **Beneficiary:** may sue to protect estate assets where the PR will not, in any kind of action, but no better placed than a proper trustee (*Wong Moy* exception; *Mustaq Ahmad* [2024] SGHC(A) 17: minority oppression claim).
- **Joint acts:** one executor's act binds the estate and the co-executors. Administrators must act jointly, and one administrator's act does not bind the estate unless all ratify (*Tacplas Property Services v Lee Peter Michael* [2000] 1 SLR(R) 159).

In SQ p74 Q8, only the named executor (option A) can sue before a grant (key A). Spouse, child, an LPA donee (whose authority ends on death) and an applicant before extraction cannot.

*Source note: PR-SYL p10 items 6–7; PR3 s18; Cyril-PR §4.6–4.7; SQ p74 Q8 (key p128: A).*

### pcp-m-0-72 — Statutory principles (s 3 MCA)
*Keywords: Mental Capacity Act, MCA s 3, presumption of capacity, all practicable steps, unwise decision, best interests, least restrictive, Code of Practice, s 41*

**End point:**
- (2) A person is assumed to have capacity unless it is shown that he lacks it.
- (3) He is not treated as unable to decide until all practicable steps to help him have failed.
- (4) An unwise decision does not by itself mean he lacks capacity.
- (5) An act or decision for a person lacking capacity must be in his best interests.
- (6) Consider whether the purpose can be achieved in a way less restrictive of his rights and freedom.

The Code of Practice guides how the Act is applied (s 41).

*Source note: PR6 s4–5; PR-SYL p10 MCA item 1; Cyril-PR §5.1.1.*

### pcp-m-0-73 — Lack of capacity: the test (ss 4–5; Re BKR)
*Keywords: lack of capacity, s 4(1) MCA, impairment or disturbance of mind or brain, s 5(1), understand, retain, use or weigh, communicate, time-specific, decision-specific, balance of probabilities, s 4(4), Re BKR, functional, clinical, causal, but-for causation, BUV v BUU, medical report, age or appearance*

**End point:** A person lacks capacity in a matter if, at the material time, he cannot make a decision on it because of an impairment of, or disturbance in the functioning of, the mind or brain (s 4(1)).
- The impairment may be temporary or permanent (s 4(2)).
- Age, appearance or behaviour alone does not establish it (s 4(3)).
- The standard is the balance of probabilities (s 4(4)).

He is unable to decide if he cannot do any one of these (s 5(1)):
- understand the relevant information;
- retain it (even briefly is enough, s 5(3));
- use or weigh it;
- communicate the decision.

The test is time-specific and decision-specific. *Re BKR* [2015] 4 SLR 81 (CA) sets three parts:
- functional inability;
- a clinical impairment of the mind;
- a causal link (but-for causation is enough; it need not be the sole cause).

Courts look at the person's actual circumstances (*BUV v BUU* [2020] 3 SLR 1041), so do not rely only on a medical certificate.

*Source note: PR6 s6–10; PR-SYL p10–11 item 2; Cyril-PR §5.2. BUV v BUU holding as described in PR6 s10 (case named in PR-SYL).*

### pcp-m-0-74 — Best interests (s 6)
*Keywords: best interests, s 6 MCA, relevant circumstances, regain capacity, encourage participation, life-sustaining treatment not motivated by desire to cause death, property preserved for maintenance, past and present wishes, written statement, beliefs and values, consult carers, donee, deputy, s 6(11) reasonable belief*

**End point:** The decision cannot rest on age, appearance or behaviour (s 6(1)). Consider all relevant circumstances (s 6(2)), and in particular:
- whether and when P may regain capacity (s 6(3));
- encouraging P to take part (s 6(4));
- for life-sustaining treatment, no motive to bring about death (s 6(5));
- for property, preserving it for P's maintenance in his life (s 6(7));
- P's past and present wishes (including written statements), beliefs and values (s 6(8));
- the views of anyone P named, carers, and any donee or deputy (s 6(9)).

Anyone other than the court complies if, having done this, he reasonably believes the act is in P's best interests (s 6(11)).

*Source note: PR6 s11–16; Cyril-PR §5.1.2.*

### pcp-m-0-75 — Acts in connection with care or treatment (ss 7–10)
*Keywords: s 7 MCA, protection from liability, care or treatment, reasonable steps to establish lack of capacity, reasonable belief, best interests, s 8 restraint, necessary to prevent harm, proportionate, s 9 necessaries reasonable price, s 10 use P's money, reimbursement, negligence not excluded*

**End point:** A person (D) doing an act in connection with P's care or treatment is not liable beyond what he would be if P had consented, if D first took reasonable steps to establish P's lack of capacity and reasonably believed P lacked capacity and the act was in P's best interests (s 7). Liability for negligence remains. Restraint is also covered only if D reasonably believes it is needed to prevent harm to P and it is proportionate (s 8). P must pay a reasonable price for necessaries (s 9). D may use P's money for the expense, or be reimbursed (s 10). Section 7 does not authorise acts against a donee's or deputy's decision.

*Source note: PR-SYL p11 item 3(a); Cyril-PR §5.3; MCA ss 7–10 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026) (s 7(3) also excludes clinical trials and human biomedical research; s 8(5) bars acts inconsistent with a donee's or deputy's decision).*

### pcp-m-0-76 — General powers of the court (ss 19, 20, 22, 23)
*Keywords: s 19 declarations, lacks capacity, lawfulness of act, s 20 court decision or deputy, s 22 personal welfare, s 23 property and affairs, no LPA no deputy needed, one-off court order, hospital charges direct payment*

**End point:** On application, even with no LPA or deputy, the court may:
- declare whether P lacks capacity, or whether an act is lawful (s 19);
- make the decision itself by order, or appoint a deputy (s 20);
- decide personal welfare matters (s 22) and property and affairs matters (s 23).

A court decision is preferred to appointing a deputy (s 20(4)).

*Source note: PR-SYL p11 item 3(b); PR6 s54, s59 (Form 63 direct-payment orders without a deputy); Cyril-PR §5.5.*

### pcp-m-0-77 — Excluded decisions (ss 26–29)
*Keywords: excluded decisions, s 26 MCA, marriage, sexual relations, divorce three years separation, adoption, religion, change of gender, sterilisation, abortion, organ donation, advance medical directive, body donation, s 27 mental disorder treatment, s 28 voting, s 29 suicide, clinical trials*

**End point:** Nobody, whether donee, deputy or court, can decide these for P under the MCA:
- **s 26:** consenting to marriage, sexual relations, divorce on 3 years' separation, or adoption; adopting or renouncing a religion; gender change; sterilisation; abortion; organ donation objections; making or revoking an advance medical directive; body donation.
- **s 27:** treatment for mental disorder regulated by the Mental Health (Care and Treatment) Act.
- **s 28:** voting in elections or referendums.
- **s 29:** the MCA does not change the law on murder, manslaughter or abetting suicide, or on biomedical research and clinical trials.

*Source note: PR-SYL p11 item 3(c); PR6 s32, s70 (table images); Cyril-PR §5.4; MCA s 26 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026) (list (a)–(j) as above; adoption limb amended by Act 20 of 2022 wef 15 Oct 2024).*

### pcp-m-0-78 — Lasting power of attorney: nature, donor, Form 1 and Form 2
*Keywords: lasting power of attorney, LPA, s 11 MCA, donor, donee, personal welfare, property and affairs, age 21, capacity to execute, First Schedule, Form 1 standard, Form 2 lawyer drafted, regulation 4C, maximum 2 donees, replacement donee*

**End point:** An LPA lets the donor (P) give donees authority over his personal welfare and/or property and affairs, to be used when P lacks capacity (s 11(1)). It is valid only if:
- s 12 (donees) is complied with;
- it is made and registered in the prescribed form (First Schedule; reg 4C MCR);
- P is at least 21 and has capacity when executing it (s 11(2)).

| | Form 1 | Form 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Who drafts | Donor fills it in | Lawyer drafts customised powers |
| Powers | General powers, basic restrictions from a fixed checklist | Specific, tailored powers |
| Donees | Up to 2, plus 1 replacement | No limit |

*Source note: PR6 s19–22, s26; PR-SYL p11 item 4(a)(ii).*

### pcp-m-0-79 — Who may be a donee; joint or joint and several
*Keywords: donee, s 12 MCA, age 21, no remuneration, professional donee, not related by blood or marriage, undischarged bankrupt cannot be property donee, s 12(2), jointly, jointly and severally, default jointly, s 12(5), s 12(6), s 12(7), replacement donee*

**End point:**
- **Property and affairs only:** an individual of 21 or above acting without pay; a professional donee not related to P by blood or marriage; or a non-individual professional donee (s 12(1)(a)).
- **Any other case (welfare):** an individual of 21 or above without pay, or an individual professional donee not related to P (s 12(1)(b)).
- An undischarged bankrupt cannot be a donee for property and affairs (s 12(2)).
- Several donees may act jointly, jointly and severally, or a mix. If the LPA is silent, they act jointly (s 12(5)).
- **Jointly:** if one donee fails the requirements, no LPA is created (s 12(6)).
- **Jointly and severally:** the LPA is still created for the donees who comply (s 12(7)).

*Source note: PR6 s25–27; PR-SYL p11 item 4(a)(i); Cyril-PR §5.6.*

### pcp-m-0-80 — Donee's powers and restrictions (ss 11(4), 13, 14)
*Keywords: donee restrictions, s 13 MCA, donor has capacity, life-sustaining treatment, restraint, s 14 gifts, cash gifts, execute will for donor, CPF nomination, insurance nomination, sell property court order, Form 1 restriction, donee duties, Code of Practice 8.5, keep accounts, s 13(10) medical report*

**End point:** The donee's authority is subject to the MCA principles and best interests, and to the LPA's own conditions (s 11(4)). A donee must not decide if he knows or reasonably believes P has capacity on that matter (s 13(1)).
- **Personal welfare:** no decisions on life-sustaining treatment, or treatment needed to prevent serious deterioration. No restraint, unless authorised, needed to prevent harm and proportionate (s 13(2)–(8)).
- **Property and affairs:** cannot execute a will for P; cannot make or revoke a CPF memorandum or a CPF or insurance nomination; cannot make cash gifts unless expressly authorised (s 14(3)); cannot sell property without a court order where Form 1 prohibits it (ss 13(9)–(9A), 14).

Duties include acting in P's best interests with care and skill, not delegating, not profiting, keeping accounts, and keeping P's money separate.

*Source note: PR6 s28–33; PR-SYL p11 item 4(d); Cyril-PR §5.6.*

### pcp-m-0-81 — Certificate issuers (reg 7 MCR; Sum Chong Mun)
*Keywords: certificate issuer, reg 7 Mental Capacity Regulations, psychiatrist, accredited doctor, advocate and solicitor with practising certificate, disqualified family member, donee, employee of donor, business partner, care home staff, Law Society v Sum Chong Mun, meet the donor, explain LPA, no fraud or undue pressure, tea lady, SQ*

**End point:** Certificate issuers are psychiatrists, OPG-accredited medical practitioners, and advocates and solicitors with a practising certificate practising Singapore law (reg 7(1)). Disqualified (reg 7(2)):
- a family member of the donor or a donee (spouse, child including adopted or step-child, parent, parent-in-law, sibling, or a blood or marriage relative in the same household: reg 2);
- a donee of this or an earlier registered LPA;
- a director or employee of a licensed trust company acting as donee;
- a business partner or employee of the donor or a donee;
- an owner, director, manager or employee of P's care facility, or their family.

The issuer certifies that P understands the LPA's purpose and scope, there is no fraud or undue pressure, and nothing else prevents creation (First Schedule para 2(e)). A solicitor must meet P personally, explain it, satisfy himself of capacity (or refer to a psychiatrist), and not delegate (*Law Society v Sum Chong Mun* [2017] 4 SLR 707).

SQ p74 Q9 (key A): the partner may certify for the firm's tea lady appointing her niece (A). He may not where the donee is his firm partner (B: business partner of a donee, reg 7(2)(f)), or where the donor is his own wife (C: family member of the donor, reg 7(2)(a)).

*Source note: PR6 s34–37; PR-SYL p11 item 4(b); SQ p74 Q9 (key p128: A); MCR regs 2 ("family member") and 7(1)–(3) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026). Conflict: the key also treats D (donee is the issuer's father-in-law) as barred. Reg 7(2) bars the issuer only if he is a family member of a donee, and the reg 2 list (spouse, child or step-child, parent, parent-in-law, sibling, same-household relative) does not include a son-in-law; so on the text D is barred only if they live in the same household. Answer per the key in the exam, but know the text.*

### pcp-m-0-82 — Registering an LPA
*Keywords: register LPA, Office of the Public Guardian, OPGO, Singpass, hardcopy s 11(2A), physical disability, system unavailable, registration fee, Form 1 free for citizens, Form 2 fee, 3-week waiting period, objection, regulation 13, regulation 15, withdraw before registration, deliver within 6 months, activation, medical report loss of capacity*

**End point:**
- **Online (default):** via OPGO with Singpass. The donor (and, for Form 2, the lawyer's draft) completes the form; donees accept on OPGO; the certificate issuer certifies digitally.
- **Hardcopy:** only where the donor or donee cannot use the system because of disability or other circumstance, the system is down, or as prescribed (s 11(2A)). Lodge with the OPG within 6 months of signing.
- **Fees:**

| Registration fee (from 1 May 2026) | Form 1 | Form 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Singapore citizens | Nil | $30 |
| Singapore PRs | $30 | $30 |
| Others | $160 | $160 |

  Cancelling a registration costs $28. The certificate issuer's professional fee is separate.

- There is a mandatory 3-week wait after the OPG notifies the donees. If no valid objection is received (regs 13, 15 MCR), it is registered. It can be withdrawn any time before registration.
- **Activation:** the donee acts only once a registered medical practitioner certifies (prescribed report) that P has lost capacity (s 13(10)).

*Source note: PR6 s23, s38–50; PR-SYL p11 item 4(a)(iii); Mental Capacity (Public Guardian Fees) Regulations 2010 Schedule (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026), amended by S 272/2026 wef 1 May 2026 and S 436/2026 wef 1 Jul 2026. ### pcp-m-0-83 — Revocation, termination and the court's powers over LPAs (ss 15–18)
*Keywords: revoke LPA, s 15 MCA, donor with capacity, revocation form, reg 21 MCR, notice to donees and Public Guardian, revocation fee $28, donor bankrupt, donee dies, donee loses capacity, donee bankrupt, disclaims, replacement donee, jointly and severally survives, s 17 validity, fraud undue pressure, s 18 directions, interpret LPA, authorise gifts*

**End point:** A donor with capacity may revoke an LPA (s 15(2)), by a new OPGO application or the revocation form with written notice to all donees and the Public Guardian (reg 21 MCR), for a $28 fee. Automatic effects (s 15(3)–(8)):
- P's bankruptcy revokes the property and affairs powers only;
- the donee's death, loss of capacity, bankruptcy (property powers only), disclaimer, or a corporate donee's winding up ends that donee's appointment and revokes the LPA, unless there is a replacement donee or another donee appointed jointly and severally remains.

The court may decide whether an LPA is valid, revoked or ended, and stop registration or revoke it for fraud, undue pressure or misconduct (s 17). It may interpret it, give directions and consents, require accounts, relieve liability, and authorise gifts beyond s 14(3) (s 18).

*Source note: PR6 s47–48; PR-SYL p11 item 4(c), (e); Cyril-PR §5.6.5–5.6.6 (ss 17–18).*

### pcp-m-0-84 — Deputies: when, who and the principles (ss 20, 21, 24, 25A)
*Keywords: deputy, deputyship, s 20 MCA, no LPA, P under 21, s 21 parents preferred, s 20(4) court decision preferred, limited scope and duration, s 24 requirements, age 21, professional deputy, s 25A registered with Public Guardian, security, reports, simplified application P 5 r 8, joint deputies*

**End point:** Where P lacks capacity in personal welfare or property matters, the court may decide for him or appoint a deputy (s 20), usually where there is no LPA covering it. P need not be 21 if he will still lack capacity at 21. The court applies s 3 and best interests, and prefers:
- its own decision to a deputy;
- powers as limited in scope and duration as practicable (s 20(4));
- the parents or guardian of P as deputy (s 21).

Deputy requirements (s 24):
- for property and affairs: an individual of 21 or above without pay, a professional deputy not related to P, or a non-individual professional deputy;
- otherwise: individuals only.

Professional deputies must register with the Public Guardian (s 25A). The court may require security and reports. An uncontested application with every relevant person consenting, for P aged at least 18, may be a simplified application (P 5 r 8 FJ(G)R 2024).

*Source note: PR6 s53–54; PR-SYL p12 item 5(a)(i)–(ii); Cyril-PR §5.7. SQ p75 Q10: a 22-year-old daughter is an eligible deputy.*

### pcp-m-0-85 — Deputyship application procedure
*Keywords: deputyship application, originating application, Form 59, supporting affidavit, Form 60, Form 63, permission to apply, s 38 MCA, related by blood or marriage no permission, P 5 r 7 General Rules, medical report not older than 6 months, examined within 12 months, doctor's affidavit Form 61, OPG search, Wills Registry search, relevant persons, serve within 1 week, Form 108B consent, notification of P, Form 81, litigation representative, TWD v UQE, costs P 22 r 4*

**End point:**
- **Permission:** needed unless the applicant is related to P by blood or marriage (s 38; P 5 r 7). Ask for it in the main application.
- **Originating application:** states welfare and/or property, joint or joint-and-several deputies, and the powers sought.
- **Supporting affidavit** (Form 60; Form 63 for direct payment of hospital or nursing home charges without a deputy): one affidavit for all applicants, exhibiting OPG searches (LPA, past MCA orders) and a Wills Registry search.
- **Medical report:** not more than 6 months old when filed, from an examination less than 12 months before the report, with a clear opinion on incapacity and prognosis, exhibited to the doctor's affidavit (Form 61). It must match the application on welfare or property.
- **Relevant persons:** spouse, children and siblings 21 and above, parents, others interested or caring. Serve them, or file their consents (Form 108B). Serve a care home within 1 week. An objector applies to be joined.
- **Notify P** in a suitable way (P 7 r 22; Form 81).
- Third-party intervention: see *TWD v UQE* [2019] 3 SLR 662 at [56]–[84] (listed in the syllabus; the Course Materials do not summarise it).
- **Costs** are paid from P's estate unless ordered otherwise (P 22 r 4).

*Source note: PR6 s55–67; PR-SYL p12 item 5(a)(iii); Cyril-PR §5.7; FJ(PAOM)R 2024 P 16 r 3(7) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026) (deputy or donee conducting proceedings for P gets costs out of P's estate unless the Court otherwise orders).*

### pcp-m-0-86 — Deputy's powers and restrictions (ss 22, 23, 25)
*Keywords: deputy powers, s 22 personal welfare, where P lives, contact, s 23 property and affairs, sell property, collect rent, carry on business, discharge debts, maintain spouse, parent, minor child, legal proceedings, s 25 restrictions, cannot prohibit contact, cannot change healthcare provider, cannot make gifts, cannot make CPF or insurance nomination, cannot execute will, life-sustaining treatment, Louise Pansy SQ*

**End point:** The court may confer powers within ss 22–23:
- **Welfare:** where P lives, contact.
- **Property:** manage, sell or charge P's property; carry on his business; carry out his contracts; pay his debts; settle property on trust; maintain or benefit P's spouse, parents, minor or intellectually disabled children; exercise P's powers; conduct legal proceedings.

A deputy may not be given power (s 25) to:
- prohibit a named person from contact with P;
- direct a change of the person responsible for P's healthcare;
- make gifts of P's property;
- make or revoke a CPF or insurance nomination;
- execute a will for P;
- refuse or consent to life-sustaining or deterioration-preventing treatment.

A deputy cannot act where he reasonably believes P has capacity on the matter.

SQ p75 Q10 (Louise in a coma; daughter Pansy deputy): collecting rent (II) and maintaining Louise's parents and Pansy's 13-year-old brother (III) are within s 23. Barring Louise's sisters from visiting (I) is a power s 25 says a deputy cannot be given. Conflict: the answer key gives D (I, II and III); on s 25 the answer is C (II and III).

*Source note: PR6 s68–69; PR-SYL p12 item 5(a)(iv); Cyril-PR §5.7.3; SQ p75 Q10 (key p128: D). Conflict: SQ key D v PR6 s69 and MCA s 25(2)(a) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026) ("Nothing in section 20(5) or 22 permits a deputy to be given power to prohibit a named person from having contact with P"); the statute is followed. The court itself may still make a no-contact order under s 22.*

### pcp-m-0-87 — The Public Guardian (ss 31–32)
*Keywords: Public Guardian, Office of the Public Guardian, OPG, s 31 MCA, s 32, register of LPAs, register of deputies, supervise deputies, Board of Visitors, security, reports from donees and deputies, complaints, investigate, require information*

**End point:** The Public Guardian keeps the registers of LPAs, deputy orders and professional deputies, and supervises court-appointed deputies. It:
- directs Board of Visitors visits to donees, deputies or P, and receives security and reports;

- deals with complaints about donees and deputies, and investigates contraventions;

### pcp-m-1-1 — Parties and their capacity
*Keywords: parties to conveyancing, capacity, individual 21 years old, sui juris, bankrupt, company capacity to own land, Companies Act s 23(2) deleted, society cannot hold land, trustees of society, foreign person, Singapore company, approved purchaser*

**End point:**
- **Individuals:** must be 21, sui juris and not bankrupt.
- **Companies:** a company registered under the Companies Act generally has capacity to own land. The old s 23(2) Companies Act licence requirement for non-profit companies has been deleted, so they now have full capacity (CS1 s31). Check the RPA definitions ("foreign person", "Singapore company", "approved purchaser") for residential property.
- **Societies:** cannot hold land in the society's name, only through their trustees.

*Source note: RE1 s4; RE-CS1 s31 (s 23(2) CA deleted); Cyril-RE (Slide 1 table).*

### pcp-m-1-2 — Types of property and land registration systems
*Keywords: Register of Deeds, common law title, Land Titles Register, Torrens system, HDB flat, minimum occupation period, JTC industrial property, non-residential, commercial, residential property, strata property, BMSMA, Land Titles (Strata) Act, zoning*

**End point:** Singapore has two land registration systems: the Register of Deeds (common law, unregistered land) and the Land Titles Register (Torrens). The type of property drives the rules:
- **HDB flats:** HDB rules (e.g. minimum occupation period); for information only in this course.
- **JTC industrial property:** JTC rules.
- **Non-residential** (commercial or industrial): GST may apply; no ABSD.
- **Residential:** RPA restrictions on foreign persons; ABSD.
- **Strata units:** BMSMA and Land Titles (Strata) Act obligations.

Use must follow zoning (residential, industrial, commercial), which also affects stamp duty.

*Source note: RE1 s3, s5–6, s11; RE-CS1 s2.*

### pcp-m-1-3 — Sale by a deceased owner's personal representative (s 35 CLPA)
*Keywords: s 35 CLPA, power of sale, legal personal representative, deceased owner, 6 years from death, sanction of court, power of sale in will, trust for sale, executor sells property*

**End point:** A personal representative has a power of sale (s 35(1) CLPA). But no sale or mortgage of a deceased's land may be made by the personal representative more than 6 years after death unless the court sanctions it, or the sale is under a power of sale or trust for sale express or implied in the will (s 35(2)).

> **CLPA s 35(2)**
> - No sale or mortgage of land of a deceased person by his legal personal representative after 6 years from his death,
> - unless with the sanction of the court, or
> - unless made under a power of sale, trust for sale or mortgage expressly contained in or implied from the will.

*Source note: RE1 s7; CLPA s 35(1)–(2) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026).*

### pcp-m-1-4 — Formalities: contracts for the sale of land
*Keywords: s 6(d) Civil Law Act, evidenced in writing, memorandum in writing, signed, email sufficient, Joseph Mathew v Singh Chiranjeev, oral contract, Law Society Conditions of Sale 2020*

**End point:** A contract for the sale of land must be evidenced in writing (s 6(d) CLA). Email correspondence can satisfy this (*Joseph Mathew v Singh Chiranjeev* [2010] 1 SLR 338). Most contracts incorporate the Law Society's Conditions of Sale 2020.

*Source note: RE1 s12; CLA s 6(d) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026): no action on a contract for the sale or other disposition of immovable property unless it, or a memorandum or note of it, is in writing signed by the party to be charged or his authorised agent.*

### pcp-m-1-5 — Ways to conclude a contract; transfers not under a contract
*Keywords: option to purchase, sale and purchase agreement, exchange of correspondence, prescribed agreement, Housing Developers (Control and Licensing) Act, Sale of Commercial Properties Act, tender, public auction, put and call option, memorandum in writing, intestacy distribution, matrimonial transfer, MCST statutory right, nature of transaction*

**End point:** A conveyancing contract may be concluded by:
- an option to purchase (exercised);
- a sale and purchase agreement;
- an exchange of correspondence;
- the prescribed agreements under the Housing Developers (Control and Licensing) Act and the Sale of Commercial Properties Act;
- a tender, a public auction, or a put and call option;
- an oral contract evidenced by a memorandum in writing.

Some conveyances are not under a contract: distribution on intestacy, matrimonial orders, and exercise of statutory rights (e.g. an MCST under the BMSMA). Transactions include purchase, sale, mortgage, lease, licence, charge, subdivision, partition and amalgamation.

*Source note: RE3 s3; RE1 s11–12.*

### pcp-m-1-6 — GST and withholding tax
*Keywords: GST, goods and services tax, 9%, residential property exempt, commercial property, taxable person, Condition 7.2 LSCS, purchaser pays GST, withholding tax, non-resident vendor, property trader, s 10(1)(a) Income Tax Act, s 45D Income Tax Act, 15% withheld, Condition 7.3*

**End point:**
- **GST** (currently 9%): only residential property is exempt. A sale or lease of non-residential property by a GST-registered (taxable) vendor attracts GST. Parties may negotiate, but under LSCS Condition 7.2 the purchaser pays any GST the vendor is accountable for.
- **Withholding tax:** where the vendor is not resident in Singapore for income tax and is taxed as a property trader on the gain (s 10(1)(a) ITA), the purchaser's solicitor must withhold tax and pay it to the Comptroller (s 45D ITA; LSCS Condition 7.3). Trader status is presumed unless the vendor says otherwise 2 weeks before completion. The vendor's solicitor gets a letter of confirmation; if he is a non-resident trader, 15% of the price is withheld.

*Source note: RE1 s10; RE3 s15–17; RE4 s34; LSCS 2020 Conditions 7.2, 7.3; IRAS: GST rate 9% from 1 Jan 2024, sale or lease of residential property an exempt supply ("Supplies exempt from GST"); withholding tax rate 15% on proceeds of sale of real property by a non-resident property trader ("Types of payment and withholding tax rates").*

### pcp-m-1-7 — What a title search shows
*Keywords: title search, INLIS, Singapore Land Authority, SLA, saleable interest, encumbrances, land tenure, freehold, leasehold, estate in perpetuity, state title, State Lease, title document number, SSCT, lot area, manner of holding, joint tenants, tenants in common, registered proprietor, citizenship, instrument of transfer, purchase price, gift, caution as to title, notices, orders of court, collective sale order, caveats, restrictive covenants, mortgages, type of development, Strata Landed Housing, share value, last contract date, SSD*

**End point:** A title search answers three questions:
- does the vendor have a saleable interest;
- what encumbrances must be cleared at completion;
- what restrictions or easements affect use.

It is done on INLIS or at the SLA, when the client has paid a booking or option fee, and updated just before completion. It shows:

| Item | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Tenure (fee simple, perpetuity, leasehold); State title | Nature of ownership; State covenants |
| Title document number; land or floor area | Identifies the property |
| Manner of holding (joint tenants or tenants in common) | Each owner's share; what happens on death |
| Registered proprietor (NRIC, address, citizenship) | Confirms the seller is the owner |
| Instrument of transfer | Price paid, or a gift (risk of undervalue: LSCS Condition 10.2) |
| Caution as to title | Qualified title needs common law investigation |
| Notices, court orders, collective sale orders | Extract and examine |
| Encumbrances (caveats, restrictive covenants, charges, mortgages) | To be discharged or examined |
| Type of development ("Strata – Landed Housing") | Restricted residential property: RPA approval needed |
| Share value | Maintenance share; collective sale |
| Last contract or option date | Whether the seller is liable for SSD |

A title search showing a mortgage does not mean bad title: arrange for it to be redeemed and discharged on or before completion (SQ p80 Q9, key C). A title search will show a caveat already lodged (SQ p79 Q6).

*Source note: RE2 s3–11; RE-CS1 s7–9; Cyril-RE §3.1–3.2; SQ p79 Q6, p80 Q9 (key p128: B, C).*

### pcp-m-1-8 — Share value and collective sales
*Keywords: share value, strata development, proportion of maintenance fees, management fund, sinking fund, voting, collective sale, en bloc, 80% share value, 90% share value, development less than 10 years, more than 10 years, distribution of sale proceeds, Strata Titles Board*

**End point:** Share value is each strata unit's proportionate share in the development. It fixes:
- the owner's share of maintenance and sinking fund charges;
- voting weight;
- the consent needed for a collective sale: at least 90% by share value and strata area for a development under 10 years old, 80% if 10 years or more;
- the distribution of collective sale proceeds.

*Source note: RE2 s9; Cyril-RE §3.4; LTSA s 84A(1)(a)–(b) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026): the 90% / 80% thresholds are by share value and by total lot area (excluding accessory lots), counted from the latest TOP (or CSC if no TOP).*

### pcp-m-1-9 — Lot Base and other searches
*Keywords: Lot Base System search, LBS, lot particulars, notice of acquisition, lot history, parent lot, amalgamation, subdivision, gift transfer affecting development, bankruptcy search, winding up search, judicial management search, updated searches on completion*

**End point:** A Lot Base System search shows lot particulars (including notices of acquisition) and lot history (how lots derive from parent lots). In one development, a gift transfer of a parent lot affected all 275 units. On concluding the contract and before completion, also update:
- bankruptcy searches against individuals;
- winding up and judicial management searches against companies;
- the title search.

*Source note: RE2 s10; RE3 s7; RE7 s12–14.*

### pcp-m-1-10 — Title deeds, solicitor's undertakings and documents of title
*Keywords: title deeds, solicitor's undertaking, return on demand without claim or lien, professional undertaking, professional misconduct, Condition 3 LSCS, documents of title one month before completion, deemed notice, suppressed title, Authorisation Form, electronic title deed*

**End point:** After the contract, title deeds go to the purchaser's solicitor for investigation of title, on the undertaking "to return the same to us on demand without any claim or lien thereon whatsoever". A solicitor must honour a professional undertaking to another solicitor, so breach may be professional misconduct. The vendor must give the title documents (or copies) for inspection at least 1 month before the scheduled completion date. The purchaser is treated as having notice of them whether or not he inspects (LSCS Condition 3.1). Where the SLA holds the title for a bank or CPF Board, the bank signs an "Authorisation Form – Suppressed Title" instead.

*Source note: RE2 s12; RE4 s31–32; LSCS 2020 Conditions 3.1–3.3.*

### pcp-m-1-11 — Investigation of title: good root, indefeasible and qualified title
*Keywords: investigation of title, good root of title, conveyance on sale, legal mortgage, bad root, lease, voluntary conveyance, will, power of appointment, Williams on Purchaser and Vendor, Torrens, indefeasible title, qualified title, caution as to title, Certificate of Title, leasehold covenants, access, Cheng Theresa v Oei Hong Leong*

**End point:** Investigation of title looks for a good root of title. That is an instrument of disposition that on its face, without extrinsic evidence, deals with the whole legal and equitable estate, describes the property identifiably, and casts no doubt on the title.
- **Good roots:** conveyance on sale, legal mortgage.
- **Bad roots:** a lease (of freehold), a voluntary conveyance, a will, an appointment under a power.

Under the Torrens system, an indefeasible (unqualified) title is the mirror of the title: investigate from the current CT or SSCT only. A qualified title (caution as to title) needs the earlier common law title investigated. For leasehold or perpetuity estates, check the terms and covenants. On whether access is part of good title, see *Cheng Theresa v Oei Hong Leong* [2006] 2 SLR(R) 637.

*Source note: RE2 s13–17; Cyril-RE §3.3.*

### pcp-m-1-12 — Legal requisitions
*Keywords: legal requisitions, LRs, nine government departments, Chu Yik Man v Rajagopal, BCA, IRAS, LTA, NEA, PUB, URA, road line plan, road reserve, landlocked, Management Corporation 10th question, s 47(1)(c) BMSMA, unsatisfactory reply, rescind, Soo Nam Thoong v Phang Song Hua, objective test, s 43 BMSMA charge for arrears, unpaid property tax, State land encroachment, 6 months before completion*

**End point:** Legal requisitions are questions to nine government departments and statutory boards (BCA, IRAS, LTA (cross-border rail, RTS, street works), NEA, PUB, URA) (*Chu Yik Man v Rajagopal*). For a strata unit, a 10th goes to the Management Corporation (s 47(1)(c) BMSMA). Replies reveal charges, fees, notices, and road widening or road lines. Non-compliance may give the authority a charge with power of sale (e.g. an MCST for maintenance arrears, s 43 BMSMA), and the authority pursues the current owner.

Subject to the contract's wording, the purchaser may rescind if a reply is unsatisfactory, judged objectively by a reasonable purchaser (*Soo Nam Thoong v Phang Song Hua* [2011] SGHC 159: an LTA reply was unsatisfactory and the standard proviso did not save the vendor). Define "unsatisfactory" clearly in the contract. Banks usually want LRs made no more than 6 months before completion. For a road line, ask for the architect's confirmation and a valuer's view, and check the property is not landlocked.

*Source note: RE2 s18–24; RE4 s23; RE-CS1 s28; Cyril-RE §3.4 (objective test).*

### pcp-m-1-13 — Granting and exercising an option to purchase
*Keywords: option to purchase, OTP, option fee 1%, option money, grant of option, exercise period 14 days, 2 weeks, Acceptance Copy, original OTP, sign acceptance copy, balance deposit 4%, 5% deposit, unqualified acceptance, cleared cheques, Vendor's solicitors CVY account, paying option money before legal advice*

**End point:** The vendor's solicitor drafts the OTP, the purchaser's solicitor reviews it, and the parties agree terms. The vendor signs and grants the OTP in exchange for the option fee (usually 1%). The purchaser usually has 2 weeks (14 days) to exercise. He exercises by signing the Acceptance Copy on the original OTP and delivering it with the balance deposit (usually 4%, making 5%) to the vendor's solicitors, often to their conveyancing account. Acceptance must be unqualified and cheques must clear. Once the option money is paid, the vendor need not amend any term, so advise before the fee is paid.

SQ p78 Q3: Mr Tan (purchaser) must sign the Acceptance Copy on the original OTP, not a photocopy (key B).

*Source note: RE3 s4–5; RE4 s14–15; RE-CS1 s23, s25; Cyril-RE §1.1; SQ p78 Q3 (key p128: B).*

### pcp-m-1-14 — Option not exercised
*Keywords: option lapses, not exercised, forfeit option fee, 1%, no breach of contract, vendor free to resell, no compensation, refund of option fee*

**End point:** If the purchaser lets the option lapse, there is no breach of contract. The purchaser loses only the option fee (1%), and the vendor may sell to someone else. Neither compensates the other. SQ p78 Q4: Mr Tan forfeits 1% of the price (key B).

*Source note: RE3 s4; RE4 s15; SQ p78 Q4 (key p128: B).*

### pcp-m-1-15 — OTP v sale and purchase agreement; vendor as trustee
*Keywords: sale and purchase agreement, S&P, SPA, both bound, deposit, breach of contract, vendor wants certainty, vendor trustee, constructive trust, fiduciary duty to consult purchaser, en bloc, EOGM vote, completion 8 to 10 weeks*

**End point:** Under an OTP, the purchaser has a choice until exercise. Under an S&P agreement, both sign and are bound at once: the purchaser pays a deposit, and either side that does not proceed is in breach and liable. A vendor who wants no chance of the buyer walking away uses an S&P. Completion is usually 8 to 10 weeks after the contract. Once the contract is concluded, the vendor holds the property as trustee for the purchaser and must consult him. For example, on an en bloc proposal before completion, the vendor votes as the purchaser wishes.

*Source note: RE3 s6–7; RE4 s16–17, s28; Cyril-RE (Sale and Purchase Agreement).*

### pcp-m-1-16 — Salient terms of the OTP or contract
*Keywords: salient terms, property address, sale price, vendor particulars, purchaser particulars, payment structure 1% 4% 95%, balance purchase price, stakeholding, subject to LSCS 2020, contract prevails over conditions, subject to tenancy, right of first refusal, inventory list, completion date 8 weeks to 3 months, redemption notice 2 or 3 months, legal requisitions, commission, mode of payment, compulsory acquisition rescind, vacant possession*

**End point:** Check:
- **Address** against the title search.
- **Price** per instructions.
- **Vendor's particulars** against the title search and NRICs; who exactly the purchasers are.
- **Payment:** 1% on grant, 4% (making 5%) on exercise, 95% on completion subject to apportionments.
- **Stakeholding** of the deposit.
- **LSCS 2020 incorporated:** the contract's own terms prevail over the 17 Conditions where they conflict.
- **Tenancy:** review rent, deposit, renewal, restrictions on assignment, and any tenant's right of first refusal. Provide for early termination and an inventory, or the purchaser can insist on removal of furniture.
- **Completion period:** 8 weeks to 3 months, for LRs, financing and the vendor's 2–3 month redemption notice.
- **Legal requisitions** with a clear "unsatisfactory" definition.
- **Commission and mode of payment:** the purchaser's solicitor must still check the mode of payment under the Conveyancing Rules.
- **Compulsory acquisition:** the purchaser may rescind.
- **Vacant possession or subject to tenancy.**

*Source note: RE-CS1 s22–30; RE4 s18–25; Cyril-RE §1.2.*

### pcp-m-1-17 — Stakeholding of the option exercise monies
*Keywords: stakeholder, stakeholding clause, deposit held by vendor's solicitors, CVY account, conveyancing account, Singapore Academy of Law, SAL Conveyancing Money Service, release deposit to vendor, purchaser consent, vendor insolvency, encumbrances, refund of deposit, non-stakeholding*

**End point:** The 4% exercise money is usually held by the vendor's solicitors (in their conveyancing account) or by SAL as stakeholders until completion, and then released to the vendor through the completion account. Purchaser: stakeholding makes a refund easier if the vendor defaults or becomes insolvent, and helps ensure the encumbrances can be discharged. Vendor: he would rather have it directly. If the purchaser agrees to non-stakeholding, his solicitor should check the encumbrances, or that the balance price comfortably covers them. Whether to stakehold is a commercial term.

A stakeholder must not release the money early just because the vendor needs it. It may release only as the contract allows or with the purchaser's consent (SQ p80 Q10, key A).

*Source note: RE-CS1 s24; RE4 s18; Cyril-RE §1.4; SQ p80 Q10 (key p128: A).*

### pcp-m-1-19 — "As is where is", entire agreement and non-merger clauses
*Keywords: as is where is, full notice of state and condition, access, repair, drainage, area, entire agreement clause, supersedes oral representations, erroneous verbal statements, non-merger clause, survives completion, BCA fine discovered after completion, vendor liable*

**End point:**
- **"As is where is":** the purchaser buys with full notice of the state and condition, access, repair, drainage, area and all other respects.
- **Entire agreement clause:** the contract supersedes all earlier oral or written representations, so the vendor is protected against erroneous verbal statements.
- **Non-merger clause:** the terms survive completion and registration. It is unfavourable to the vendor. For example, the BCA's LR reply says no fines, but after completion the BCA reveals a $50,000 fine. With the clause the vendor is liable; without it he is not, as notice came after completion.

*Source note: RE3 s9; RE4 s24–27.*

### pcp-m-1-20 — Caveats: when and why to lodge
*Keywords: caveat, lodge caveat, after exercise of option, conclusion of contract, Singapore Land Authority, Land Titles Act, Registry of Deeds, notice to third parties, notified of subsequent dealings, prohibits registration of conflicting instrument, priority, bank caveat, mortgagee caveat, uncompleted property*

**End point:** The purchaser's solicitor should lodge a caveat as soon as the contract is concluded (option exercised), with the SLA for Land Titles Act land (or at the Registry of Deeds for common law land, where it is notice only). Any person claiming an interest in land may lodge a caveat in the approved form stating the interest, its grounds and the prohibition of dealings claimed; a purchaser must state the price and the contract or option date (LTA s 115(1)). A caveat:
- gives third parties notice of the purchaser's interest;
- ensures the purchaser is told of later dealings;
- under the LTA, stops registration of a conflicting instrument;
- gives priority if lodged early against competing claims.

A caveat lapses 30 days after service of a notice under s 120 (unless the court extends), or after 5 years unless extended (s 121(1)). A caveat does not stop the purchaser completing (SQ p79 Q6: I and III correct, key B). The mortgagee bank's solicitors also lodge a caveat.

*Source note: RE3 s18; RE5 s6; Cyril-RE §1.3.2; SQ p79 Q6 (key p128: B); LTA ss 115(1), 121(1) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026).*

### pcp-m-1-21 — When stamp duty is payable
*Keywords: stamp duty deadline, 14 days, date of exercise of option, date of sale and purchase agreement, date of transfer, signed overseas 30 days, BSD, ABSD, SSD, material date, Commissioner of Stamp Duties, IRAS*

**End point:** BSD and ABSD are payable within 14 days after the exercise of the OTP. If there is no OTP, within 14 days of the S&P agreement; if neither, within 14 days of the transfer. If the document is signed overseas, ABSD is due within 30 days of receipt in Singapore. SSD is payable within 14 days of the date of the seller's contract (acceptance of the OTP, or the S&P date).

### pcp-m-1-22 — Buyer's Stamp Duty: rates and worked example
*Keywords: Buyer's Stamp Duty, BSD rates, 15 February 2023, residential 1% 2% 3% 4% 5% 6%, non-residential up to 5%, first $180,000, next $180,000, next $640,000, next $500,000, next $1,500,000, higher of price or market value, rounded down, gift, worked example*

**End point:** BSD is charged on the higher of the price and the market value, rounded down to the nearest dollar. It also applies to gifts and to some trust and estate distributions.

| Price or market value (from 15 Feb 2023) | Residential | Non-residential |
|---|---|---|
| First $180,000 | 1% | 1% |
| Next $180,000 | 2% | 2% |
| Next $640,000 | 3% | 3% |
| Next $500,000 | 4% | 4% |
| Next $1,500,000 | 5% | 5% |
| Remainder (above $3m) | 6% | 5% |

**Worked example (residential, $2,500,000):** 1,800 + 3,600 + 19,200 + 20,000 + 5% × 1,000,000 (50,000) = **$94,600**.

**Shortcuts (residential):** $1m–$1.5m: BSD = 4% × price − $15,400 (e.g. $1,200,000 → $32,600). $1.5m–$3m: BSD = 5% × price − $30,400 (e.g. $2,500,000 → $94,600). Above $3m: 6% × price − $60,400.

*Source note: RE3 s13 (rates "as of 15 February 2023"; table image); Cyril-RE §2.1 (rates and example); IRAS BSD page, checked 10 Nov 2026: rates from 15 Feb 2023 still current (top marginal rate 6% residential, 5% non-residential). Shortcut is the drafter's algebra from the table.*

### pcp-m-1-23 — Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty: rates

| Buyer (rates from 27 Apr 2023) | 1st property | 2nd | 3rd and after |
|---|---|---|---|
| Singapore citizen | 0% | 20% | 30% |
| Singapore PR | 5% | 30% | 35% |
| Foreigner | 60% | 60% | 60% |
| Entity (e.g. company) | 65% | | |
| Trustee | 65% | | |
| Housing developer | 35% (+5% non-remittable) | | |

For joint buyers with different profiles, the highest applicable rate applies to the whole price. A commercial unit does not count as a residential property.

### pcp-m-1-25 — ABSD remissions and anti-avoidance
*Keywords: remission, Free Trade Agreement, FTA, United States nationals, Switzerland, Liechtenstein, Iceland, Norway, nationals and permanent residents, same treatment as Singapore citizens, ABSD (Trust) remission, 6 months, identifiable beneficiary, 99-1 scheme, backdating contracts, Ochroid Trading v Chua Siok Lui, void for illegality*

**End point:**
- **FTA remission:** nationals and PRs of Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland, and nationals of the USA, get the same stamp duty treatment as Singapore citizens (on application for remission).
- **ABSD (Trust) of 65%:** may be remitted down to the rate of the beneficial owner with the highest profile, where the property is held on trust for identifiable individuals and the application is made within 6 months of the instrument.
- **Anti-avoidance:** contracts backdated to avoid ABSD, BSD or ACD are void for illegality (*Ochroid Trading v Chua Siok Lui* [2018] SGCA 5). The CS1 slide on the "99-1" scheme shows a news report (25 Feb 2025) of the first prosecution: a mother and son jailed 2 weeks for lying to IRAS over a "99-to-1" property purchase.

*Source note: RE-CS1 s18, s20–21; RE1 s9; Cyril-RE §2.2 (Trust remission); Stamp Duties (Free Trade Agreements) (Remission) Rules (RE-Statutes copy); IRAS "Common Stamp Duty Remissions and Reliefs for Property" (FTA remission listed) and ABSD page (ABSD (Trust) refund: apply within 6 months after execution; developers' trusts 40%, 5% non-remittable); RE-CS1 s20 (slide image read).*

### pcp-m-1-26 — Seller's Stamp Duty
*Keywords: Seller's Stamp Duty, SSD, holding period, 4 July 2025, 16%, 12%, 8%, 4%, 4 years, 11 March 2017, 3 years, industrial SSD 15% 10% 5%, exemptions, housing developer, HDB, compulsory acquisition, bankruptcy sale, RPA forced sale, Condition 7.4 deduct SSD from price, last contract date*

**End point:** SSD is payable by a seller who disposes of residential (or industrial) property within the holding period. It is charged on the higher of price and market value, and the holding period runs from the seller's acquisition to his disposal.

| Residential, acquired | ≤1 yr | >1–2 yrs | >2–3 yrs | >3–4 yrs | longer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| On or after 4 Jul 2025 | 16% | 12% | 8% | 4% | nil |
| 11 Mar 2017 – 3 Jul 2025 | 12% | 8% | 4% | nil | nil |

| Industrial (from 11 Mar 2017) | ≤1 yr 15% | >1–2 yrs 10% | >2–3 yrs 5% | longer nil |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Exempt include licensed developers selling what they built, HDB/JTC, compulsory acquisition, bankruptcy or winding-up sales, and foreigners forced to sell under the RPA. If the vendor does not pay, the purchaser may deduct the SSD from the price and pay it, and that counts as payment to the vendor (LSCS Condition 7.4.2). The vendor must show proof 5 clear business days before completion (7.4.1).

*Source note: RE-CS1 s18 (3 Jul 2025 announcement); IRAS SSD page, checked 10 Nov 2026 (rates by acquisition date as tabled; also 16/12/8/4% over 4 years for acquisitions 14 Jan 2011–10 Mar 2017); Cyril-RE §2.3 (exemptions); LSCS 2020 Condition 7.4. Conflict: Cyril-RE gives only the 2017 rates (3-year period); IRAS's current table is followed.*

### pcp-m-1-27 — Mixed-use property
*Keywords: mixed-use property, shophouse, HDB shop with living quarters, apportion residential and non-residential, BSD on each part, ABSD residential part only, SSD residential part, industrial part*

**End point:** For a property with residential and non-residential parts, apportion the value:
- BSD: residential rates on the residential part, non-residential rates on the rest;
- ABSD: residential part only;
- SSD: the residential rates on the residential part and industrial rates on any industrial part, by holding period.

*Source note: Cyril-RE §2.4; RE1 s11; IRAS "Definition of residential property": for a unit or part of a building, each part follows its permitted use (e.g. a shophouse or HDB shop with living quarters); for land or a whole building the residential share follows zoning and GFA (e.g. "Commercial & Residential": 60% of GFA deemed residential), or a valuer may apportion. SSD applies to the residential component on the same definitions.*

### pcp-m-1-28 — Late or wrong stamping
*Keywords: late stamping penalty, not exceeding 3 months, $10 or amount of duty, exceeding 3 months, $25 or 4 times duty, deficient duty, 4 times penalty, unstamped document inadmissible, cannot register at SLA, mandatory declarations, seller and buyer declaration*

**End point:**

| Delay | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Up to 3 months late | the greater of $10 and the duty payable |
| More than 3 months late | the greater of $25 and 4 times the duty payable |

Deficient or unpaid duty (including SSD and ABSD) may attract a penalty of up to 4 times the deficiency. Seller and buyer declarations to IRAS are mandatory. An unstamped document cannot be registered with the SLA or relied on.

### pcp-m-1-29 — Who is a foreign person
*Keywords: Residential Property Act, RPA, foreign person, not a Singapore citizen, permanent resident is a foreign person, foreign company, foreign society, foreign LLP, Singapore company, approved purchaser, citizenship on title search*

**End point:** Under the RPA, a "foreign person" is anyone who is not (a) a Singapore citizen, (b) a Singapore company, (c) a Singapore LLP or (d) a Singapore society. So a Singapore PR is a foreign person, as are foreign companies, societies and LLPs. Check the client's citizenship and the property type before any residential deal.

*Source note: RE1 s4, s6; Cyril-RE §1.6; RPA s 2 ("foreign person", "approved purchaser") (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026).*

### pcp-m-1-30 — Restricted residential property and LDAU approval
*Keywords: restricted residential property, Land Dealings Approval Unit, LDAU, approval, vacant residential land, landed property, detached, semi-detached, terrace, linked house, townhouse, strata landed housing, shophouse residential, association premises, workers' dormitory, place of worship, condominium unit allowed, flats allowed, executive condominium, lease 7 years or less, industrial and commercial no restriction, s 3 RPA, void, Sentosa Cove*

**End point:** A foreign person must not buy or acquire residential property, and a transfer to him is void, except by mortgage, charge or reconveyance, or as the Act allows (s 3(1)–(2)). In practice he needs LDAU approval for restricted residential property:
- vacant residential land;
- landed houses (detached, semi-detached, terrace, linked, townhouse);
- landed units in strata developments not approved as condominiums ("Strata – Landed Housing");
- shophouses used for residential purposes;
- association premises, workers' dormitories and boarding houses;
- places of worship.

Under s 4(1) the Act does not apply to a flat in a residential development that is not a landed dwelling house, a unit in an approved "condominium" development, or an executive condominium unit, but a foreign person needs the Minister's approval to buy all the flats or units in such a development (s 4(2)). Land or premises zoned or used solely for industrial or commercial purposes, and registered hotels, are not "residential property" (s 2). SLA considers approval case by case: an individual applicant should have been a PR for at least 5 years and make an exceptional economic contribution, and an approved individual may not dispose of the property within 5 years of completion.

SQ p77 Q2: a foreigner may buy the condominium unit (key C).

> **RPA s 3(1)**
> - (a) a person must not transfer any residential property or interest to a foreign person (for value, by gift or otherwise);
> - (b) a person must not create a trust for sale of residential property in favour of a foreign person;
> - (c) a foreign person must not purchase or acquire residential property except by mortgage, charge or reconveyance.
> - s 3(2): any such transfer, trust or purchase is void.

*Source note: RPA ss 2–4 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026); RE2 s9 (Strata – Landed Housing); RE1 s6; SLA "Foreign ownership of property" and LDAU e-services FAQs (sla.gov.sg): vacant residential land, landed houses, residential shophouses, association premises and places of worship listed as restricted; SQ p77 Q2 (key p128: C). Conflict: Cyril-RE §1.6 also lists workers' dormitories and leases of 7 years or less; these were not found in the RPA or SLA pages checked and are left out.*

### pcp-m-1-31 — Foreigners and inheritance, joint tenancy, loss of PR
*Keywords: RPA s 3(3), no inheritance by foreign person, s 3(4) sell within 5 years, citizen or approved purchaser, pay proceeds, Controller of Residential Property, extension s 3(12), s 3(13) surviving joint tenant, PR status cancelled sell within 2 years*

**End point:** Residential property of a person dying on or after 11 Sep 1973 does not pass by will or intestacy to a foreign person (s 3(3)). The personal representatives must sell it to a citizen or approved purchaser within 5 years of death (or any extension), and pay the net proceeds to the foreign beneficiary (s 3(4)). If they do not, the Controller may be directed to attach and sell (ss 3(5)–(6)). The Act does not apply to a foreign surviving joint tenant (s 3(13)).

*Source note: RPA s 3(3)–(13) (RE-Statutes SSO copy at 3 Dec 2023; s 3 also read on SSO 10 Nov 2026 with ss 2 and 4). Conflict: Cyril-RE §1.6 says a PR who loses PR status must sell within 2 years; SLA's FAQs give a 2-year disposal period only for converted foreign entities, so the PR rule is left out.*

### pcp-m-1-32 — Vendor's considerations in setting the price
*Keywords: vendor considerations, redemption amount, lock-in period, prepayment penalty, CPF refund, principal withdrawn, accrued interest, seller's stamp duty, legal fees, agent's commission, negative sale*

**End point:** From the sale proceeds the vendor must meet:
- the bank's redemption amount, including any penalty for redeeming within the lock-in period;
- the CPF refund: the capital withdrawn for the purchase plus the accrued interest it would have earned;
- SSD, if sold within the holding period;
- legal fees and the agent's commission.

*Source note: RE4 s4; Cyril-RE (Slide 4).*

### pcp-m-1-33 — Shortfall: bank v CPF priority, worked example
*Keywords: shortfall, insufficient sale proceeds, first charge, bank first, 1 September 2002, CPF second, penalty charge not covered, loss of CPF, vendor tops up, CPF first charge, reverse charge, worked example $1,000,000 sale*

**End point:** For properties bought on or after 1 Sep 2002, the bank has first claim, but only for the outstanding housing loan and interest, not penalty charges. CPF is refunded from what remains, so the vendor can lose part or all of his CPF.

| Sale $1,000,000; bank loan $600,000 + $6,000 penalty; CPF refund due $500,000 | Bank has first charge | CPF has first charge |
|---|---|---|
| Paid first | Bank $600,000 | CPF $500,000 in full |
| Paid second | CPF $400,000 (vendor loses $100,000 of CPF) | Bank $500,000 |
| Vendor must find | $6,000 penalty (+ SSD, fees) | $106,000 shortfall (+ SSD, fees) |

If the vendor cannot fund a shortfall, he may apply to the bank and CPF Board to reverse the charges.

*Source note: RE4 s5–10.*

### pcp-m-1-34 — CPF Board's requirements before completion
*Keywords: CPF Board requirements, valuation report, sale price v valuation, bank's initial and final redemption statements, penalty charges, vendor not related to purchaser, relative declaration*

**End point:** Before completion, CPF Board requires:
- a valuation report comparing the sale price with the valuation;
- the bank's initial and final redemption statements (to see any penalty charges);
- the vendor's confirmation that he is not a relative of the purchaser.

*Source note: RE4 s7.*

### pcp-m-1-35 — The solicitors involved and their duties
*Keywords: capacities, number of solicitors, vendor's solicitor, purchaser's solicitor, bank's solicitor, CPF Board's solicitor, notice of redemption, Digital Total Discharge of Mortgage, TDM, Application to Notify Total Discharge of CPF Charge, ANDC, SSD declaration form, letter of confirmation property trader, letter of authority, redemption statement, completion account 7 business days, Notice of Transfer IRAS, notify tenant, management corporation notice, cashier's order to bank by 10am next working day, CPF next working day, access 3 working days*

**End point:** One firm may act in up to five capacities: for the vendor (towards the purchaser's solicitor, the bank's and the CPF Board's solicitors) and for the bank and CPF Board in the discharge. The vendor's solicitor:
1. serves redemption notices on the bank and CPF Board;
2. gets the SSD cheque (paid within 14 days), the property tax and maintenance bills, and the tenancy agreement with its stamp certificate;
3. searches title and bankruptcy;
4. drafts the digital TDM and ANDC for the bank's and CPF's solicitors;
5. takes the title deed on undertaking and passes it to the purchaser's solicitor on undertaking;
6. approves the purchaser's draft transfer;
7. prepares the SSD declaration, the letter of confirmation (non-resident trader: 15% withheld) and the letter of authority for third-party payments;
8. gets the redemption statements;
9. sends the completion account and mode of payment at least 7 business days before completion;
10. attends execution and completion;
11. afterwards delivers the proceeds, e-files the Notice of Transfer with IRAS, notifies the tenant, and reports the TDM and ANDC registration.

The bank's solicitor delivers the cashier's order to the bank by 10am the next working day. The CPF Board's solicitor delivers by close of the next working day. Both grant electronic access to the TDM or ANDC within 3 working days.

*Source note: RE4 s29–41; RE7 s19–21.*

### pcp-m-1-36 — Buying from a developer: progress payments and defects liability (non-examinable)
*Keywords: building under construction, BUC, progressive payments, Housing Developers Rules Form 5, booking fee 5%, S&P within 8 weeks, 15%, foundation 10%, TOP 25%, CSC 15%, project account, late payment interest, repudiation 21 days notice, forfeit 20%, defects liability period 12 months, rectify within one month, 14 days further notice, non-examinable*

**End point:** (Non-examinable.) Developer sales use the prescribed Form 5 S&P. The price is paid in stages into a project account: 5% booking fee, 15% on signing the S&P (within 8 weeks of the option), then construction stages, 25% on TOP and 15% on CSC and title. Late payment attracts interest. Persistent default lets the developer annul after 21 days' notice and forfeit 20%. The defects liability period is 12 months from vacant possession (or the 15th day after TOP or CSC notice, if earlier). The developer has 1 month to rectify after notice, then a further 14 days, after which the buyer may rectify and recover the cost.

*Source note: RE-LP Schedule 1 A(vi)–(vii) (non-examinable); Cyril-RE §1.6.1–1.6.3 (Form 5 clauses 5, 7, 17).*

### pcp-m-1-37 — Financing and parties to a mortgage
*Keywords: financing purchase, purchase price, CPF money, own funds, bank loan, mortgagor, borrower, mortgagee, direct mortgage, indirect mortgage, MAS Notice 632, paragraph 25, residential property loans, third-party mortgage*

**End point:** The price is funded by the purchaser's cash, CPF money (secured by CPF Board's charge) and a bank loan (secured by a mortgage). The mortgagor gives the security, the borrower takes the loan, and the mortgagee lends. In a direct mortgage the mortgagor and borrower are the same; in an indirect mortgage they differ. For residential property loans, a bank must not grant a purchase facility (and certain other facilities secured by residential property) to a borrower who is not a mortgagor of the property, alone or jointly (MAS Notice 632 para 25), save the para 26 exceptions (facility not secured by the property; non-individual borrower for some facilities).

*Source note: RE5 s3–5; MAS Notice 632 paras 25–26 (mas.gov.sg; as amended by MAS Notice 632 (Amendment) 2025, 21 Aug 2025).*

### pcp-m-1-38 — Mortgage process and security documents
*Keywords: mortgage process, loan application, proof of income, credit assessment, letter of offer, acceptance, bank's solicitors, caveat, searches, report on title, disbursement, registration, letter of offer terms, interest rate, repayment, prepayment penalty, lock-in, standard terms and conditions, mortgage all monies, memorandum of mortgage, supplemental deed, deed of assignment of rental proceeds, deed of assignment uncompleted property, mortgage in escrow*

**End point:** Process:
1. apply for the loan, with proof of income;
2. credit assessment;
3. letter of offer;
4. acceptance, and the bank instructs solicitors;
5. the solicitors lodge a caveat, search, prepare documents and check conditions;
6. documents are signed;
7. report on title;
8. disbursement and registration.

Documents:
- **Letter of offer:** loan type, rates, repayment, prepayment penalties, conditions precedent, collateral; the bank's printed standard terms apply unless the letter of offer negates them.
- **Mortgage:** SLA format, usually "open" or "all monies".
- **Memorandum of mortgage:** further covenants, incorporated by reference and registered (e.g. MM No. I/94778S).
- **Others:** supplemental deed; assignment of rental proceeds for investment property; for an uncompleted property, a deed of assignment and a mortgage in escrow.

The bank's solicitor must read the letter of offer and terms and use the correct form of mortgage. All of this must happen within the completion period (6–8 weeks).

*Source note: RE5 s6–12, s21; Cyril-RE §1.7.1–1.7.2.*

### pcp-m-1-39 — Registered mortgages; equity of redemption; company borrowers
*Keywords: Land Titles Act s 68(3), mortgage does not transfer title, security only, s 77 discharge, equity of redemption, common law mortgage, Registration of Deeds Act, s 53 CLPA, company borrower, constitution, directors' resolution, statement of charge, ACRA, s 131 Companies Act, 30 days, void against liquidator and creditors*

**End point:** A mortgage under the LTA does not transfer the land; it takes effect as security (s 68(3) LTA). The mortgagor may obtain a discharge once he has met his obligations, i.e. he has an equity of redemption (s 77 LTA). Compare common law mortgages under the Registration of Deeds Act and s 53 CLPA. If the borrower is a company:
- check its constitution;
- obtain directors' resolutions;
- the bank files a statement of the charge with ACRA within 30 days of creation, or the charge is void against the liquidator and creditors (s 131 Companies Act).

*Source note: RE5 s13–14; Cyril-RE §1.7.3; LTA ss 68(3), 77(1), (3) and Companies Act s 131(1) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026) (s 77(1) is subject to s 22 CLPA).*

### pcp-m-1-40 — Using CPF monies for a purchase
*Keywords: CPF monies, residential property only, not commercial, letter of authorisation declaration and consent, not a bankrupt declaration, withdrawal towards purchase price, stamp duty reimbursement, monthly instalments, valuation limit, withdrawal limit, apply 6 to 8 weeks before completion, letter of approval, CPF Board's solicitor conveyancing account*

**End point:** CPF money may be used only for residential property, not commercial property. The member signs a letter of authorisation, declaration (including that he is not bankrupt) and consent, authorising his solicitor to apply to withdraw CPF towards the price, stamp duty reimbursement and monthly loan instalments. Use is capped by the valuation limit and withdrawal limit. Apply online about 6–8 weeks before completion. CPF Board issues a letter of approval, and at completion the money comes from the CPF Board's solicitor's conveyancing account by cashier's order.

*Source note: RE5 s15; RE1 s8; Cyril-RE §1.7.4.*

### pcp-m-1-41 — CPF Board's statutory charge and priorities
*Keywords: CPF statutory charge, s 21B CPF Act, Application to Notify Charge, Letter of Confirmation of Priority Arrangements, Memorandum IA/991901M, Instrument of Postponement, mortgage and CPF charge co-exist, priority of sale proceeds, mortgagee's first outstandings, CPF first outstandings, CPF second outstandings, refinancing, CPF consent, new letter of confirmation*

**End point:** Using CPF creates a statutory charge in favour of CPF Board (s 21B CPF Act), notified to the SLA by an Application to Notify Charge. Other documents are the Letter of Confirmation of Priority Arrangements, Memorandum IA/991901M, and an Instrument of Postponement where needed. A bank mortgage and the CPF charge co-exist (SQ p79 Q7, key C). CPF Board's guidance (ask.gov.sg, CPF) for properties bought or refinanced on or after 1 Sep 2002: sale proceeds go first to the outstanding housing loan, then the required CPF refund, then other sale expenses; a shortfall is shared as agreed between the owner, the bank and the Board. Where the proceeds of sale (by the bank, by CPF Board or otherwise, or compulsory-acquisition compensation) cannot pay both in full, Memorandum IA/991901M cl 8.1 fixes the order, regardless of which mortgage was created or registered first. First the costs and expenses incurred by the Board and/or the bank directly in connection with the sale are deducted. Then:
1. **Firstly**, the bank's First Outstandings: loan principal and interest unpaid as at the Date of Default (cl 9.1(n));
2. **Secondly**, the Board's First Outstandings: CPF used for stamp duty, valuation/survey fees, charges and legal costs of the purchase and the mortgages, plus CPF principal withdrawn up to 100% of the Ascribed Value (cl 9.1(b));
3. **Thirdly**, shared pro rata between the Board's Second Outstandings (CPF withdrawn beyond the First Outstandings, plus all accrued interest under the Board's mortgage; cl 9.1(c)) and the bank's balance of loan interest;
4. **Fourthly**, shared pro rata between the Board's and the bank's other costs, expenses and moneys under their mortgages;
5. **Fifthly**, all other moneys owing to the bank, e.g. unpaid principal, interest and fees on other facilities.

Clause 8 is subject to the CPF Act and its regulations and any other written law (cl 8.2).

On refinancing, the old mortgage is discharged and a new one registered. CPF Board consents and issues a new letter of confirmation of priority.

*Source note: RE5 s16–20; Cyril-RE §1.7.4 (s 21B); SQ p79 Q7 (key p128: C). CPF Board guidance on sale proceeds (ask.gov.sg/cpf, 2026). Five-step order: Memorandum of Mortgage IA/991901M cl 8.1–8.2 and definitions cl 9.1(b), (c), (n), checked against the copy published by CPF Board (cpf.gov.sg, `Housing_RPS_Memorandum.pdf`, 10 Nov 2026); it agrees with RE5 s18.*

### pcp-m-1-42 — Completion accounts: definition and types
*Keywords: completion account, Frans Furniture v Good Property Land Development, account rendered by vendor's solicitors, amount payable on completion, Type 1 vendor's solicitors to purchaser's solicitors, Type 2 vendor's solicitors to vendor, Type 3 purchaser's solicitors to purchaser*

**End point:** A completion account is "an account rendered by Vendor's solicitors to Purchaser's solicitors to inform Purchaser of the amount payable on completion" (*Frans Furniture v Good Property Land Development*). Three types:
1. vendor's solicitors → purchaser's solicitors: the amount the purchaser pays the vendor;
2. vendor's solicitors → vendor: the vendor's net sale proceeds;
3. purchaser's solicitors → purchaser: the cash the purchaser must find.

*Source note: RE6 s3–4.*

### pcp-m-1-43 — Apportioning outgoings and rent
*Keywords: apportionment, property tax, maintenance charges, sinking fund, rent, rental deposit, Condition 6, Condition 7.1, vendor liable down to and including completion date, purchaser after, prepaid, refund to vendor, day count, days in month, owner-occupier rate, non-owner-occupied rate*

**End point:** The vendor bears outgoings (property tax, maintenance and sinking fund) and takes the rent down to and including the scheduled completion date; the purchaser does so after (LSCS Conditions 6.1–6.2, 7.1). Usually the vendor has prepaid, so:
- outgoings prepaid beyond completion are **added** (refunded to the vendor);
- rent received beyond completion and the rental deposit are **deducted** (credited to the purchaser).

Formula: monthly amount ÷ days in that month × days after completion in that month, plus the monthly amount × whole months after.

Example (completion 18 Aug, so 13 days 19–31 Aug belong to the purchaser):
- Rent $2,000 paid to 31 Aug: 2,000 ÷ 31 × 13 = **$838.71** (deduct).
- Property tax $200 a month paid to 31 Dec: 200 ÷ 31 × 13 + 200 × 4 = **$883.87** (add).
- Maintenance $150 a month paid to 30 Sep: 150 ÷ 31 × 13 + 150 × 1 = **$212.90** (add).

*Source note: RE6 s5–10; LSCS 2020 Conditions 6.1–6.2, 7.1.1. Conflict: Cyril-RE §4.1 uses completion on 15 Aug with the same 13-day figures and adds "200 × 1 month" for maintenance; RE6 (completion 18 Aug, $150 × 1) is followed.*

### pcp-m-1-48 — Legal completion: meaning, attendees, venue
*Keywords: legal completion, Kilner v France, complete conveyance and final settlement, who attends, vendor's solicitors, discharging CPF chargee's solicitors, discharging mortgagee's solicitors, purchaser's solicitors, charging CPF solicitors, purchaser's mortgagee's solicitors, where to complete, CPF chargee's office, mortgagee's office, vendor's solicitors' office, developer's solicitors title not issued*

**End point:** Legal completion is "the complete conveyance and final settlement of business" (*Kilner v France* [1946] 2 All ER 83). Where the property is charged to CPF and mortgaged, and the buyer uses CPF and a loan, up to six solicitors attend: the vendor's, the discharging CPF chargee's, the discharging mortgagee's, the purchaser's, the charging CPF chargee's, and the purchaser's mortgagee's. Venue, in priority:
1. the discharging CPF chargee's solicitors' office;
2. else the discharging mortgagee's solicitors' office;
3. else the vendor's solicitors' office.

If the title has not issued, completion is at the developer's solicitors.

*Source note: RE7 s3–6; RE-CS1 s27.*

### pcp-m-1-49 — Completion steps and post-completion
*Keywords: completion process, preparations, release stakeholder monies, letters to management corporation and tenant, cashier's orders, exchange discharge documents, title deed, transfer, Application to Notify CPF Charge, mortgage, registration at SLA, stamp the mortgage, Notice of Transfer IRAS, owner-occupier tax status, Completion Report, 10am next working day, 3 working days access*

**End point:**
- **Before:** the vendor signs and gives evidence of tax and maintenance paid, the tenancy agreement and keys. The vendor's solicitors arrange release of stakeholder money and prepare letters to the MC, tenant and IRAS. The purchaser pays the cash difference into his solicitors' CVY account. Every side updates its searches.
- **At completion:** the purchaser's solicitors collect cashier's orders from the charging CPF and mortgagee's solicitors and hand them to the vendor's solicitors. These pay CPF Board and the bank in exchange for the discharge documents. The purchaser's solicitors check everything and hand the originals (title deed, discharge of CPF charge, discharge of mortgage, transfer, new CPF charge and mortgage) to the charging CPF Board's solicitors.
- **After:** the charging CPF solicitors stamp the mortgage and register everything at the SLA. The vendor's solicitors e-file the Notice of Transfer with IRAS and write to the tenant. The purchaser's solicitors countersign the MC letter and advise applying for owner-occupier tax rates. The discharging bank's solicitors deliver the cashier's order by 10am the next working day, CPF's by the next working day. Access to the electronic instruments is given within 3 working days.

*Source note: RE7 s7–25; Cyril-RE §1.8.*

### pcp-m-1-50 — Late completion interest (Conditions 9.1–9.3), worked
*Keywords: late completion, late completion interest, liquidated damages, Condition 9.1 purchaser default, Condition 9.2 vendor default, 8% per annum, day after scheduled completion date to completion date inclusive, purchase price less deposit, vendor pays on full price, Condition 9.3 notional rent, Condition 6.4 vendor elects rents and profits, worked example*

**End point:** If completion is late solely through one party's default, that party pays interest as liquidated damages at 8% a year, from the day after the scheduled completion date up to and including the actual completion date.
- **Purchaser in default (9.1):** interest on the price less the deposit and sums paid on account. The vendor may instead keep the rents and profits and account for the outgoings (6.4).
- **Vendor in default (9.2):** interest on the purchase price. It is reduced by a notional rent if the purchaser already has vacant possession rent-free (9.3).

| Worked: price $1,000,000; 5% deposit paid; 3 days late | Interest |
|---|---|
| Purchaser's default | $950,000 × 8% × 3/365 = **$624.66** |
| Vendor's default | $1,000,000 × 8% × 3/365 = **$657.53** |

*Source note: LSCS 2020 Conditions 6.4, 9.1–9.3 (RE-Statutes copy); RE3 s11. Conflict: Cyril-RE §1.5.2 deducts the deposit in the vendor-default example; Condition 9.2 says interest is on "the purchase price" (no deduction), which is followed.*

### pcp-m-1-51 — When no late completion interest is payable (Conditions 9.4–9.7)
*Keywords: no interest payable, both parties at fault, cause other than default, Condition 9.4, death, loss of mental capacity, postpone up to 3 months, grant of probate, deputy, Condition 9.5, mode of payment 7 clear business days, Condition 9.6, EPI system down more than 4 consecutive hours, Condition 9.7*

**End point:** No interest is payable where:
- the delay is due to neither party or to both (9.4);
- a party dies or loses mental capacity before the scheduled date: completion may be put off for a reasonable time, not more than 3 months, to obtain a grant or deputyship, with no interest for that period (9.5);
- the purchaser is late because the vendor did not give the mode of payment at least 7 clear business days before the scheduled date, for up to the number of days' shortfall (9.6);
- the EPI system on STARS is down for more than 4 consecutive hours on a day that countersigning is needed, for that day (9.7).

*Source note: LSCS 2020 Conditions 9.4–9.7. Conflict: Cyril-RE §1.5.3 gives "5 clear business days" for the mode of payment; Condition 9.6 says 7, which is followed.*

### pcp-m-1-52 — Notice to complete (Condition 15)
*Keywords: notice to complete, Condition 15, time not of the essence, either party may serve, after scheduled completion date, vendor wait 5 clear business days after mode of payment, ready able and willing, 21 days, time of the essence, forfeit deposit, resell within one year, liquidated damages for loss on resale, purchaser rescind refund of deposit, extend notice, failure to complete does not end contract*

**End point:** Unless the contract makes time of the essence for the completion date, failing to complete on that date does not end the contract. Either party may then serve a notice to complete at any time after the scheduled date (15.3–15.4). The vendor must first wait 5 clear business days after delivering the mode of payment (15.5). The server must be ready, able and willing, or prevented only by the other's default (15.6). The parties must then complete within 21 days of service, excluding the day of service, and time becomes of the essence (15.7).
- **Purchaser fails:** he returns the title deeds and cancels register entries. The vendor may forfeit the deposit and resell. On a resale within 1 year at a loss, the purchaser pays the loss and costs as liquidated damages, credit given for the deposit; the vendor keeps any surplus (15.9–15.10).
- **Vendor fails:** the purchaser may enforce his rights or demand the deposit back, with damages. If he takes the refund, he gives up specific performance (15.11–15.12).

The notice may be extended by agreement (15.13). SQ p79 Q8: I and II are correct; late completion does not automatically end the contract (key B).

*Source note: LSCS 2020 Condition 15; RE3 s11; SQ p79 Q8 (key p128: B).*

## 2.I Applicability of Muslim law and AMLA

- **What is AMLA? What institutions does it set up? Is it a code?** MUIS, Syariah Court, ROMM; not a code → [2-1](#pcp-m-2-1)
- **Who is a Muslim? Which laws do not apply to Muslims?** WC Parts 2–6 (married under Muslim law), ISA, IFPA → [2-2](#pcp-m-2-2)
- **Which civil laws still apply to Muslims?** WC maintenance and PPO, GIA, adoption, probate, Wills Act formalities, CPF and insurance nominations → [2-3](#pcp-m-2-3)

### pcp-m-2-1 — AMLA and its institutions
*Keywords: Administration of Muslim Law Act 1966, AMLA, came into force 1968, MUIS, Majlis Ugama Islam Singapura, Islamic Religious Council, Syariah Court, SYC, Registry of Muslim Marriages, ROMM, not a codified law, Shafeeq bin Salim Talib, Second Charter of Justice, Mohammedan Marriage Ordinance 1880, Muslim Ordinance 1957*

**End point:** AMLA was enacted in 1966 and came into force in 1968. It is not a codified set of substantive Muslim law. It regulates Muslim religious institutions and sets up MUIS (the Islamic Religious Council), the Syariah Court and the Registry of Muslim Marriages (ROMM). Background: the Second Charter of Justice 1826 received English law subject to the inhabitants' personal laws (*Shafeeq bin Salim Talib v Fatimah bte Abud bin Taib* [2010] 2 SLR 1123 at [19]–[20]). The Mohammedan Marriage Ordinance 1880 first recognised Muslim personal law, and the Muslim Ordinance 1957 set up the Syariah Court.

*Source note: MU1 s5–8.*

### pcp-m-2-2 — Who is a Muslim; laws excluded for Muslims
*Keywords: Muslim defined, s 2 AMLA, professes the religion of Islam, all laws apply unless specifically excluded, Women's Charter s 3(2), s 3(2A), Parts 2 to 6 do not apply to person married under Muslim law, s 3(4) no Muslim marriage under WC, Intestate Succession Act s 2, IFPA not applicable, Legitimacy Act*

**End point:** A "Muslim" is a person who professes the religion of Islam (s 2 AMLA). All laws apply to Muslims unless they are specifically excluded. Exclusions include:
- WC Parts 2–6 (except ss 6A–6C), Parts 10 and 10A and ss 181–182 do not apply to anyone married under Muslim law or to a Muslim-law marriage (WC s 3(2));
- no marriage between Muslims may be solemnised or registered under the WC (s 3(4));
- the ISA does not apply to any Muslim's estate (ISA s 2);
- the IFPA does not apply to Muslims.

*Source note: MU1 s9; MU3 s6; MU-CS1 s31; WC s 3(2), (4) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026). Conflict: MU1 s9 cites "s 3(2), (2A)"; s 3(2A) deals with an incapacitated husband's maintenance, and the exclusion is in s 3(2).*

### pcp-m-2-3 — Civil laws that apply to Muslims
*Keywords: Women's Charter maintenance of wife s 69, maintenance of children s 69(2), s 113 does not apply after Muslim divorce, personal protection order PPO, ECAO s 126B enforcement of child access orders, Guardianship of Infants Act leave s 35A, Adoption of Children Act adopted child not automatic beneficiary, Probate and Administration Act same process, inheritance certificate s 115, Wills Act formalities only, CPF nomination, Insurance Act revocable nominations since 2017*

**End point:**

| Law | Applies to Muslims? |
|---|---|
| WC: maintenance of wife during marriage (s 69) and of children (s 69(2)) | Yes, even with SYC proceedings; but post-divorce wife maintenance (s 113) does not apply |
| WC: PPO | Yes, with or without SYC proceedings |
| WC: ECAO (s 126B, from 2 Jan 2025) | Yes: SYC access orders are enforced under it |
| WC: divorce | No: the FJC and HC have no jurisdiction over divorces of Muslim marriages |
| Guardianship of Infants Act | Yes. With no SYC divorce, no leave is needed; with SYC proceedings, leave is needed (s 35A AMLA) |
| Adoption of Children Act | Same process, but an adopted child does not automatically inherit |
| Probate and Administration Act | Same grant process; distribution by Muslim law; Inheritance Certificate first (s 115 AMLA) |
| Wills Act | Formalities only |
| CPF Act nominations; Insurance Act revocable nominations (since 2017) | Yes |

*Source note: MU1 s10; MU-CS1 s5–6, s31.*

## 2.II Jurisdiction of the Syariah Court

- **What can the SYC hear? (s 35(2))** marriage, talak / khuluk / taklik / fasakh, betrothal, nullity, children, property on divorce, emas kahwin, mutaah → [2-4](#pcp-m-2-4)
- **Personal jurisdiction: are both Muslims / married under Muslim law? Converted after a civil (ROM) marriage?** *Yeo Pei Chern*, *Noor Azizan* → [2-5](#pcp-m-2-5)
- **What makes a valid Muslim marriage? Married overseas, unregistered?** 5 essentials; s 109 → [2-6](#pcp-m-2-6)
- **Foreigner / not domiciled: territorial jurisdiction** → domicile or 3 years' habitual residence (s 35(3)) → [2-7](#pcp-m-2-7)
- **Already divorced overseas (e.g. Johore SYC)** → no SYC divorce jurisdiction (save nafkah iddah and mutaah) (*TMO v TMP*) → [2-8](#pcp-m-2-8)
- **Things the SYC cannot do: orders without a divorce application, second-wife application, Mareva injunction, enforce its money orders, child maintenance in practice** → [2-9](#pcp-m-2-9)

### pcp-m-2-4 — Subject-matter jurisdiction (s 35(2) AMLA)
*Keywords: s 35(2) AMLA, subject matter jurisdiction, creature of statute, marriage, fasakh, cerai taklik, khuluk, talak, betrothal, nullity, custody care and control access maintenance of minor children on divorce, disposition or division of property on divorce, emas kahwin, hantaran belanja, marriage expenses, maintenance, mutaah, consolatory gift, ER v ES*

**End point:** The SYC is a creature of statute and has only the jurisdiction AMLA gives it (*ER v ES* (2021) 8 SSAR 389). Under s 35(2) it hears actions where all the parties are Muslims, or were married under Muslim law, involving disputes about:
- (a) marriage;
- (b) divorces known as fasakh, cerai taklik, khuluk and talak;
- (c) betrothal or nullity;
- (ca) custody, care and control, access or maintenance of minor children on divorce or nullity;
- (d) disposition or division of property on divorce or nullity;
- (e) emas kahwin, marriage expenses (hantaran belanja), maintenance and consolatory gifts (mutaah).

*Source note: MU1 s12, s28; MU-CS1 s8. AMLA s 35(2)–(3) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026); s 35(2)(c), (ca) amended by Act 4 of 2024 wef 1 Jul 2024, wording as above.*

### pcp-m-2-5 — Personal jurisdiction: Muslims married under Muslim law
*Keywords: personal jurisdiction, all parties Muslims, married under Muslim law, valid Muslim marriage required, Yeo Pei Chern v Seow Zheng Xin Isa, conversion certificate unsigned, MUIS Appeal Board set aside decree, Noor Azizan bte Colony v Tan Lip Chin, ROM marriage then ROMM marriage, SYC decree dissolves civil marriage too, BO v BP, converted to Islam*

**End point:** Section 35(2) reads disjunctively ("all the parties are Muslims or … married under Muslim law"), but in practice a valid Muslim marriage is required (*Yeo Pei Chern v Seow Zheng Xin* (2007) 4 SSAR 146). There the wife's conversion certificate was unsigned, so there was no Muslim marriage, and the Appeal Board set aside the SYC divorce. If parties married civilly (ROM) and later, after conversion, also under Muslim law (ROMM), an SYC decree dissolves the whole marriage, civil included (*Noor Azizan bte Colony v Tan Lip Chin* [2006] 3 SLR(R) 707; *BO v BP* [2015] 7 SSAR 19). So SQ p68 Q6 option C (Muslim man, non-Muslim wife married abroad, wife consents) is wrong: consent cannot create jurisdiction. Option B (FJC or SYC at the parties' election) is wrong too: a ROMM marriage can be dissolved only at the SYC.

*Source note: MU1 s16–24; MU2 s7; SQ p68 Q6 (key p128: D).*

### pcp-m-2-6 — Essentials of a valid Muslim marriage
*Keywords: valid Muslim marriage, essential elements, male party, female party, wali, guardian of bride, two Muslim male witnesses, offer and acceptance, ijab kabul, ROMM registration accepted at face value, s 109 AMLA registration does not validate or invalidate, overseas marriage, affidavit of foreign marriage, attested marriage certificate, location does not determine validity*

**End point:** Five essential elements:
- (a) a male party;
- (b) a female party;
- (c) a wali (the bride's guardian);
- (d) two Muslim male witnesses;
- (e) offer and acceptance.

Location does not matter (*Yeo Pei Chern* at [30]). The SYC accepts a ROMM registration at face value. Registration does not by itself make a marriage valid or invalid (s 109 AMLA). For an overseas or unregistered marriage, file the attested foreign marriage certificate and usually an affidavit of foreign marriage showing the five elements, and whether it was registered.

*Source note: MU2 s8–10; MU1 s23, s26.*

### pcp-m-2-7 — Territorial jurisdiction: domicile or 3 years' habitual residence
*Keywords: territorial jurisdiction, s 35(3) AMLA, 22 October 2018, domiciled in Singapore, habitually resident 3 years, either party, foreigner, s 35(5) citizen presumed domiciled, connecting factor*

**End point:** For proceedings started on or after 22 Oct 2018, the SYC has jurisdiction only if either party is (a) domiciled in Singapore when the action starts, or (b) habitually resident in Singapore for at least 3 years immediately before (s 35(3)). A Singapore citizen is presumed domiciled in Singapore until the contrary is proved (s 35(5)). So a foreign Muslim married under Muslim law who has lived here for 3 years can file (SQ p68 Q6, key D).

*Source note: MU1 s13, s26; MU-CS1 s8; SQ p68 Q6 (key p128: D).*

### pcp-m-2-8 — Parties already divorced overseas
*Keywords: foreign divorce, overseas divorce, recognised divorce, Syariah Court Johore Malaysia, TMO v TMP, ED v EE, no SYC jurisdiction over divorce, nafkah iddah and mutaah only, children and assets after overseas divorce, s 121B Women's Charter financial relief after overseas divorce, 2026 AMLA amendment proposal*

**End point:** Once there is a recognised overseas divorce by a competent court, the SYC has no jurisdiction over the divorce, and can deal only with nafkah iddah and mutaah (*ED v EE* (2021) 8 SSAR 292; *TMO v TMP* [2017] 1 SLR 585). It cannot make orders on children or matrimonial assets. Those go to the civil courts under their residual jurisdiction, e.g. an application for financial relief after an overseas divorce (WC s 121B; see [2-12](#pcp-m-2-12)). A May 2026 MCCY consultation proposes giving the SYC these powers; not yet law (as at the course materials). SQ p67 Q2(III) and p68 Q8(C): a divorce application after a Johore SYC divorce cannot be heard.

*Source note: MU1 s26, s36; MU2 s50; SQ p67 Q2, p69 Q8 (key p128: D, A).*

### pcp-m-2-9 — What the Syariah Court cannot do
*Keywords: no ancillary orders without divorce application, maintenance against non-Muslim husband, application to marry second wife, polygamy, Kadi, Mareva injunction, no specific power, enforcement of financial orders at FJC, child maintenance referred to FJC, EN v EO, wife maintenance during marriage s 51 not used, former wife maintenance after divorce*

**End point:** The SYC:
- makes no ancillary orders unless there is a divorce application before it;
- does not hear a wife's maintenance claim against a non-Muslim husband (no personal jurisdiction);
- does not hear an application to marry a second wife: a marriage by a man already married may be solemnised only by a Kadi, or by the wali with a Kadi's written consent after inquiry (AMLA s 96(2)–(3)), so it is not within s 35(2) (SQ p67 Q2, p69 Q8);
- cannot grant a Mareva injunction, since it has no specific power to (SQ p68 Q4, key B);
- does not enforce its own financial orders (the FJC does: [2-23](#pcp-m-2-23));
- in practice does not hear child maintenance, which goes to the FJC (*EN v EO* [2021] SSAR 359);
- does not use its s 51(1) power for wife maintenance during marriage (WC s 69 at the FJC is used).

A former wife divorced years ago cannot claim spousal maintenance at the SYC (SQ p69 Q8 D).

*Source note: MU-CS1 s9; MU1 s42–44; SQ p67 Q2, p68 Q4, p69 Q8 (key p128: D, B, A). AMLA ss 35(2), 96(2)–(3) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026). The Mareva point rests on the SQ key: AMLA s 35(2) and s 52(3) give no such power.*

## 2.III Concurrent and residual jurisdiction with the civil courts; finality

- **SYC divorce ongoing: can custody / assets be heard in the FJC?** Leave of SYC to commence or continue (s 35A AMLA; s 17A SCJA) → [2-10](#pcp-m-2-10)
- **How does SYC decide leave? Consent route** → *BU v BV* balancing test; counselling → [2-11](#pcp-m-2-11)
- **No SYC divorce (or overseas divorce): civil courts' residual jurisdiction** *TMO v TMP*; s 121B WC → [2-12](#pcp-m-2-12)
- **Can an SYC or Appeal Board decision be judicially reviewed?** s 56A → [2-13](#pcp-m-2-13)

### pcp-m-2-10 — Concurrent jurisdiction: leave to commence or continue
*Keywords: concurrent jurisdiction, golden rules, SYC exclusive jurisdiction over Muslim divorce, s 17A(2) SCJA, maintenance custody division of property, s 35A(1) AMLA leave to commence, s 35A(2) leave to continue, s 17A(3) SCJA, GIA proceedings, children and matrimonial assets not in both forums, PPO and maintenance independent, 1999 amendments, s 17A(7) civil law applies*

**End point:** Golden rules:
1. The SYC has exclusive jurisdiction over a Muslim divorce. Once it is seised of the divorce and ancillaries, the civil courts have none.
2. Civil proceedings on children already running when SYC divorce proceedings begin need SYC leave to **continue** (s 35A(2) AMLA; s 17A(3)(b) SCJA).
3. After SYC proceedings start (or a decree), civil proceedings on children or matrimonial assets need SYC leave to **commence** (s 35A(1); s 17A(3)(a)).
4. Where there is no SYC divorce application, the civil courts have residual jurisdiction (ss 16–17 SCJA).

Children and assets cannot be heard in both forums at once. PPO and maintenance proceedings at the FJC are independent of the SYC divorce and may run simultaneously. Section 17A(2) SCJA gives the civil courts jurisdiction over wife or child maintenance, custody, and property division on divorce, despite AMLA. When the civil court decides children or assets, civil law applies (s 17A(7)).

*Source note: MU1 s29–35; MU-CS1 s12–13; SCJA s 17A(1)–(6) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026): the General Division has no jurisdiction over matters in AMLA s 35(2)(a)–(c) (marriage, divorce, betrothal, nullity) between Muslims; it stays custody or property proceedings started after SYC divorce proceedings unless a Syariah Court commencement certificate is filed, and custody proceedings overtaken by SYC divorce proceedings unless a continuation certificate is filed; SYC proceedings started the same day are deemed first (s 17A(4)).*

### pcp-m-2-11 — Leave from the SYC: the balancing test; continuing by consent
*Keywords: test for leave, BU v BV, AK v AL, balancing of interests, expedient resolution, duplication of proceedings, undue prejudice, tenets of Syariah law, welfare of children, consent to continuation, s 35A(5) (6) (7) AMLA, counselling, certificate of attendance*

**End point:** Leave is decided by balancing the parties' legitimate interests (*AK v AL* [2012] 6 SSAR 184; *BU v BV* [2018] 7 SSAR 64). The SYC asks whether leave:
- (a) allows more expedient resolution, and whether expediency is needed;
- (b) avoids or causes duplicated proceedings;
- (c) unduly prejudices the other party in the SYC proceedings, or undermines Syariah tenets;
- (d) affects the children's welfare.

No undue burden is placed on an applicant. Parties may instead consent to the FJC proceedings continuing, but must attend counselling and get a certificate of attendance (s 35A(5)–(7)).

*Source note: MU1 s35, s37–38.*

### pcp-m-2-12 — Residual jurisdiction of the civil courts
*Keywords: residual jurisdiction, TMO v TMP, must-read, no specific exclusion, no separate Muslim law, overseas divorce, application for financial relief after overseas divorce, s 121B Women's Charter, UJM v UJL, GIA application without SYC divorce, ss 16 17 SCJA*

**End point:** Where there is no specific exclusion and no separate Muslim law, the civil courts have residual jurisdiction over Muslims (*TMO v TMP* [2017] SGCA 14). The SYC can make ancillary orders only with a divorce application before it. So if a Muslim couple were divorced overseas, either may apply for financial relief after an overseas divorce (WC s 121B; *UJM v UJL* [2021] 2 SLR 1467). Children issues go to the FJC under the GIA. Do not confuse this with concurrent jurisdiction (an SYC divorce is running).

*Source note: MU1 s36; MU-CS1 s14–15.*

### pcp-m-2-13 — Finality of SYC and Appeal Board decisions (s 56A)
*Keywords: s 56A AMLA, decision final, no appeal to civil courts, no judicial review, quashing order, prohibiting order, mandatory order, injunction, Mohamed Yusoff bin Mohd Haniff v Umi Kalsom, Qureshi Mohamed Aslam v MUIS 2026 SGCA 19*

**End point:** A decision of the SYC or the Appeal Board is final. It cannot be challenged, appealed, reviewed, quashed or questioned in any court, nor be subject to a quashing, prohibiting or mandatory order or injunction (s 56A). The High Court dismissed a judicial review application in *Mohamed Yusoff bin Mohd Haniff v Umi Kalsom bte Abas* [2010] 3 SLR 481. In *Qureshi Mohamed Aslam v MUIS* [2026] SGCA 19 (17 Apr 2026), the Court of Appeal refused permission for judicial review of an Appeal Board decision that gave no reasons: the threshold was not met.

*Source note: MU2 s62; MU-CS1 s11.*

### pcp-m-2-14 — FJC v SYC divorce process
*Keywords: bifurcated system, interim judgment, final judgment, ancillary matters hearing, SYC not bifurcated, decree of divorce and ancillary orders at same hearing, divorce certificate 3 months, iddah, originating summons, case statement, mediation, PTC, hakam, appeal 30 days*

**End point:**

| | FJC (Women's Charter) | SYC (AMLA) |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Bifurcated: interim judgment on the divorce, then the ancillary matters hearing | Not bifurcated: decree of divorce and ancillary orders at the same hearing |
| Final step | Final judgment after all orders (or 3 months after IJ if all is by consent) | Divorce certificate issues 3 months after the divorce date (end of the wife's iddah) |

SYC process: originating summons (case statement), defence, mediation, PTC, affidavits, possible counselling or custody evaluation report, hakam if needed, hearing, then appeal within 30 days.

*Source note: MU2 s12–14; MU-CS1 s17.*

### pcp-m-2-15 — Grounds of divorce: WC v AMLA
*Keywords: grounds of divorce, Women's Charter s 95, irretrievable breakdown, adultery, unreasonable behaviour, desertion, separation, divorce by mutual agreement 1 July 2024, AMLA, talak, taklik, fasakh, khuluk, wife can divorce, prescribed form*

**End point:**

| FJC (WC s 95): irretrievable breakdown shown by | SYC (AMLA): divorce on one of |
|---|---|
| (a) adultery; (b) unreasonable behaviour; (c) desertion; (d) separation; (e) divorce by mutual agreement (from 1 Jul 2024) | Talak (husband's pronouncement, s 46B); cerai taklik (breach of marriage condition, s 48); fasakh (judicial dissolution, s 49); khuluk (redemption, s 47(4)) |

The husband applies by talak. The wife may apply to register a talak or ask the husband to pronounce one (s 47), or apply for taklik, fasakh or khuluk, each on its prescribed form. A Muslim wife can obtain a divorce even if the husband refuses talak (SQ p67 Q1, key D).

*Source note: MU2 s18, s22–23; MU-CS1 s19; SQ p67 Q1 (key p128: D).*

### pcp-m-2-16 — Talak
*Keywords: talak, talaq, pronouncement by husband, I divorce you with one talak, s 46B AMLA, no need to prove fault, disputed pronouncement, certainty of words, BQ v BR, registration of divorce, s 46, valid only after registration by SYC, rujuk, revocation within 3 months, re-solemnise, third talak, cannot remarry unless wife marries another, 2008 amendments all divorces registered in court*

**End point:** The most common divorce is talak, pronounced by the husband (e.g. "I divorce you with one talak") (s 46B). No fault need be proved. If the wife disputes it, the SYC hears the evidence and decides whether the words were certain and unequivocal (*BQ v BR* [2016] 7 SSAR 36). A talak pronounced outside court is valid only once registered by the SYC on either party's application (ss 46, 46B). Since the 2008 amendments, all divorces are registered at the SYC.
- First or second talak: revocable by reconciliation (rujuk) within 3 months; after that, the parties must re-solemnise.
- Third talak: the parties cannot remarry unless the wife has married another man and been widowed or divorced.

*Source note: MU2 s21; MU1 s40 (2008 amendment note).*

### pcp-m-2-17 — Cerai taklik
*Keywords: cerai taklik, taklik, breach of condition, marriage contract conditions, ROMM standard taklik, leave wife 4 months, fail to maintain 4 months, injury to body, damage to property, loss of self-respect, one talak, s 48 AMLA, s 48(2) examine written taklik, foreign marriage no taklik, add own taklik*

**End point:** Taklik are conditions in the marriage contract. The standard ROMM taklik (printed in the marriage certificate) says that if the husband:
- (a) leaves the wife for 4 months or more continuously, intentionally or not;
- (b) fails to maintain her for more than 4 months while she is obedient; or
- (c) injures her body, damages her property or causes her to lose self-respect,

she may complain to the SYC, and if proved she is divorced by one talak (s 48). The court must examine the written taklik (s 48(2)). Some foreign marriage certificates have none, and then this ground is unavailable. Parties may agree additional taklik, to be registered with ROMM.

*Source note: MU2 s24–26; MU-CS1 s20.*

### pcp-m-2-18 — Fasakh (s 49)
*Keywords: fasakh, judicial dissolution, s 49 AMLA, failed to maintain 3 months, imprisonment 3 years, marital obligations one year, impotent, insane, chronic disease, cruelty, habitually assaults, ill repute, immoral life, obstructs religion, cohabits with another woman, inequitable treatment of wives, apostasy*

**End point:** The wife may seek fasakh on any ground in s 49(1):
- (a) the husband has failed to maintain her for 3 months;
- (b) he has been sentenced to 3 years' imprisonment or more, and the sentence is final;
- (c) he has failed without reasonable cause to perform marital obligations for 1 year;
- (d) he was impotent at the marriage and still is;
- (e) he is insane or has a chronic disease injurious to her;
- (f) cruelty: he
  - (i) habitually assaults her or makes her life miserable;
  - (ii) associates with women of ill repute;
  - (iii) tries to force her into an immoral life;
  - (iv) obstructs her religious practice;
  - (v) cohabits with another woman not his wife; or
  - (vi) does not treat co-wives equitably;
- (g) any other ground valid for fasakh under Muslim law (e.g. apostasy).

*Source note: MU2 s28–29; AMLA s 49(1)–(7) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026) (s 49(2): on impotence the husband may ask for 1 year to show he has ceased to be impotent; s 49(4): sworn statements of the wife and at least 2 witnesses; s 49(7): (1)(g) also available to a husband).*

### pcp-m-2-19 — Khuluk (divorce by redemption)
*Keywords: khuluk, khula, divorce by redemption, s 47(4) AMLA, wife pays husband, assessed by court, status and means, nominal amount, irrevocable, cannot remarry each other, rare, no nafkah iddah*

**End point:** If the husband will not divorce the wife but they agree to a divorce by redemption, the SYC assesses the payment from the wife by the parties' status and means, and has the husband pronounce the divorce (s 47(4)). It is rare, and the sum is usually nominal and agreed. Khuluk is irrevocable: the parties cannot remarry each other. The wife gets no nafkah iddah after khuluk ([2-24](#pcp-m-2-24)).

*Source note: MU2 s31; MU-CS1 s23.*

### pcp-m-2-20 — Hakam (marriage conciliators)
*Keywords: hakam, marriage conciliators, arbitrators renamed 2022, s 50 AMLA, husband refuses to pronounce talak, each party appoints hakam, reconciliation, full authority, pronounce divorce on husband's behalf, AW v AX, wife will get divorce, talak tafwidh, 2026 proposal*

**End point:** If the husband refuses to pronounce talak, hakam may be appointed whatever the ground (s 50(1)). Each party appoints one from the SYC's list. The hakam try to reconcile the parties, and with full authority may pronounce a divorce and report it for registration. The SYC may direct the husband to pronounce talak, or the hakam do so for him (*AW v AX* [2013] 6 SSAR 309). In practice the wife will get her divorce: the SYC does not dismiss her application because the husband refuses, or because she failed to prove taklik or fasakh (SQ p69 Q7, key D). The 2022 amendments replaced "arbitrators" with hakam. A 2026 proposal would dispense with hakam where the husband delegates talak to the wife (talak tafwidh).

*Source note: MU2 s32; MU-CS1 s21; SQ p69 Q7 (key p128: D).*

### pcp-m-2-21 — SYC structure, appeals and representation
*Keywords: Syariah Court President, Senior President, Registrar, MUIS Appeal Board, s 55 AMLA, three members, appeal from Registrar 14 days to President, rule 38 MMDR, no further appeal, appeal from President 30 days to Appeal Board, rule 39, consent order leave s 55(2), ROMM appeal s 105, s 39 appearance, lawyers need not be Muslim, Jamal Mahammath v Zarina Majid*

**End point:**
- **Registrar's direction or order:** notice of appeal within 14 days to the President (one person), with no further appeal (r 38 MMDR).
- **President's final order:** notice of appeal within 30 days to the MUIS Appeal Board, three members (r 39 MMDR; s 55(1)). Appealing a consent order needs the Board's leave (s 55(2)). The Board also hears ROMM appeals (s 105).

Parties appear in person, by advocate and solicitor, or by an authorised agent (s 39). Lawyers need not be Muslim or trained in Islamic law (*Jamal Mahammath v Zarina Majid* [2008] 4 SSAR 165 at [21]).

*Source note: MU2 s4–5, s15; MU-CS1 s18.*

### pcp-m-2-22 — Orders the SYC makes on divorce (s 52(3))
*Keywords: s 52(3) AMLA, powers on divorce, emas kahwin, hantaran belanja, mutaah, custody maintenance and education of minor children, disposition or division of property, dissolution of marriage, nafkah iddah, child of the parties defined, adopted child, children above 21, no orders without divorce application*

**End point:** At any stage of divorce or nullity proceedings, or after the decree, the SYC may on application order (s 52(3)):
- (a) emas kahwin and marriage expenses (hantaran belanja) to the wife;
- (b) mutaah to the wife;
- (c) custody, maintenance and education of the minor children;
- (d) disposition or division of property.

The common orders are dissolution (confirmation of talak), nafkah iddah, mutaah, custody, care and control and access, and division of matrimonial assets. "Child of the parties" includes a legally adopted child (s 2). In practice the SYC makes no orders for children over 21.

*Source note: MU1 s40–41; MU-CS1 s9, s27.*

### pcp-m-2-23 — Wife's maintenance during marriage
*Keywords: wife maintenance during marriage, s 69 Women's Charter, FJC, s 51(1) AMLA not invoked, Chaytor Alan James v Zaleha Rahman, talak pronounced but no divorce certificate, still married woman, maintenance order lapses on divorce certificate, arrears*

**End point:** During the marriage, a Muslim wife claims maintenance at the FJC under WC s 69, applying civil law. The SYC's s 51(1) power is not used in practice. A husband who has pronounced talak must still pay until the SYC issues the divorce certificate. Until then she is a "married woman" under s 69 (*Chaytor Alan James v Zaleha Rahman* [2001] 1 SLR(R) 504). On the certificate, the WC maintenance order lapses, though arrears can still be claimed. Her post-divorce claim is to nafkah iddah and mutaah at the SYC.

*Source note: MU1 s42–43; MU-CS1 s5.*

### pcp-m-2-24 — Nafkah iddah
*Keywords: nafkah iddah, iddah, eddah, 3 months, waiting period, cannot remarry, maintenance during iddah, ss 35(2)(e) and 51 AMLA, quantum factors, husband's means, social standing, clothing and lodging, $500 to $1,500 per month below $7,000, not payable khuluk, hakam pronouncement, third talak, waiver, instalments, from husband's share of sale proceeds*

**End point:** A divorced wife may not remarry during her iddah (about 3 months), so the husband must maintain her for that period (ss 35(2)(e), 51). Quantum depends on:
- the husband's means;
- the parties' social standing;
- clothing and suitable lodging;
- the cost of living in Singapore.

The norm for husbands earning below $7,000 a month is $500–$1,500 a month. She gets nafkah iddah except where the divorce is by hakam pronouncement, by khuluk, or a third talak. She may waive it. The court may order instalments or payment from the husband's share of the sale of matrimonial property.

*Source note: MU2 s34; MU-CS1 s23–24. Conflict: Cyril-MU (line on entitlements) lists triple talak and post-iddah only; MU-CS1 s23 (hakam, khuluk, third talak) is followed.*

### pcp-m-2-25 — Mutaah
*Keywords: mutaah, consolatory gift, ss 35(2)(e) and 52(3)(b) AMLA, husband's means at divorce, standard of living, capacity to pay, length of marriage, $ per day of marriage, entitlement not conduct, CQ v CR, wife initiated divorce, husband renounces Islam, Daud Salim v Hayati*

**End point:** On divorce, the husband pays mutaah, a consolatory gift to help the wife with the hardship of divorce (ss 35(2)(e), 52(3)(b)). Quantum depends on:
- the husband's means at the time of divorce (not after);
- the standard of living before divorce;
- his capacity to pay;
- the length of the marriage.

It is computed as a daily rate × the number of days from marriage to divorce (talak). It is an entitlement, not dependent on the wife's conduct (*CQ v CR* [2019] 7 SSAR 210 at [19]), and is payable in all cases, even if she started the divorce. It is still payable if the husband has left Islam (*Daud Salim v Hayati bte Atnawi* (2003) 3 SSAR 124).

*Source note: MU2 s35; MU-CS1 s23, s25.*

### pcp-m-2-26 — Children: custody, care and control, access
*Keywords: custody, care and control, access, welfare of the child paramount, BQ v BR, joint custody norm, Zaini bin Ibrahim v Rafidah Rahman, CX v CY, AE v AF adultery does not disqualify mother, DH v DI, AY v AZ spiritual welfare, children under seven with mother, mother's remarriage, judicial interview above seven mumaiyiz, AA v AB, child expert CAPS, custody evaluation report HM v HL, AI v AJ access, DA v DC no orders for third parties, grandparents*

**End point:** AMLA does not codify Muslim custody rules, and the SYC applies the child's welfare as paramount (*BQ v BR* [2016] 7 SSAR 36), often following civil cases.
- **Joint custody** is the norm (*Zaini bin Ibrahim v Rafidah Rahman* [2007] 3 SSAR 135, adopting *CX v CY*).
- **Adultery** does not automatically cost a mother custody (*AE v AF* [2011] 6 SSAR 65; *DH v DI*). **Remarriage** may affect care and control but only through welfare. **Income** is not decisive.
- **Spiritual welfare** counts but is not the sole factor (*AY v AZ* [2015] 6 SSAR 386: care and control to the Muslim mother in Singapore).
- Children **under 7** are generally with the mother, subject to welfare.
- Children **above 7** (mumaiyiz) of sufficient maturity are usually interviewed by the President with a CAPS child expert since 2022. Their wishes do not override welfare (*AA v AB* [2011] 6 SSAR 120).
- The court may call for a custody evaluation report (*HM v HL* [2021] 9 SSAR 316).
- Access orders are like the FJC's (*AI v AJ* [2011] 6 SSAR 143).
- s 52(3)(c) covers only the married couple, so the SYC cannot give custody, care and control to grandparents or other third parties (*DA v DC* [2020] 8 SSAR 72).

SQ: p68 Q5 (key B: no order for grandparents); p70 Q10 (key B: interview above 7).

*Source note: MU2 s37–50; SQ p68 Q5, p70 Q10 (key p128: B, B).*

### pcp-m-2-27 — Division of matrimonial assets
*Keywords: matrimonial assets, s 52(14) AMLA definition, acquired during marriage, pre-marriage asset substantially improved, gift or inheritance excluded unless matrimonial home or improved, similar to s 112(10) Women's Charter, s 52(7) just and equitable, s 52(8) factors, s 112(2) WC, ANJ v ANK structured approach, CY v CZ, DZ v EA, TNL v TNK single income, broad brush, homemaker indirect contributions, no transfer to High Court above $5 million, no divorce no division*

**End point:** Without a divorce before the SYC, there is no power to divide assets (s 35(2)(d)). "Property" (s 52(14), like WC s 112(10)) is:
- any asset acquired before marriage and substantially improved during it by the other or both; and
- any other asset acquired during the marriage by either or both,

but not a gift or inheritance (other than the matrimonial home) not substantially improved. The SYC divides in such proportions as are just and equitable (s 52(7)), guided by the non-exhaustive factors in s 52(8)(a)–(m), like WC s 112(2). It applies the *ANJ v ANK* [2015] 4 SLR 1043 structured approach for dual-income marriages (*CY v CZ* [2020] 8 SSAR 38; *DZ v EA* [2021] 8 SSAR 241). It also applies the single-income approach in *TNL v TNK* and the broad-brush approach where suitable. So a homemaker's indirect contributions count. There is no automatic transfer to the High Court for large estates (SQ p68 Q3 A and B wrong).

*Source note: MU2 s52–58; SQ p68 Q3 (key p128: C).*

### pcp-m-2-28 — Third-party claims to matrimonial assets
*Keywords: third party, intervener, co-owner, wife's mother, stay of divorce proceedings, UDA v UDB, DA v DC, Safie bin Jantan v Zaiton bte Adom, DD v DF, EB v EC, no jurisdiction over third parties, HDB regulations, CPF regulations*

**End point:** Where a third party claims an interest in a matrimonial asset, the divorce proceedings must be stayed (*UDA v UDB* [2018] SGCA 20). AMLA gives the SYC no jurisdiction or power to decide the substantive rights of anyone but the husband and wife (*DA v DC* [2020] 8 SSAR 72; *Safie bin Jantan v Zaiton bte Adom* [2023] SGHC(A) 8). An intervener may be heard but no order is made for or against him. So the SYC cannot make orders against the wife's mother as co-owner (SQ p68 Q3, key C). Option D (orders on property or CPF "regardless of the HDB and CPF regulations") is marked wrong by the key; other laws still govern how property is dealt with (compare *Haja Maideen*: an HDB transfer without HDB consent is void, [2-45](#pcp-m-2-45)).

*Source note: MU2 s47, s60; SQ p68 Q3 (key p128: C). Option D: key p128 (C correct); MU3 s42 (Haja Maideen).*

### pcp-m-2-29 — Enforcing nafkah iddah and mutaah (s 53(1))
*Keywords: enforcement, s 53(1) AMLA, nafkah iddah and mutaah treated as maintenance orders, Women's Charter enforcement, FJC, no leave required, no commencement certificate, Maintenance Enforcement Process, Family Justice Reform Act 2024, variation*

**End point:** Orders for nafkah iddah and mutaah are treated as WC maintenance orders for enforcement (s 53(1), from the 2008 amendments). The party files enforcement proceedings separately at the FJC, with no SYC leave or certificate needed, since the SYC does not enforce financial orders. All WC enforcement tools, including the Maintenance Enforcement Process under the Family Justice Reform Act 2024, are available (SQ p70 Q9, key D; p68 Q4 C wrong). The key also marks wrong option C (parties may choose the FJC or the SYC to vary a mutaah order).

*Source note: MU1 s43, s46; AMLA s 53(1) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026): orders under s 51 (maintenance incl. nafkah iddah), s 52(2) or (3)(b) (mutaah) and s 52(3)(c) (child maintenance) are treated as Part 9 WC maintenance orders for enforcement by the Family Court; SQ p68 Q4, p70 Q9 (key p128: B, D).*

### pcp-m-2-30 — Enforcing other SYC orders (s 53(2), s 53A)
*Keywords: s 53(2) AMLA, SYC orders deemed civil court orders, BOSCO, breach of Syariah Court orders complaint, mediation, committal, contempt, VFU v VFV, ECAO, enforcement of child access orders, s 126B WC, 2 January 2025, s 53A proxy power, SYC signs transfer documents, defaulting party absent neglects refuses*

**End point:** Other SYC orders (children, division of assets) are deemed civil court orders for enforcement (s 53(2)). At the FJC the options are:
- a BOSCO (Breach of Syariah Court Orders) complaint, with mediation before an FJC judge or mediator;
- committal for contempt (*VFU v VFV* [2020] SGHCF 3);
- since 2 Jan 2025, an ECAO application (WC s 126B) with make-up access, counselling and more.

The SYC itself cannot imprison a husband who refuses access (SQ p70 Q9 B wrong). Its only enforcement power is to sign transfer or sale documents for matrimonial property when a party, given the chance, is absent, neglects or refuses (s 53A).

*Source note: MU1 s47–48; MU-CS1 s6; SQ p70 Q9.*

### pcp-m-2-31 — When Muslim inheritance law applies; statutes table
*Keywords: s 112 AMLA intestacy, Muslim domiciled in Singapore, distributed according to Muslim law, Malay custom, partial intestacy, harta sepencarian, jointly acquired property, s 112(3), s 111 wills, 1 July 1968, ISA not applicable, IFPA not applicable, Legitimacy Act, adopted children, CPF Act nominations, Insurance Act, Probate and Administration Act*

**End point:** The estate of a Muslim domiciled in Singapore who dies intestate (wholly or partly) is distributed by Muslim law, as modified by Malay custom where applicable (s 112(1)–(2)). For a Malay dying intestate, the court may divide harta sepencarian (jointly acquired property) as it thinks fit (s 112(3); mentioned obiter in *Shafeeq*). Wills must follow Muslim law (s 111; [2-36](#pcp-m-2-36)).

### pcp-m-2-32 — Faraid: the basics
*Keywords: faraid, hukum faraid, Quranic shares, fixed shares, estate after funeral expenses debts and valid bequests, son receives double daughter, male responsibility to maintain, renunciation of share, redistribution, Fatwa Committee, deed of family arrangement, Syariah Court online inheritance calculator, calculation not examinable, husband wife parents inherit*

**End point:** Faraid is the Islamic inheritance system of shares fixed in the Quran. It distributes the estate left after funeral expenses, debts and valid bequests. Spouses, females and parents all inherit. The general rule is that a daughter takes half a son's share, reflecting the male heir's duty to maintain female relatives. Heirs may by consensus renounce and redistribute their shares (allowed by MUIS's Fatwa Committee), usually by a deed of family arrangement. **The course does not require calculating individual shares or knowing the differences between the schools.** Shares come from the SYC Inheritance Certificate or its online calculator.

*Source note: MU3 s3, s11–18; MU-CS1 s29. No share table is given here because MU3 s3 and MU-CS1 s29 say calculations are not examinable.*

### pcp-m-2-33 — School of law (mazhab)
*Keywords: Sunni, Shia, Shafii, Hanafi, Maliki, Hambali, s 33 AMLA, Fatwa Committee follows Shafii, s 33(2) other schools, s 113 AMLA school of law stated in probate affidavit, Inheritance Certificate based on deceased's school, Fauziyah Mohd Ahbidin v MUIS 2026 SGHC 104, documentary evidence*

**End point:** Singapore Muslims are mainly Sunni. The Fatwa Committee ordinarily follows the Shafii school (s 33), though others may be followed where appropriate (s 33(2)). The Inheritance Certificate follows the deceased's school at death. A probate or administration affidavit for a Muslim must state the school (mazhab) he professed (s 113). Where the school is disputed, it is decided on documents and the best evidence available, not witnesses' assumptions (*Fauziyah Mohd Ahbidin v MUIS* [2026] SGHC 104 at [42]–[53]).

*Source note: MU3 s14–15.*

### pcp-m-2-34 — Baitulmal
*Keywords: Baitulmal, General Endowment Fund, s 57 AMLA, MUIS trustee, no rightful beneficiary, convert with only non-Muslim heirs, no male beneficiaries, appeal to MUIS to return share on hardship, not applicable to Hanafi*

**End point:** The Baitulmal (General Endowment Fund, s 57), administered by MUIS, takes a share of an estate where there is no rightful heir (e.g. a single person with no relatives, or a convert leaving only non-Muslim heirs who made no will), or where there are no male beneficiaries. Heirs may appeal to MUIS to return the share on hardship (e.g. a widow or daughter). Baitulmal does not apply where the deceased was Hanafi. A will cannot simply exclude the Baitulmal's share (MU3 s26 example).

*Source note: MU3 s20, s26.*

### pcp-m-2-35 — Grants for Muslim estates: Inheritance Certificate first
*Keywords: Inheritance Certificate, s 115 AMLA, Syariah Court issues, before applying for probate or letters of administration, grants issued by FJC or High Court not Syariah Court, estate above $5 million High Court, resealing, file will hibah nuzriah deed of family arrangement, schedule of assets excludes joint tenancy and nominated CPF*

**End point:** All grants for Muslim estates are issued by the FJC or the High Court (over $5m), not the SYC. Before applying, obtain an Inheritance Certificate from the SYC (s 115). It lists the faraid beneficiaries and their shares under the deceased's school. It is needed even if there is a will or deed of family arrangement, and must be filed with the papers. Also file any will, hibah, nuzriah or deed of family arrangement. Leave joint tenancy property and nominated CPF out of the Schedule of Assets.

*Source note: MU1 s10; MU3 s28–29; Cyril-MU (nuzriah).*

### pcp-m-2-36 — The one-third rule (s 111)
*Keywords: Muslim will, s 111 AMLA, no Muslim domiciled in Singapore may dispose by will except per Muslim law, 1 July 1968, one-third, bequest limit, two-thirds to faraid heirs, no bequest to faraid heir without consent of all heirs, Mohamed Ismail bin Ibrahim v Mohamad Taha bin Ibrahim, s 114, invalid Muslim will, everything to my wife, prohibit Baitulmal*

**End point:** A Muslim domiciled in Singapore may not, after 1 Jul 1968, dispose of property by will except as his school of Muslim law allows (s 111(1)). The principal restrictions:
- he may not bequeath more than one-third of his estate; the other two-thirds go to the faraid heirs in fixed shares;
- he may not give any of the one-third to a faraid heir;

unless all the heirs consent (*Mohamed Ismail bin Ibrahim v Mohamad Taha bin Ibrahim* [2004] 4 SLR(R) 756 at [31]). So "I give everything to my wife" or "everything to my wife and only daughter, and no share to Baitulmal" are invalid beyond what Muslim law allows, absent all heirs' consent. Section 111 does not affect the Wills Act (other than s 3), the Insurance Act, the PAA, or wills of Muslims who died before 1 Jul 1968 (s 111(2)).

*Source note: MU3 s9, s23–24, s26. AMLA s 111 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026).*

### pcp-m-2-37 — Who may take under the one-third
*Keywords: one-third recipients, adopted children, stepchildren, step-parents, child out of wedlock, unrelated third parties, friends, charitable organisations, recipients need not be Muslim, convert gifts to non-Muslim family*

**End point:** Without the heirs' consent, the one-third must go to non-faraid beneficiaries, such as:
- (a) adopted children;
- (b) stepchildren or step-parents;
- (c) a child born out of wedlock;
- (d) unrelated third parties (friends, charities).

Recipients need not be Muslims, so a convert can provide for non-Muslim family this way. A gift to an adopted child of the testator's share of a joint tenancy fails for another reason: joint tenancy passes by survivorship ([2-43](#pcp-m-2-43)).

*Source note: MU3 s23, s26; MU-CS1 s35.*

### pcp-m-2-38 — Formalities of Muslim wills
*Keywords: Wills Act s 3(1) does not apply to Muslims, formalities apply, s 4 age 21, s 6 writing and execution, s 10 beneficiary witness, s 13 revoked by marriage, s 16 alterations, s 28 rectification, witnesses two Muslim males, no gender requirement under Wills Act, executor any gender or religion*

**End point:** Section 3 of the Wills Act (freedom of disposition) does not apply to Muslims, but the formalities do:
- s 4 (age 21, except soldiers on actual service);
- s 6 (writing and execution);
- s 10 (a beneficiary or spouse who witnesses loses the gift);
- s 13 (revocation by marriage);
- s 16 (alterations);
- s 28 (rectification).

Two witnesses present at the same time are needed (WA s 6, preserved for Muslims by AMLA s 111(2)(a)); the Wills Act sets no religion or gender requirement for witnesses. The course notes that in practice two Muslim men are often used. Executors and trustees may be of any gender or religion.

*Source note: MU3 s22–23; MU1 s10. WA s 6 and AMLA s 111(2)(a) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026). Conflict: MU3 s23 says witnesses are "2 Muslim male persons"; the statutes impose no such requirement, so it is treated as practice.*

### pcp-m-2-39 — Solicitor's duty: ask the client's religion
*Keywords: Zailani bin Abdullah Tan v K Jayakumar Naidu, solicitor negligence, Muslim name, duty to inquire religion, Syariah law, will drafting, Tan Puay Boon JC*

**End point:** A solicitor who sees a Muslim name on a will must at least ask the testator's religion, so as to consider whether Syariah law affects the instructions. It does not matter that the client did not mention it (*Zailani bin Abdullah Tan v K Jayakumar Naidu* [2017] SGDC 172).

*Source note: MU3 s25.*

### pcp-m-2-40 — The civil courts' approach
*Keywords: civil courts apply Muslim law, LS Investments v MUIS, Muslim law part of the law of the land, not proved like foreign law, wakaf, MUIS v Saeed Salman, starting point Muslim law, instruments must comply with other laws, three-step approach, estate planning instruments, faraid default, fatwa not binding*

**End point:** Muslim law is part of the law of the land and need not be proved like foreign law (*LS Investments v MUIS* [1998] 3 SLR 369: whether a wakaf existed is decided by Muslim law, not the common law of trusts; followed in *MUIS v Saeed Salman* [2016] SGHC 4). Approach:
1. The estate of a Muslim is distributed by Muslim law (s 111 or s 112).
2. Is the asset subject to an estate planning instrument (joint tenancy, CPF or insurance nomination, hibah)? If so, it may be outside the estate.
3. The remaining estate goes by faraid.

Maxims: an intention that breaks Muslim law (the one-third rule) fails. An instrument valid in Muslim law must still comply with other laws (e.g. HDB consent). A fatwa is an opinion and does not bind the court.

*Source note: MU3 s31–33, s50, s52; MU-CS1 s30, s39.*

### pcp-m-2-41 — Challenging an Inheritance Certificate
*Keywords: Re Will of Shaikh Ahmad bin Abdullah Wahdain Basharil, Inheritance Certificate challenged, several wives, whether married under Muslim law, question of fact, High Court forum, Syariah Court issues on information provided*

**End point:** The SYC issues an Inheritance Certificate on the applicant's information. If it is challenged (e.g. whether the deceased was validly married to other wives, so their children inherit), the High Court is the forum to decide, as a question of fact (*Re Will of Shaikh Ahmad bin Abdullah Wahdain Basharil* [2002] SGHC 165).

*Source note: MU3 s34–36.*

### pcp-m-2-42 — Letter of wishes and deed of family arrangement (Shiraz)
*Keywords: Shiraz Abidally Husain, letter of wishes, equal shares sons and daughters, agreement among children, deed of family arrangement, not inconsistent with Muslim law, binding, later-discovered will*

**End point:** After the father's death, his six children agreed to divide bank accounts equally according to his letter of wishes, before a will was found. The agreement was not inconsistent with Muslim law and bound them, so a child could not later insist on faraid shares (*Shiraz Abidally Husain* [2007] SGCA 16). Heirs may thus agree a different distribution.

*Source note: MU3 s37–39.*

### pcp-m-2-43 — Joint tenancy and survivorship (Shafeeq)
*Keywords: joint tenancy, right of survivorship, Shafeeq bin Salim Talib v Fatimah bte Abud bin Talib, Land Titles Act, property not part of estate, morphed into sole ownership, nothing to distribute, s 112 AMLA, assets of estate determined by personal law, 2007 fatwa, 2019 fatwa joint tenancy religiously valid*

**End point:** A house held by husband and wife as joint tenants passed to the wife by survivorship. Faraid did not apply because the property was not part of the husband's estate: it "morphed" into her sole ownership, so nothing passed (*Shafeeq bin Salim Talib v Fatimah bte Abud bin Talib* [2010] SGCA 11). The court of appeal added that what makes up a deceased Muslim's estate is decided first by his personal law, so Muslim law is not irrelevant. A 2019 fatwa now treats joint tenancy as a religiously valid contract, so civil and Muslim law are harmonised.

*Source note: MU3 s44–47.*

### pcp-m-2-44 — CPF nominations (Saniah Ali; 2010 fatwa)
*Keywords: CPF nomination, Saniah Ali v Abdullah Ali, stepsister nominee, CPF monies not part of estate, s 25 CPF Act, s 112 AMLA not applicable, fatwa not binding, revised fatwa 3 August 2010, nomination as hibah, no nomination faraid applies, insurance nomination*

**End point:** CPF monies paid to a nominee (here the deceased's step-sister) do not form part of the estate, so s 112 does not apply and the nominee keeps them. The fatwa that a nominee holds on trust for the faraid heirs did not bind the court (*Saniah Ali v Abdullah Ali* [1990] 1 SLR(R) 555). The fatwa revised on 3 Aug 2010 treats a CPF nomination as a hibah (gift). Without a nomination, faraid applies. A nomination can be changed.

*Source note: MU3 s48–51.*

### pcp-m-2-45 — Hibah (gift) and HDB flats
*Keywords: hibah, inter vivos gift, Haja Maideen v Roshan Begum, HDB flat, s 50 Housing and Development Act, HDB consent, transfer void, MUIS opinion not binding, hibah ruqba, gift conditional on death, nuzriah, vow*

**End point:** A mother's hibah of her HDB flat to her daughter was void because HDB's prior written consent was not obtained (s 50 HDA). MUIS's view that the hibah was valid in Muslim law did not bind the court (*Haja Maideen v Roshan Begum* [2017] SGHC 164). Whether property was validly disposed of is decided by the general law. A hibah ruqba is a gift conditional on death ("if you die before me it is mine; if I die before you it is yours").

*Source note: MU3 s40–43.*

## 3.I Legislative framework and classes of employees

- **Which statutes govern employment? Is the EA a floor?** → [3-1](#pcp-m-3-1)
- **Is this person an "employee"? Contract of service v for service; who is excluded (seafarer, domestic worker)** → [3-2](#pcp-m-3-2)
- **Part-time / temporary / contract staff covered by the EA?** → [3-3](#pcp-m-3-3)
- **Does Part IV (rest days, hours, overtime) apply? Workman ≤ $4,500; others ≤ $2,600; managers** → [3-4](#pcp-m-3-4)
- **Foreign employees: work passes; minors (s 12)** → [3-5](#pcp-m-3-5)

### pcp-m-3-1 — Key legislation
*Keywords: Employment Act 1968, EA, floor, minimum terms, contract of service, Employment of Foreign Manpower Act, CPF Act, Child Development Co-Savings Act, Industrial Relations Act, Trade Unions Act, Retirement and Re-employment Act, Work Injury Compensation Act, Workplace Safety and Health Act, Employment Claims Act 2016, Workplace Fairness Act 2025, Tripartite Advisories, Tripartite Guidelines*

**End point:** The Employment Act 1968 is the main labour statute. It applies to those under a contract of service and sets a floor: parties may agree better terms but not worse. Related laws include:
- the Employment of Foreign Manpower Act 1990 and CPF Act 1953;
- the Child Development Co-Savings Act 2001;
- the Industrial Relations Act 1960 and Trade Unions Act 1940;
- the Retirement and Re-employment Act 1993;
- the Work Injury Compensation Act 2019 and Workplace Safety and Health Act 2006;
- the Employment Claims Act 2016;
- the Workplace Fairness Act 2025 (passed, not yet in force).

Tripartite Advisories and Guidelines add guidance without the force of law.

*Source note: EM1 s4–5; EM-LP p3 (reading list).*

### pcp-m-3-2 — Who is an employee
*Keywords: employee definition, s 2(1) Employment Act, contract of service, contract for service, independent contractor, workman, seafarer excluded, domestic worker excluded, Minister's exclusion, Government employees, de facto employment, control test, remuneration, leave and benefits, training, access to confidential information, WICA s 3(1), CPF Act s 2(1)*

**End point:** Under EA s 2(1), an "employee" is a person who has entered into or works under a contract of service with an employer, including a workman. The definition excludes:
- seafarers;
- domestic workers;
- any class the Minister excludes by gazette notification.

Government employees are in only if declared. Independent contractors (contracts *for* service) are outside the EA. Whether a de facto employment relationship exists is decided by common law indicia: control, remuneration, leave and benefits, training, access to confidential information. WICA s 3(1) and CPF Act s 2(1) have their own definitions.

*Source note: EM1 s7; EA s 2(1) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026).*

### pcp-m-3-3 — Categories of employee
*Keywords: full-time, part-time employee, less than 35 hours a week, temporary, contract employee, fixed term, all covered by Employment Act*

**End point:** Full-time, part-time (under 35 hours a week), temporary and contract employees are all covered by the EA.

*Source note: EM1 s8; Cyril-EM §1.2.*

### pcp-m-3-4 — Who Part IV of the EA covers
*Keywords: Part IV Employment Act, rest days, hours of work, other conditions of service, workman, labourer, vehicle operator, earns up to $4,500, non-workman earns up to $2,600, s 35, manager, executive, overtime, SQ Part IV*

**End point:** Part IV (rest days, hours of work, overtime and other conditions) applies only to:
1. a **workman** (labourer, vehicle operator or other specified person) earning not more than $4,500 a month; and
2. an **employee who is not a workman** earning not more than $2,600 a month (s 35).

SQ p81 Q1: Part IV does not apply to a facilities manager earning $2,500 (key D). Being a manager takes him outside, whatever the pay. It does apply to a sales associate on $2,500, a bus driver on $4,400 (workman) and a construction worker on $2,800 (workman).

*Source note: EM1 s9; EA s 35 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026): (b) applies to every employee "other than a workman or a person employed in a managerial or an executive position" earning up to $2,600, salary excluding overtime, bonuses, AWS, incentives and allowances; SQ p81 Q1 (key p129: D).*

### pcp-m-3-5 — Foreign employees; minors
*Keywords: foreign employee, work pass, Work Permit, S Pass, Employment Pass, Personalised Employment Pass, ONE Pass, Ministry of Manpower, EFMA, contractual age, s 12 EA, below 18, not enforceable unless for his benefit*

**End point:** Foreign employees need an MOM work pass: Work Permit, S Pass, Employment Pass, or others (PEP, ONE Pass). A person under 18 may enter a contract of service, but it is not enforceable against him, and no damages are recoverable from him, unless it is for his benefit (EA s 12).

*Source note: EM1 s10; EM-Statutes EA s 12.*

### pcp-m-3-6 — Payment of salary and CPF
*Keywords: salary, no minimum wage, salary period not exceeding one month, s 20(2) EA, pay within 7 days, s 21(1), CPF contributions, s 7(1) CPF Act, citizens and permanent residents, record keeping, s 13 deemed breach for non-payment*

**End point:** There is no general minimum wage. The salary period is agreed but must not exceed one month (s 20(2)). Salary must be paid before the end of the seventh day after the salary period ends (s 21(1)). Employers must make CPF contributions for citizen and PR employees, at rates depending on wages and age (CPF Act s 7(1)). Failure to pay salary under Part 3 means the employer is deemed to have broken the contract (s 13(1); see [3-31](#pcp-m-3-31)).

*Source note: EM1 s12; EM-CS1 s7.*

### pcp-m-3-7 — Hours of work and overtime (Part IV)
*Keywords: hours of work, s 38 EA, 6 consecutive hours without break, 8 hours a day, 44 hours a week, 9 hours a day 5-day week, 48 hours alternate week, overtime, one and a half times hourly basic rate, s 38(4), 72 hours overtime a month, s 38(5), hourly basic rate, Fourth Schedule, worked example*

**End point:** A Part IV employee must not be required to work:
- more than 6 consecutive hours without a break;
- more than 8 hours a day or 44 hours a week (s 38(1)).

The variations are: up to 9 hours a day where the week is 5 days or fewer or some days are shorter; and up to 48 hours in one week where the alternate week is under 44 (at most 88 hours in 2 weeks). Work beyond these limits at the employer's request is overtime, paid at least 1.5 times the hourly basic rate (s 38(4)). Overtime is capped at 72 hours a month (s 38(5)).

**Worked example (monthly-rated):** hourly basic rate = (12 × monthly basic pay) ÷ (52 × 44). For $2,200 a month: 26,400 ÷ 2,288 = $11.54 an hour, so overtime is $17.31 an hour.

*Source note: EA s 38(1), (4), (5) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026). The lectures mention Part IV only by title. The monthly-rate formula is EA Fourth Schedule items 1–2 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026) (read from the formula image): 12 × monthly basic rate of pay ÷ (52 × 44), for both workmen and non-workmen on monthly rates.*

### pcp-m-3-8 — Annual leave (s 88A)
*Keywords: annual leave, s 88A EA, 3 months' service, 7 days first year, one more day each year, maximum 14 days, pro-rated, completed months, fraction half day, forfeit if absent more than 20%, take within 12 months, pay for untaken leave on dismissal other than misconduct*

**End point:** After 3 months' service, an employee is entitled to paid annual leave (on top of rest days, holidays and sick leave):

| Year of continuous service | Days |
|---|---|
| 1st | 7 |
| 2nd | 8 |
| 3rd | 9 |
| 4th | 10 |
| 5th | 11 |
| 6th | 12 |
| 7th | 13 |
| 8th and after | 14 (maximum) |

An incomplete year is pro-rated by completed months; a fraction of half a day or more counts as a day (s 88A(2)–(3)). Leave is forfeited if the employee is absent without permission or excuse for more than 20% of working days (s 88A(5)). Part IV employees must take it within 12 months (s 88A(6)). Untaken leave is paid if dismissed other than for misconduct (s 88A(8)).

*Source note: EM1 s13; EA s 88A (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026).*

### pcp-m-3-9 — Sick leave and hospitalisation leave (s 89)
*Keywords: sick leave, s 89 EA, 6 months' service, 14 days outpatient, 60 days hospitalisation, inclusive, medical practitioner examination, 3 months 5 days 15 days, 4 months 8 days 30 days, 5 months 11 days 45 days*

**End point:** After examination by a medical practitioner:

| Service | Outpatient sick leave a year | Hospitalisation (including outpatient) a year |
|---|---|---|
| 3 to under 4 months | 5 days | 15 days |
| 4 to under 5 months | 8 days | 30 days |
| 5 to under 6 months | 11 days | 45 days |
| 6 months or more | 14 days | 60 days |

Hospitalisation leave is the lesser of the cap and the outpatient days plus the days hospitalised (s 89(1)–(2)).

*Source note: EM1 s13 (14 days after 6 months); EA s 89 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026).*

### pcp-m-3-10 — Maternity, paternity, childcare and shared parental leave
*Keywords: maternity leave, paternity leave, childcare leave, shared parental leave, Child Development Co-Savings Act, CDCSA, Employment Act Part 9, child Singapore citizen at birth, not prescribed by EA, SQ paternity*

**End point:** The EA provides maternity and childcare leave. The CDCSA provides maternity, paternity, childcare and shared parental leave, generally only where the child is a Singapore citizen at birth. Paternity leave is not prescribed by the EA (SQ p82 Q6, key C). Current entitlements (MOM, Nov 2026):

| Leave | Entitlement |
|---|---|
| Maternity | 16 weeks of Government-Paid Maternity Leave (child a citizen and other criteria; first 8 weeks employer-paid, last 8 reimbursed), otherwise 12 weeks |
| Paternity | 4 weeks of Government-Paid Paternity Leave, mandatory from 1 Apr 2025; by default 4 continuous weeks within 16 weeks of the birth |
| Shared parental | 10 weeks for children born on or after 1 Apr 2026 (6 weeks for births 1 Apr 2025 – 31 Mar 2026), shared between the parents, within 12 months of the birth |

*Source note: EM1 s13; SQ p82 Q6 (key p129: C); MOM pages "Maternity leave eligibility and entitlement", "Paternity leave" and "Shared parental leave" (mom.gov.sg, Nov 2026). Childcare leave days were not confirmed on MOM and are left out.*

### pcp-m-3-11 — Retirement, re-employment and the EAP
*Keywords: Retirement and Re-employment Act, RRA, minimum retirement age 64, from 1 July 2026, raised from 63, re-employment up to 69, eligibility, Employment Assistance Payment, EAP, no suitable vacancy, s 8 RRA, dismissal on grounds of age prohibited, EAP is not retrenchment benefit*

**End point:** The minimum retirement age is 64, raised from 63 on 1 Jul 2026. Employers must offer re-employment to eligible employees reaching 64, up to age 69. If there is no suitable vacancy, they must pay an Employment Assistance Payment (EAP). An employee may not be dismissed on the ground of age before the minimum retirement age (RRA s 8). Retrenchment is a different matter: SQ p81 Q3 (Richard, 63, department made redundant): he can be retrenched and has no statutory retrenchment benefit. The EAP is not a retrenchment payment (key D).

*Source note: EM1 s15, EM-CS1 s10 (64 / 69 from 1 Jul 2026); EM2 s25 (s 8 RRA); SQ p81 Q3 (key p129: D). Conflict: Cyril-EM §1.3.5 gives re-employment at 63 up to 68 (2025 law); the Course Materials (64 / 69 from 1 Jul 2026) are followed.*

### pcp-m-3-12 — Employee personal data (PDPA)
*Keywords: Personal Data Protection Act 2012, PDPA, personal data defined, consent, exceptions, investigations and proceedings, evaluative purposes, course of employment, managing or terminating employment relationship, protection obligation*

**End point:** Personal data is data about an individual who can be identified from it alone, or with other information the organisation has or is likely to have. Employers must protect it. Consent is not required to collect, use or disclose it:
- for investigations and proceedings;
- for evaluative purposes;
- in the course of employment or business;
- to manage or end the employment relationship.

*Source note: EM1 s16.*

### pcp-m-3-13 — Workplace safety (WSHA) and work injury compensation (WICA)
*Keywords: Workplace Safety and Health Act, employer duty, reasonably practicable, persons at work, principals, contractors, criminal penalties, officers personally liable, Work Injury Compensation Act, WICA, no civil suit needed, all employees, excluded independent contractors, self-employed, domestic workers, uniformed personnel, arising out of and in course of employment, occupational diseases Second Schedule, compulsory insurance, claim within 1 year*

**End point:**
- **WSHA:** employers must take reasonably practicable measures for the safety and health of their workers and of others affected by their work. Principals must protect their contractors and sub-contractors. Persons at work must use protective equipment and cooperate. Breach is criminal, with personal liability for officers.
- **WICA:** employees claim for work injuries and listed occupational diseases (Second Schedule) without suing. It covers all employees except independent contractors, the self-employed, domestic workers and uniformed personnel. It covers any accident arising out of and in the course of employment, wherever it happens. Employers must insure. Claims must be made within 1 year of the accident. A WICA claim cannot be brought in the ECT (SQ p84 Q9, key B).

*Source note: EM1 s17–18; SQ p84 Q9 (key p129: B).*

## 3.III The employment contract: express terms, implied terms, variation

- **Express terms: letter of appointment, handbook incorporated; restrictive covenants (non-compete, non-solicit)** → [3-14](#pcp-m-3-14)
- **Implied terms: mutual trust and confidence (*Prashant Mudgal v SAP*), confidence, care, fidelity; custom** → [3-15](#pcp-m-3-15)
- **Employer changes the handbook / terms unilaterally: valid?** → [3-16](#pcp-m-3-16)

### pcp-m-3-14 — Express terms and restrictive covenants
*Keywords: express terms, letter of appointment, incorporation, company rules and regulations, handbook, implied incorporation, restrictive covenant, non-compete, non-solicitation, restraint of trade, prima facie void, legitimate business interest, reasonably necessary, public interest, trade secrets, trade connections, stability of workforce*

**End point:** Express terms are interpreted by ordinary contract rules. They are usually in the letter of appointment, which may incorporate company rules or a handbook, expressly or impliedly. Restrictive covenants (non-compete, non-solicitation of clients, suppliers or staff after leaving) are restraints of trade and prima facie void. They are upheld only if they protect a legitimate business interest (e.g. trade secrets, trade connections, workforce stability) and go no further than reasonably necessary to protect it, and are in the public interest.

*Source note: EM1 s20; Cyril-EM §1.4 (examples of legitimate interests).*

### pcp-m-3-15 — Implied terms
*Keywords: implied terms in law, obligation of confidence, duty of care, duty of good faith and fidelity, implied term of mutual trust and confidence, IMTC, Prashant Mudgal v SAP 2026 SGHC 15, performance improvement plan with intention to terminate, nominal damages, exclude by express wording, implied by custom, certain and reasonable, cannot contradict express term*

**End point:** Terms implied in law into employment contracts are:
- obligations of confidence;
- a duty of care;
- a duty of good faith and fidelity;
- mutual trust and confidence (IMTC).

The IMTC exists under Singapore law (*Prashant Mudgal v SAP* [2026] SGHC 15: an employer that put an employee on a PIP intending to terminate him was in breach, but only nominal damages were awarded). Employers may modify or exclude the IMTC by express wording. Terms may also be implied by custom if certain and reasonable. No implied term may contradict an express term.

*Source note: EM1 s21.*

### pcp-m-3-16 — Variation of terms
*Keywords: variation, unilateral change, variation clause, key employment term, handbook amendments, consensual variation, consideration, intention to create legal relations, estoppel*

**End point:** Ask:
1. Is there a variation clause?
2. If so, does it reach a key employment term?
3. With no clause, or for a key term, the variation must be consensual and supported by consideration with intention to create legal relations, or arise by estoppel.

*Source note: EM1 s22.*

## 3.IV Industrial relations

- **Tripartite partners (MOM, NTUC, SNEF)** → [3-17](#pcp-m-3-17)
- **What is a trade union? Registration within 1 month** → [3-18](#pcp-m-3-18)
- **Collective bargaining: recognition, proposals, Industrial Arbitration Court certification; who is bound; executives** → [3-19](#pcp-m-3-19)

### pcp-m-3-17 — Tripartism
*Keywords: tripartite partners, Ministry of Manpower, MOM, National Trades Union Congress, NTUC, Singapore National Employers Federation, SNEF, Tripartite Alliance for Dispute Management, TADM*

**End point:** The tripartite partners are the Ministry of Manpower, the National Trades Union Congress (the national confederation of unions) and the Singapore National Employers Federation. They issue the Tripartite Advisories and Guidelines, and run TADM for employment disputes.

*Source note: EM1 s24; EM2 s27.*

### pcp-m-3-18 — Trade unions (Trade Unions Act)
*Keywords: trade union, Trade Unions Act s 2, association of workmen or employers, regulate relations, good industrial relations, working conditions, productivity, register within one month*

**End point:** A trade union is any association of workmen or employers, temporary or permanent, whose principal object is to regulate their relations in order to:
- (a) promote good industrial relations;
- (b) improve workmen's working conditions or economic and social status; or
- (c) raise productivity (TUA s 2).

It must register within one month of being established.

*Source note: EM1 s25.*

### pcp-m-3-19 — Collective bargaining (Industrial Relations Act)
*Keywords: Industrial Relations Act, collective bargaining, recognition, secret ballot, majority, notice of proposals, collective agreement, Industrial Arbitration Court certification, binding on employer and unionised employees only, interpretation application, executive employees, conflict of interest, limited representation, reinstatement, retrenchment benefits*

**End point:**
1. **Recognition:** the union seeks recognition. If the employer refuses, a secret ballot is held, and recognition is required if a majority are members.
2. **Notice of proposals:** the recognised union invites the employer to negotiate a collective agreement.
3. **Certification:** the agreement binds only once certified by the Industrial Arbitration Court, and then only the employer and the unionised employees, not all staff.
4. **Interpretation:** any party bound may apply to the IAC to interpret it.

Unionised employees are governed by both their contract and the agreement. A union whose members are mostly non-executives cannot seek recognition for executives where there may be a conflict of interest, and may represent executives only for limited matters.

*Source note: EM1 s26–27; Cyril-EM §1.5.*

- **WFA claims: grievance process, mandatory mediation, ECT ≤ $250,000 / High Court; lawyers** → [3-24](#pcp-m-3-24)

### pcp-m-3-20 — Workplace harassment and bullying
*Keywords: workplace harassment, bullying, Workplace Safety and Health Act duty, reasonably practicable measures, Protection from Harassment Act 2014, POHA, civil remedies, criminal sanctions, Tripartite Advisory on Managing Workplace Harassment, Mental Well-Being at Workplaces*

**End point:** Every employer has a statutory duty under the WSHA to take reasonably practicable measures to ensure employees' safety and health at work. A victim of workplace harassment can seek civil remedies and criminal sanctions against the harasser (person or entity) under the Protection from Harassment Act 2014. The Tripartite Advisories on Managing Workplace Harassment and on Mental Well-Being at Workplaces give guidance.

*Source note: EM2 s5.*

### pcp-m-3-21 — Existing anti-discrimination framework (FCF, TGFEP)
*Keywords: Fair Consideration Framework, FCF, Tripartite Guidelines on Fair Employment Practices, TAFEP, no force of law, administrative penalties, curtail work pass privileges, false declaration, Employment of Foreign Manpower Act prosecution*

**End point:** Before the WFA, discrimination is addressed by the Fair Consideration Framework and the Tripartite Guidelines on Fair Employment Practices. They do not have the force of law and are enforced by administrative penalties, such as curtailing the employer's work pass privileges. A false declaration in a work pass application, that all candidates were fairly considered, may be prosecuted under the EFMA.

*Source note: EM2 s6.*

### pcp-m-3-22 — Flexible work arrangement requests
*Keywords: Tripartite Guidelines on Flexible Work Arrangement Requests, 1 December 2024, FWA, formal process, reasonable business grounds, detrimental to output, unreasonable ground management does not believe in FWA, written decision with reasons, employer prerogative*

**End point:** Since 1 Dec 2024, the Tripartite Guidelines on Flexible Work Arrangement Requests (replacing the 2014 Advisory and 2017 Standard) set a framework. Employers should have a formal process, discuss with the employee, and decide in writing, giving reasons for a rejection. A reasonable ground is that the FWA would harm output. An unreasonable ground is that management does not believe in FWAs. Employers keep the final say.

*Source note: EM2 s7.*

### pcp-m-3-23 — Workplace Fairness Act 2025: scope and discrimination
*Keywords: Workplace Fairness Act 2025, WFA, not yet in force, expected end-2027, protected characteristics, age, nationality, sex, marital status, pregnancy, caregiving responsibilities, race, religion, language, disability, mental health, s 17(1), employment decision adversely affects, one of two or more reasons, exceptions ss 20 to 24, indirect discrimination not covered, numeracy test, benefits not covered, flexible work arrangements not covered, discriminatory direction instruction policy, advertisement, hiring promotion training dismissal retrenchment*

**End point:** The WFA was passed in 2025 and is expected to take effect at end-2027, so it is not yet in force. Protected characteristics:
- age;
- nationality;
- sex, marital status, pregnancy status and caregiving responsibilities;
- race, religion and language;
- disability and mental health.

Discrimination is making an employment decision that adversely affects an individual on the ground of a protected characteristic, or of two or more reasons one of which is a protected characteristic (s 17(1)), subject to the exceptions in ss 20–24. It covers decisions at every stage: hiring, promotion and training, dismissal and retrenchment. It also covers discriminatory directions, instructions or policies (even if no decision follows) and job advertisements naming a protected characteristic. It does not cover indirect discrimination (e.g. a numeracy test that disadvantages older applicants), provision of benefits, or flexible work arrangements.

*Source note: EM2 s8–10; EM-LP p3 ("passed and pending Gazette notification"); WFA 2025 (Act 8 of 2025, Government Gazette 13 Feb 2025). Commencement: the course says "expected end-2027"; an SSO search of current (in-force) Acts on 10 Nov 2026 does not return the WFA, so it has not commenced.*

### pcp-m-3-24 — Workplace Fairness Act 2025: grievances and claims
*Keywords: WFA dispute resolution, internal grievance handling process required, statutory tort of discrimination, mandatory mediation, Employment Claims Tribunal up to $250,000, High Court above $250,000, no legal representation in ECT, lawyers at mediation only if claim above $250,000, private hearings, judge-led*

**End point:** Employers must have an internal grievance-handling process. The WFA creates a statutory tort of discrimination. The path is:
1. raise it through the employer's grievance process;
2. attempt mediation, which is mandatory before a claim;
3. claim in the ECT (up to $250,000) or the High Court (above $250,000).

No lawyers are allowed in the ECT, and at mediation only for claims above $250,000. Both fora sit in private by default and use a judge-led approach.

*Source note: EM2 s11.*

### pcp-m-3-25 — Performance management
*Keywords: performance management, performance improvement plan, PIP, genuine and reasonable, real opportunity to improve, contractual process, documentation, wrongful dismissal, constructive dismissal risk, Prashant Mudgal*

**End point:** Performance management gives the employee a genuine chance to improve, and good records help defend wrongful dismissal claims. Check whether the contract requires a PIP or other process. A PIP must be genuine and reasonable, with a real opportunity to meet the targets, and reviews should be documented. A sham PIP may found constructive dismissal or an IMTC claim ([3-15](#pcp-m-3-15)).

*Source note: EM2 s13; EM1 s21.*

### pcp-m-3-26 — Dismissal for misconduct after due inquiry (s 14(1))
*Keywords: misconduct, s 14(1) EA, due inquiry, dismiss without notice, misconduct inconsistent with express or implied conditions, instantly down-grade, suspend without pay one week, repudiatory breach, wilful disobedience, negligence, incompetence, contractual disciplinary process, cashier theft allegation, SQ Dean*

**End point:** An employer may, after due inquiry, dismiss without notice an employee for misconduct inconsistent with the express or implied conditions of service. Instead of dismissing, it may instantly downgrade him, or suspend him without pay for up to one week (s 14(1)). Misconduct includes conduct amounting to a repudiatory breach, wilful disobedience of lawful and reasonable orders, and negligence or incompetence. Check any contractual disciplinary process too.

SQ p81 Q2 (cashier Dean accused of theft by a co-worker): the manager may lawfully terminate with contractual notice (C). He may not dismiss summarily without inquiry (A). He may not suspend without pay pending investigation, since pay during suspension must be at least half (B). He may not ambush Dean with a disguised meeting (D) (key C).

*Source note: EM2 s14–15; EM-Statutes EA s 14(1); SQ p81 Q2 (key p129: C).*

### pcp-m-3-27 — What a proper due inquiry requires
*Keywords: due inquiry, no prescribed process, informality danger, Long Kim Wing v LTX-Credence, informed of allegations and evidence, opportunity to be heard, Wong Sung Boon v Fuji Xerox, CEO summary dismissal wrongful, $1.4 million damages, formal investigation, no bias, Singapore Recreation Club v Abdul Rashid, ambushed, oppressive questioning*

**End point:** There is no prescribed form, but the more informal the inquiry, the greater the risk it was not proper (*Long Kim Wing v LTX-Credence* [2017] SGHC 151). The employee must:
- be told the allegations and evidence against him;
- have a fair opportunity to be heard and present his defence;
- face decision-makers free of bias.

There should be a formal investigation. Failure can make a summary dismissal wrongful (*Wong Sung Boon v Fuji Xerox* [2021] SGHC 24: a CEO awarded over $1.4m). In *Singapore Recreation Club v Abdul Rashid* [2020] SGHC 156, the GM was "ambushed" at a meeting said to be about other matters and questioned oppressively, so there was no proper inquiry.

*Source note: EM2 s15–17.*

### pcp-m-3-28 — Suspension during the inquiry (s 14(8))
*Keywords: suspension pending inquiry, s 14(8) EA, not exceeding one week, longer with Commissioner's approval, at least half salary, s 14(9) restore full salary if no misconduct, no common law right to suspend without pay*

**End point:** There is no common law right to suspend without pay. For a s 14(1) inquiry, the employer may suspend the employee for up to one week (or longer if the Commissioner approves), but must pay at least half his salary (s 14(8)). If no misconduct is found, the withheld salary must be restored immediately (s 14(9)). Contrast the one-week suspension *without* pay as a punishment after inquiry (s 14(1)(b)).

*Source note: EM2 s18; EM-Statutes EA s 14(8)–(9).*

## 3.VII Termination of employment

- **Terminate with notice: how much notice? Contract says 1 week, employee has 3 years' service** → contract governs; s 10(3) default table → [3-29](#pcp-m-3-29)
- **Pay salary in lieu of notice; must the employer give reasons?** → [3-30](#pcp-m-3-30)
- **Terminate without notice: wilful breach (s 11(2)); absent more than 2 days (s 13(2)); unpaid salary (s 13(1)); common law repudiation** → [3-31](#pcp-m-3-31)
- **Retrenchment / redundancy: any statutory benefit? < 2 years; MOM notification** → [3-32](#pcp-m-3-32)
- **Protected employees: pregnant, on maternity leave, NS, age** → [3-33](#pcp-m-3-33)

### pcp-m-3-29 — Notice periods (s 10)
*Keywords: notice of termination, s 10 EA, same length for both parties, contract term governs, s 10(2), default s 10(3), one day under 26 weeks, one week 26 weeks to 2 years, 2 weeks 2 to 5 years, 4 weeks 5 years or more, notice in writing, waiver, day notice given counts, SQ Christopher*

**End point:** Either party may give notice at any time. The notice must be the same length for both, and is fixed by the contract. Only if the contract is silent does s 10(3) apply (s 10(2)):

| Length of employment | Minimum notice (s 10(3)) |
|---|---|
| Under 26 weeks | 1 day |
| 26 weeks to under 2 years | 1 week |
| 2 years to under 5 years | 2 weeks |
| 5 years or more | 4 weeks |

Notice must be in writing, and the day it is given counts (s 10(5)). Either party may waive notice (s 10(4)). SQ p82 Q5: Christopher (3 years' service; contract says 1 week) is properly terminated on 1 week's notice, because the contract governs (key D).

*Source note: EM-Statutes EA s 10 (in force from 29 May 2022); EM1 s14; SQ p82 Q5 (key p129: D).*

### pcp-m-3-30 — Salary in lieu of notice; reasons
*Keywords: salary in lieu of notice, s 11(1) EA, gross rate of pay, one day's gross pay, terminate without waiting for notice to expire, no obligation to give reasons, burden of proving reason given*

**End point:** Either party may end the contract without notice, or without waiting for notice to expire, by paying salary in lieu at the gross rate for the notice period. For a monthly-rated employee and a notice period under a month, it is one day's gross pay per day (s 11(1)). An employer terminating with notice or pay in lieu need not give reasons. If it does give a reason, it bears the burden of proving it.

*Source note: EM2 s21; EM-Statutes EA s 11(1).*

### pcp-m-3-31 — Termination without notice
*Keywords: summary termination, s 11(2) wilful breach of condition, s 13(1) employer fails to pay salary deemed breach, s 13(2) absent more than 2 days continuously, no prior leave, no reasonable excuse, does not inform, s 14(1) misconduct, common law repudiation, RDC Concrete v Sato Kogyo, four situations, Phosagro Asia v Piattchanine, Uday Mehra v L Capital, redundancy not a ground, SQ Heather*

**End point:** Without notice, a contract may end:
- **s 11(2):** on a wilful breach by the other party of a condition of service;
- **s 13(1):** the employer is deemed to have broken the contract if it fails to pay salary;
- **s 13(2):** the employee is deemed to have broken it if absent for more than 2 days continuously without prior leave, and either has no reasonable excuse or does not inform (or try to inform) the employer;
- **s 14(1):** misconduct after due inquiry;
- **common law:** a repudiatory or fundamental breach (*RDC Concrete v Sato Kogyo* [2007] 4 SLR 413), where the contract allows termination for that breach, a party renounces, a condition is breached, or the breach deprives the innocent party of substantially the whole benefit (*Phosagro Asia v Piattchanine* [2016] 5 SLR 1052; *Uday Mehra v L Capital* [2022] 5 SLR 113).

Redundancy is not a ground for termination without notice (SQ p84 Q10, key C). SQ p83 Q8: Heather, absent 4 days without leave, excuse or notice, is deemed to have broken her contract, so she can be terminated without notice despite the contract's 5-day trigger (key A).

*Source note: EM2 s22–23; EM-Statutes EA ss 11(2), 13; SQ p83 Q8, p84 Q10 (key p129: A, C).*

### pcp-m-3-32 — Retrenchment and redundancy
*Keywords: retrenchment, redundancy, s 45 EA, less than 2 years' service no retrenchment benefit, disentitlement provision, no statutory retrenchment benefit, contractual or collective agreement benefit, Tripartite Advisory on Managing Excess Manpower and Responsible Retrenchment, prevailing norms, mandatory retrenchment notification, MOM, 10 or more employees, 5 working days*

**End point:** Retrenchment is a form of termination, governed by the contract. An employee with less than 2 years' continuous service is not entitled to any retrenchment benefit (s 45). That is a disentitlement only: no one has a statutory right to retrenchment benefits unless the contract or a collective agreement gives one. Consider the Tripartite Advisory on Managing Excess Manpower and Responsible Retrenchment and its norms. An employer with at least 10 employees must notify MOM within 5 working days of telling the employee.

*Source note: EM1 s14; EM2 s24; SQ p81 Q3 (key p129: D).*

### pcp-m-3-33 — Protected employees
*Keywords: protected classes, maternity leave cannot be dismissed, s 81 EA, s 12 CDCSA, pregnant employee 3 months' service, dismissed without sufficient cause s 84, retrenched s 84A, pay maternity benefits, National Service, s 22 Enlistment Act, age, s 8 Retirement and Re-employment Act, SQ Sherry*

**End point:**
- **On maternity leave:** cannot be dismissed (s 81 EA; s 12 CDCSA).
- **Pregnant, with at least 3 months' service:** if dismissed without sufficient cause (s 84) or retrenched (s 84A), the employer must still pay all maternity benefits (s 12 CDCSA).
- **National service:** no dismissal solely because the employee is or may be liable for NS (s 22 Enlistment Act).
- **Age:** no dismissal on grounds of age before the minimum retirement age (s 8 RRA).

SQ p82 Q4: Sherry (5 months pregnant, 2 years' service) can be terminated for redundancy with notice, but must be paid all her maternity benefits (key B). She cannot be dismissed while on maternity leave.

*Source note: EM2 s25; SQ p82 Q4 (key p129: B).*

## 3.VIII Wrongful dismissal and remedies

- **Employee says dismissal was "without just cause or excuse": who can claim (managers, 6 months)? reinstatement or compensation** → s 14(2) EA → [3-34](#pcp-m-3-34)
- **Resigned because the employer stopped giving work: constructive dismissal** → [3-35](#pcp-m-3-35)
- **TADM and ECT: time limit (1 month), claim limits ($20,000 / $30,000), which claims the ECT can hear** → [3-36](#pcp-m-3-36)
- **Sue in court instead: limitation 6 years; damages limited to notice (minimum obligation rule)** → [3-37](#pcp-m-3-37)

### pcp-m-3-34 — Statutory wrongful dismissal claim (s 14(2)–(3) EA)
*Keywords: wrongful dismissal, without just cause or excuse, s 14(2) EA, relevant employee, s 14(2A), manager or executive, 6 months' service if dismissed with notice or salary in lieu, dismissed without notice or pay, non-managerial any service, reinstatement, back wages, compensation, s 14(3), s 13 Employment Claims Act, Tripartite Guidelines on Wrongful Dismissal, deprivation of benefits*

**End point:** A "relevant employee" who considers he was dismissed without just cause or excuse may claim reinstatement or compensation under s 13 ECA (s 14(2)). Relevant employees (s 14(2A)) are:
- (a) a manager or executive dismissed with notice or salary in lieu, after at least 6 months' service;
- (b) a manager or executive dismissed without notice or pay in lieu (any service);
- (c) any non-managerial employee.

If the Tribunal agrees, it may order reinstatement with the wages lost, or compensation in wages (s 14(3)). The Tripartite Guidelines on Wrongful Dismissal guide what is wrongful, including dismissal to deprive the employee of benefits.

*Source note: EM2 s27; EM-Statutes EA s 14(2)–(3).*

### pcp-m-3-35 — Constructive dismissal
*Keywords: constructive dismissal, forced out, employer repudiatory breach, employee treats himself as discharged, dismissal includes constructive dismissal s 2 EA, Cheah Peng Hock v Luzhou Bio-Chem, Wee Kim San Lawrence Bernard v Robinson, no work assigned, resign, SQ Daniel*

**End point:** Constructive dismissal is where the employer's repudiatory breach entitles the employee to treat himself as discharged. Although he resigns, he is treated as dismissed (*Wee Kim San Lawrence Bernard v Robinson & Co* [2014] 4 SLR 357). "Dismissal" in the EA includes constructive dismissal (s 2), and it can be wrongful dismissal (*Cheah Peng Hock v Luzhou Bio-Chem* [2013] 2 SLR 577). So resigning is no bar to a claim. In SQ p83 Q7, Daniel's ECT claim fails only because it is out of time: filed more than 1 month after the dismissal (his resignation), ECA s 3(2)(d) (key C).

*Source note: EM2 s28; SQ p83 Q7 (key p129: C).*

### pcp-m-3-36 — TADM and the Employment Claims Tribunal
*Keywords: Employment Claims Act 2016, ECA, Tripartite Alliance for Dispute Management, TADM, mediation, e-negotiation, Employment Claims Tribunal, ECT, wrongful dismissal limit $20,000, $30,000 union assisted, combined with salary claim $40,000, $60,000, discrimination claims excluded, file within 1 month after last day of employment, pregnancy exception, s 3 ECA, cap claim, WICA claims not in ECT, CDCSA maternity benefits, contractual retrenchment benefits*

**End point:** A claim under the EA or ECA goes first to TADM (e-negotiation, then mediation). If unresolved, it is referred to the ECT. Limits per claim:

| Claim | Limit | Union-assisted |
|---|---|---|
| Wrongful dismissal | $20,000 | $30,000 |
| Combined with a salary-related claim | $40,000 | $60,000 |

Discrimination claims are separate (WFA: [3-24](#pcp-m-3-24)). The mediation request (the first step for every ECT claim) must be submitted (ECA s 3(2)):
- **wrongful dismissal** (EA s 14(2)): within 1 month after the date of dismissal;
- **dismissal during pregnancy or maternity** (EA s 84(2)): within 2 months after the confinement;
- **other disputes once employment has ended** (e.g. salary): within 6 months after the last day of employment;
- **any other dispute** (employment continuing): within 1 year after the material facts. A claimant may abandon the excess over the limit (e.g. a $50,000 claim capped at $20,000). The ECT can hear unpaid CDCSA maternity benefits and contractual retrenchment benefits, but not a work injury compensation claim (SQ p84 Q9, key B).

*Source note: EM2 s29; SQ p84 Q9 (key p129: B). ECA s 3(2) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026). Conflict: EM2 s29 says "within 1 month after last day of employment"; s 3(2)(d) runs from the date of dismissal, which is followed.*

### pcp-m-3-37 — Claims in court; measure of damages
*Keywords: wrongful dismissal in court, above ECA limits, limitation 6 years, higher costs, measure of damages, minimum obligation rule, least onerous obligation, notice period, Alexander Proudfoot Productivity Services v Sim Hua Ngee Alvin, Wee Kim San*

**End point:** An employee may instead sue in court, e.g. to claim more than the ECA limits, within the usual 6-year limitation period. The TADM/ECT route is cheaper. Damages are limited by the "minimum obligation" (least onerous obligation) rule: the employer is assumed to have performed in the way least burdensome to it, typically by giving the contractual notice. So damages are usually the notice pay (*Alexander Proudfoot Productivity Services v Sim Hua Ngee Alvin* [1992] 3 SLR(R) 933; *Wee Kim San Lawrence Bernard v Robinson & Co* [2014] 4 SLR 357).

*Source note: EM2 s30.*

### pcp-m-4-1 — Tort: the four elements
*Keywords: negligence, tort, duty of care, breach of duty, causation, damage, personal injury, property damage, consequential loss, Tan Woo Thian*

**End point:** To succeed in negligence, the claimant must prove:
1. a duty of care (the defendant had to take reasonable steps to prevent harm);
2. breach of that duty;
3. that the breach caused the personal injury, property damage or consequential loss;
4. damage.

Loss must be proved even in a bifurcated trial (*Tan Woo Thian*: see [4-29](#pcp-m-4-29)).

*Source note: PI1 s3; PI4 s8.*

### pcp-m-4-2 — Contract and insurance law in PIPD
*Keywords: contract law, repairs, vehicle owner, repairer, workshop, hospital contract of service, insurance law, insurer and insured, duty of good faith, policyholder, third party claim*

**End point:** Contract law governs the relationships behind a claim: the client, the vehicle owner and the repairers, and an injured claimant and the hospitals. Insurance law governs insurer and insured (including the duty of good faith). Acting for an insurer, you advise on its rights against the policyholder and against third parties claiming on the motor policy.

*Source note: PI1 s4–5; PI-LP p2.*

## 4.II At the scene: immediate steps and the GIA Motor Claims Framework

- **What should a driver do immediately after a collision? Other driver refuses to give particulars** → [4-5](#pcp-m-4-5)
- **Motor Claims Framework: 3 steps; report within 24 hours to a reporting centre or authorised workshop** → [4-6](#pcp-m-4-6)
- **Minor accident, parties want to settle privately: still report? Fail or late to report** → disclaim cover; no-claim discount → [4-7](#pcp-m-4-7)
- **What goes in the Singapore Accident Report? Police report needed?** → [4-8](#pcp-m-4-8)

### pcp-m-4-5 — Immediate steps after a collision
*Keywords: immediate steps, stop vehicle, call police, ambulance, exchange particulars, photographs, do not admit fault, tow truck, insurer's hotline, other driver refuses particulars, licence plate, SQ Annette*

**End point:** At the scene:
1. stop immediately;
2. call the police and/or an ambulance if needed;
3. exchange particulars;
4. take proper photographs;
5. do not admit fault or liability;
6. call a tow truck if needed;
7. call the insurer's hotline for advice.

If the other driver will not give particulars, take down his licence plate number (SQ p88 Q8, key D).

*Source note: PI2 s3, s6; SQ p88 Q8 (key p129: D).*

### pcp-m-4-6 — GIA Motor Claims Framework (MCF)
*Keywords: General Insurance Association, GIA, Motor Claims Framework, MCF, three-step reporting, exchange particulars, vehicle numbers, insurance hotline tow truck, approved reporting centre, authorised workshop, within 24 hours, damaged or not*

**End point:** The MCF sets a common practice for reporting motor accidents in three steps:
1. exchange particulars and note the vehicle numbers;
2. call the insurer's hotline for a tow truck;
3. report, and take the vehicle (damaged or not) to an approved reporting centre or authorised workshop **within 24 hours**.

SQ p85 Q1: bringing the vehicle to a reporting centre or authorised workshop within 24 hours is part of the MCF (key A). Not the nearest workshop, not a police post, and not simply calling a tow truck.

*Source note: PI2 s4–6, s8; SQ p85 Q1 (key p129: A).*

### pcp-m-4-7 — Duty to report; consequences of not reporting
*Keywords: Singapore Accident Report, report regardless of claim, private settlement, failure to report, insurer disclaims coverage, late report beyond 24 hours, no claim discount, NCD reduced, report first decide later, SQ David*

**End point:** The Singapore Accident Report must be made whether or not the motorist wants to claim. Failing to report is enough for the insurer to disclaim its contractual obligation to cover. Reporting after 24 hours may reduce the no-claim discount. Report first and decide later. Even if both sides plan a private settlement, comply with the MCF reporting requirement (SQ p88 Q9, key B).

*Source note: PI2 s8–9; SQ p88 Q9 (key p129: B).*

### pcp-m-4-8 — Content of the accident report; police reports
*Keywords: accident report details, date, time, location, road and weather conditions, visibility, speed, hazard lights, signal indicators, number of impacts, traffic light colour, lanes, injuries, offers made, intoxication, photographs, first account probative value, police report, no injuries, medical leave less than 3 days*

**End point:** Advise the client to be as detailed as possible: date, time, place, road, weather and visibility, speeds, lights and indicators, number of impacts, traffic lights, relative positions, lanes, visible injuries, any offer by the other side, signs of intoxication, and photographs. It is the first account, so it carries high probative weight. A police report is generally not needed if no one is injured, nor if the injured person has under 3 days' medical leave. One may always be made for the record.

*Source note: PI2 s10–11.*

## 4.III At the lawyer's office: claimant's and defendant's options; repudiation; MIB

- **Client is the innocent driver: workshop, lawyer or DIY? Own damage (OD) claim or third-party claim? Private settlement?** → [4-9](#pcp-m-4-9)
- **Client is the driver at fault: private settlement or let insurers handle** → [4-10](#pcp-m-4-10)
- **When can a motor insurer refuse cover (repudiate)? (drunk, invalid licence, admitting fault, not notifying)** → [4-11](#pcp-m-4-11)
- **Defendant uninsured, untraced or insurance void: Motor Insurers' Bureau** → [4-12](#pcp-m-4-12)

### pcp-m-4-9 — Advising a claimant
*Keywords: claimant options, workshop, lawyers, do it yourself, own damage claim, OD claim, no-claim bonus lost, third party claim, other driver's insurer, private settlement, pre-action protocol bypassed*

**End point:** A claimant can claim through a workshop (which may repair and claim for him), through lawyers, or himself. The options:
- **Own damage (OD) claim** on his own policy: fuss-free, since repairers handle it, but he loses the no-claim bonus.
- **Third-party claim** against the other driver's insurer: for repairs or personal injury, through a workshop, lawyer or DIY.
- **Private settlement**, outside the pre-action protocol: at the scene or by a lawyer-drafted agreement.

*Source note: PI2 s12–13.*

### pcp-m-4-10 — Advising a defendant
*Keywords: defendant options, private settlement, no admission of fault, motor policy prohibits admission, preserve no-claim bonus, let insurers handle, insurer appoints lawyers, defend in person*

**End point:** The at-fault driver may:
- **settle privately**, often to keep his no-claim bonus, but nothing in it may amount to an admission of fault, which motor policies usually prohibit;
- **let his insurer handle it**, appointing lawyers if needed;
- **defend in person**.

*Source note: PI2 s14.*

### pcp-m-4-11 — Repudiation by the motor insurer
*Keywords: repudiation, disclaim liability, refuse insurance cover, drink driving, drugs, unregistered vehicle, illegal modifications, invalid driving licence, exceeding seating capacity, admitting fault, failing to notify insurer of accident or proceedings, India International Insurance v Ho Chai Hong Joanne, composition offer accepted, traffic police, SQ*

**End point:** An insurer may refuse to cover the insured or driver (repudiate) for:
- driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs;
- an unregistered vehicle, or prohibited or unapproved modifications;
- driving without a valid licence;
- carrying passengers beyond the legal seating capacity;
- admitting fault or liability;
- failing to notify the insurer of the accident or of any proceedings.

In *India International Insurance v Ho Chai Hong Joanne* [2013] SGDC 193, the insured accepted a traffic police composition offer without telling the insurer. She was in breach, and the insurer could disclaim. SQ p85 Q2: driving without a valid licence (key B). Late reporting, phone use and running a red light are not listed grounds.

*Source note: PI2 s15–18; SQ p85 Q2 (key p129: B).*

### pcp-m-4-12 — Motor Insurers' Bureau (MIB)
*Keywords: Motor Insurers' Bureau, MIB Agreement, uninsured defendant, untraced defendant, unknown driver, void insurance, insurer concerned, apply to MIB directly, Motor Vehicles (Third-Party Risks and Compensation) Act*

**End point:** Where the defendant is untraced, unknown, cannot be found, or has void insurance, the MIB Agreement (with the Motor Vehicles (Third-Party Risks and Compensation) Act) provides compensation, since motor insurance is compulsory. If the defendant is uninsured, the MIB asks his last known motor insurer (the "insurer concerned") to handle the claim. If he is completely unknown, apply to the MIB directly.

*Source note: PI4 s20; PI-LP p2 (MIB under "Repudiation"); Cyril-PI (non-core).*

### pcp-m-4-14 — NIMA: notice of accident and pre-repair survey
*Keywords: notice of accident, 3 working days, pre-repair survey, PRS, insurer reply 2 working days, list of three motor surveyors, deemed agreement, single joint expert surveyor, survey within 2 working days, negotiate repair cost, post-repair inspection, repaired without survey, costs sanctions para 14, SQ Mei*

**End point:**

| Step | Time limit (App B para 2) |
|---|---|
| Claimant sends notice of accident to each potential defendant and insurer | 3 working days from the accident |
| Insurer replies, listing at least 3 motor surveyors if it wants a pre-repair survey | 2 working days from the notice |
| Claimant agrees or objects (silence = agreement); objecting, he proposes 3 | 2 working days |
| Insurer agrees or objects to the claimant's list (silence = agreement) | 2 working days |
| The single joint expert completes the pre-repair survey | 2 working days from appointment |

The parties then negotiate the repair cost. If there is no agreement, the claimant may repair, and the insurer may ask for a post-repair inspection. The survey checks the damage is consistent with the accident and limits repairs to it. A claimant who repairs without a survey and without good reason may face costs sanctions, but the claim can proceed (para 14.1; SQ p86 Q6, key C).

*Source note: PI-PD App B paras 2, 14; PI3 s7; SQ p86 Q6 (key p129: C).*

### pcp-m-4-15 — Letter of claim
*Keywords: letter of claim, every potential defendant and insurer, relevant facts, nature of damage or injuries, estimate of general and special damages, heads of claim, witnesses, police or MOM officer reference, prosecution results, State Coroner's verdict, proposed experts, Magistrate's Court quantum, non-resident claimant departure date, list of documents, accident statement, police sketch plan, photographs, video, medical reports, receipts, income tax notices, repairer's bill, LTA search, excess, COE, PARF, rental, formal apology not required, SQ*

**End point:** The claimant sends a letter of claim to every potential defendant and insurer, setting out (para 3.2):
- (a) the relevant facts;
- (b) the nature of the damage or injuries;
- (c) an estimate of general and special damages by head;
- (d) the names of all witnesses;
- (e) investigation case references and officers (police or MOM);
- (f) results of any prosecution and, if the claimant has died, the State Coroner's verdict.

For Magistrate's Court-value claims, he proposes experts (para 3.3). A non-resident claimant states his departure date (para 3.5). He encloses the documents listed in [4-4](#pcp-m-4-4): accident statements, police reports and sketch, photographs and video, medical reports and bills, income evidence, and for NIMA the repair bill, LTA search, excess, COE and PARF, and rental documents. A formal apology from the defendant is not required (SQ p87 Q7, key D).

*Source note: PI-PD App B paras 3.1–3.6; PI3 s8–10; SQ p87 Q7 (key p129: D).*

### pcp-m-4-16 — Potential defendant's response; joint medical experts
*Keywords: acknowledgement letter 14 days, no acknowledgement commence proceedings without sanction, joint selection of medical experts, Magistrate's Court quantum, letters of appointment 14 days, deemed agreement, single joint expert, claimant pays medical report first, substantive reply 8 weeks, 14 days after vehicle inspection, liability and quantum, offer, accident statements, driver identity*

**End point:**
- **Acknowledgement:** within 14 days of receiving the letter of claim. Without it, the claimant may sue without sanction (paras 4.3–4.4).
- **Medical experts (PI, Magistrate's Court value):** the defendant agrees or objects to the claimant's experts within 14 days of acknowledging. The agreed expert is a single joint expert, appointed by letter within 14 days. Silence counts as agreement. The claimant pays the report first and recovers it as a disbursement (para 4.5).
- **Substantive reply:** within 8 weeks of receiving the letter of claim, or 14 days after inspecting the vehicle if later. It states whether the insurer or the defendant defends, the position on liability and quantum or an offer, reasons and documents, the driver's identity, full accident statements and police reports, survey reports, and the MAG scenario relied on (para 4.6).

*Source note: PI-PD App B paras 4.3–4.6; PI3 s11.*

### pcp-m-4-17 — When the claimant may commence proceedings
*Keywords: commence proceedings, 8 weeks from letter of claim, no reasonable prospect of settlement, 10 clear days' notice, notice of intention to proceed, e-mail fax certificate of posting, names of parties sued, final offer notice, limitation near expiry para 15.2, SQ Rachel*

**End point:** If, after reasonable efforts and at least 8 weeks from receipt of the letter of claim, there is no reasonable prospect of settlement, the claimant may sue after giving **10 clear days' notice** of his intention to the potential defendant and insurer, naming everyone he will sue (para 11.2). No notice is needed if he had already said an offer was final and he would sue if it was not accepted in time. Near a limitation deadline, give as much notice as practicable and consider extending time for service or a stay (para 15.2). SQ p86 Q5 (key C, 10 days); p89 Q10 (key C: let the notice period expire).

*Source note: PI-PD App B paras 11.2, 15.2; PI3 s11; SQ p86 Q5, p89 Q10 (key p129: C, C).*

### pcp-m-4-18 — Non-compliance: sanctions and costs guidelines
*Keywords: non-compliance, costs sanctions, court discretion, good reasons, other ADR attempted, para 15.1, pre-action costs guidelines, NIMA costs $300 to $1,000, PI settlement under $20,000 costs $1,500 to $2,500, claim not dismissed*

**End point:** Non-compliance leads to costs sanctions at the court's discretion, not dismissal. No sanctions are imposed where there are good reasons, such as trying another ADR forum (para 15.1). Pre-action costs guide where settled before action (para 13):

| NIMA sum settled | Costs |
|---|---|
| Under $1,000 | $300 |
| $1,000–$9,999 | $300–$700 |
| $10,000 and above | $500–$1,000 |
| PI settled under $20,000 | $1,500–$2,500 |

*Source note: PI-PD App B paras 13–15; PI3 s11.*

## 4.V FIDReC Non-Injury Motor Accident scheme

- **NIMA claim under $3,000: must it go to FIDReC? Who can use it? Mediation and adjudication fees** → [4-19](#pcp-m-4-19)
- **Exceptions: company claimant, government / foreign / diplomatic vehicle, counterclaim $3,000+, repudiation, fraud; still at FIDReC after 6 months** → [4-20](#pcp-m-4-20)
- **Sued in court instead of FIDReC: sanctions, stay; limitation not stopped** → [4-21](#pcp-m-4-21)

### pcp-m-4-19 — FIDReC: claims below $3,000
*Keywords: FIDReC, Financial Industry Disputes Resolution Centre, NIMA claims below $3,000, lodge at FIDReC first instance, MAS Dispute Resolution Scheme Regulations 2007, banks, insurers, financial advisers, individuals and sole proprietors only, self-represented, mediation free for claimant, insurer pays $50, adjudication fee $250 claimant, $500 insurer, $200 refund, binds financial institution only*

**End point:** NIMA claims below $3,000 must be lodged with FIDReC at first instance (App B para 17). FIDReC is an affordable ADR scheme that financial institutions (banks, insurers, finance companies, advisers, brokers) must join. It is open only to individuals and sole proprietors, who represent themselves. There are two stages:

| Stage | Fees |
|---|---|
| Mediation | Free for the claimant; the insurer pays $50 |
| Adjudication (if mediation fails): a panel adjudicator, written submissions and a hearing | $250 + GST (claimant); $500 + GST (insurer); $200 may be refunded per FIDReC's rules |

An adjudication binds the financial institution only, so the claimant may still go to court if dissatisfied.

*Source note: PI3 s12–13; PI-PD App B para 17; Cyril-PI (binding effect).*

### pcp-m-4-20 — Exceptions to FIDReC
*Keywords: exceptions to FIDReC, para 18 Appendix B, body corporate or partnership claimant, government vehicle, foreign-registered vehicle, diplomatic vehicle, counterclaim $3,000 or more, counterclaim not under own policy, insurer repudiated, fraud, criminal offence police report, ongoing 6 months, good and sufficient reason*

**End point:** The claimant may go directly to court where (para 18):
- the claimant is a body corporate or partnership;
- a government, foreign-registered or diplomatic vehicle is involved;
- there is a counterclaim of $3,000 or more, or one under $3,000 that the claimant is not claiming under his own policy;
- an insurer has repudiated cover;
- the claim, counterclaim or defence is alleged to be tainted by fraud or another crime reported to the police;
- FIDReC proceedings are still going 6 months after all requested documents were submitted or the first interview, whichever is later;
- there is another good and sufficient reason.

*Source note: PI-PD App B para 18; PI3 s14–15.*

### pcp-m-4-21 — Bypassing FIDReC: costs, stay and limitation
*Keywords: costs sanctions para 19, commenced court before FIDReC adjudication, quantum below $3,000 but pleaded above, judgment not better than FIDReC award, good reasons, stay of action para 19.3, Limitation Act not stopped para 20, protective writ*

**End point:** If a claimant who should have used FIDReC sues, the court considers on costs:
- whether he sued before FIDReC adjudicated;
- whether the true quantum was below $3,000 but he pleaded more;
- whether his judgment was no better than the FIDReC award (para 19.1).

There are no sanctions for good reasons (para 19.2), and the court may stay the action so he can go to FIDReC (para 19.3). Lodging at FIDReC does not stop limitation running, so a claimant may sue to protect against the time bar, and the court may then stay the action (para 20).

*Source note: PI-PD App B paras 19–20; PI3 s15.*

## 4.VI Other pre-writ issues: multiple collisions, limitation, one action

- **Chain collision: whose insurer pays the NIMA claim?** → the vehicle directly behind → [4-22](#pcp-m-4-22)
- **Limitation: personal injury 3 years; property damage 6 years; date of knowledge (s 24A)** → [4-23](#pcp-m-4-23)
- **Separate suits for repairs, injury and excess?** → one cause of action (*Ng Koon Choon*) → [4-24](#pcp-m-4-24)

### pcp-m-4-22 — Multiple (chain) collisions
*Keywords: multiple collision, chain collision, multi-vehicle accident, market agreement, insurer of vehicle directly behind claimant pays, no contribution sought, NIMA, SQ*

**End point:** In a chain collision with a NIMA claim, by a market agreement among insurers, the motor insurer of the vehicle directly behind the claimant pays and settles the NIMA claim. It does not seek contribution from the drivers or insurers further behind. The agreement binds insurers, not claimants (SQ p86 Q4, key A).

*Source note: PI3 s16; Cyril-PI (binds insurers); SQ p86 Q4 (key p129: A).*

### pcp-m-4-23 — Limitation periods (Limitation Act s 24A)
*Keywords: limitation period, personal injury 3 years, PIMA, property damage 6 years, NIMA, s 24A Limitation Act, date of knowledge, latent damage, 3 years from knowledge, accrual, negligence nuisance breach of duty*

**End point:** For negligence, nuisance or breach of duty:
- **Personal injury:** 3 years from accrual, or 3 years from the claimant's date of knowledge if later (s 24A(2)).
- **Other damage** (e.g. NIMA property damage): 6 years from accrual, or 3 years from knowledge if later (s 24A(3)).

| Claim | Limitation |
|---|---|
| PI / PIMA | 3 years |
| NIMA (vehicle damage) | 6 years |

*Source note: PI3 s16; PI-Statutes Limitation Act s 24A(2)–(3) (as amended by Act 25 of 2021 wef 1 Apr 2022); LA s 24A(2)–(3) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026).*

### pcp-m-4-24 — One accident, one action (Ng Koon Choon)
*Keywords: one cause of action, Ng Koon Choon v Tang Wee Goh, Ng Kong Choon, separate suits, NIMA, personal injury, insurance excess, abuse of process, splitting cause of action, single action*

**End point:** A claimant filed three suits for one accident (NIMA repairs, personal injury, insurance excess). There is only one cause of action, with different reliefs, so all claims must be brought in a single action. Splitting it into several suits is not allowed (*Ng Koon Choon v Tang Wee Goh* [2016] 3 SLR 935).

*Source note: PI3 s17; PI-LP p4. Note: PI-LP spells the case "Ng Kong Choon", PI3 "Ng Koon Choon"; both are given for searching.*

## 4.VII Where to sue and how the courts manage PIPD cases

- **Which court? High Court PI claims transferred automatically to the District Court (2016 Transfer Order)** → [4-25](#pcp-m-4-25)
- **High Court v State Courts: PTC, bifurcation, AR below $1m; CDR, early neutral evaluation, ADCDR, Quantum Indication Form** → [4-26](#pcp-m-4-26)
- **Claim grows above the District Court limit: enhanced jurisdiction by memorandum (s 23 SCA)** → [4-27](#pcp-m-4-27)
- **Single joint expert (O 12 r 3 ROC 2021); simplified trial** → [4-28](#pcp-m-4-28)

### pcp-m-4-25 — Deciding where to sue; the 2016 Transfer Order
*Keywords: where to commence, Supreme Court of Judicature (Transfer of Specified Proceedings to District Court) Order 2016, automatic transfer, High Court to District Court, claims $250,000 to $500,000, same rules as District Court cases, appeal structure, unique to PIPD*

**End point:** Under the Supreme Court of Judicature (Transfer of Specified Proceedings to District Court) Order 2016, specified PI actions started in the High Court are transferred automatically to the District Court. They then follow the same statutes, Rules of Court, Practice Directions, hearings and appeal structure as District Court cases. This is unique to PIPD work. It covers (a) any action arising out of a road accident involving a collision or apprehended collision with a motor vehicle (with or without a personal injury claim), and (b) any personal injury action arising out of an industrial accident, where the amount claimed does not exceed $500,000 (para 2A); the District Court has jurisdiction although that exceeds its ordinary $250,000 limit (para 3). So such claims between $250,000 and $500,000 are filed in the High Court and transferred down.

*Source note: PI4 s4; PI-LP p4; Cyril-PI ("$250,000 and $500,000"). Court limits: Magistrate's Court $60,000, District Court $250,000 (SCA s 2 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026)). Transfer Order paras 2A, 3 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026) (as amended by S 1079/2020 wef 2 Jan 2021).*

### pcp-m-4-26 — High Court and State Courts case management
*Keywords: High Court, pre-trial conference, case conference, judge PTC, bifurcated trial, liability then quantum, assessment of damages, Assistant Registrar below $1 million, judge above $1 million, no indication on settlement, private mediation, SMC, State Courts, Court Dispute Resolution, CDR, asynchronous, Appendix C, early neutral evaluation, percentage of fault, interlocutory judgment, ADCDR, Quantum Indication Form, QIF, free of charge, assessment of damages hearing*

**End point:**

| High Court | State Courts |
|---|---|
| PTCs / case conferences; a Judge PTC sets trial dates and issues | Mandatory Court Dispute Resolution (CDR), often asynchronous (State Courts PD 2021 App C) |
| Trials usually bifurcated: liability, then assessment of damages | The CDR judge gives an early neutral evaluation of fault (a percentage) |
| AD under $1m by an Assistant Registrar; above $1m by a judge | After interlocutory judgment or settlement on liability: ADCDR, with a Quantum Indication Form and an indicated fair figure by head |
| No court indication on settlement, so consider private or SMC mediation | If no settlement, an AD hearing is fixed. CDR and ADCDR are free |

*Source note: PI4 s5–6; Cyril-PI.*

### pcp-m-4-27 — Enhanced jurisdiction by memorandum (s 23 SCA)
*Keywords: enhanced jurisdiction, s 23(a) State Courts Act, memorandum signed by parties or lawyers, District Court hears claim exceeding limit, injuries worsen, quantum increases, High Court case transferred to State Courts for CDR*

**End point:** If the quantum grows beyond the District Court limit during the case (e.g. complications from the injuries), the parties may sign a memorandum (themselves or by their lawyers). The District Court then has jurisdiction although the claim exceeds its limit (s 23(a) State Courts Act). High Court parties can also use this to move to the State Courts and use CDR and ADCDR.

*Source note: PI4 s7; Cyril-PI; SCA s 23 (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026) (the lecture's "s 23(a)" is s 23, limb (a) being a claim exceeding the District Court limit).*

### pcp-m-4-28 — Single joint experts
*Keywords: single joint expert, SJE, Order 12 rule 3 Rules of Court 2021, High Court one common expert, not more than one expert per issue, simplified trial, court may appoint, CDR, case conference, single application pending trial, pre-action SJE, pre-repair survey, Lee Song Yam v Hafizah binte Abdul Rahman*

**End point:** Parties should have one common expert (O 12 r 3 ROC 2021).
- **High Court:** they must agree one common expert and may not rely on more than one expert per issue.
- **State Courts simplified trials:** they must appoint an independent expert; if they cannot agree, the court may appoint one.

This is usually dealt with at a CDR or case conference, or by a Single Application Pending Trial. The protocol also uses SJEs pre-action (pre-repair surveyors, medical experts). *Lee Song Yam v Hafizah binte Abdul Rahman* [2019] SGMC 24 is optional reading on SJEs.

*Source note: PI4 s14; PI-PD App B paras 2.7, 2.8, 4.5.*

## 4.VIII Trials and assessment of damages

- **Bifurcated trial: must loss be proved at the liability stage?** → yes (*Tan Woo Thian*) → [4-29](#pcp-m-4-29)
- **Consent interlocutory judgment reserving causation; disputed injuries** → *Salmizan v Crapper* (HC) reversed by *Crapper v Salmizan* (CA) → [4-30](#pcp-m-4-30)

### pcp-m-4-29 — Proving loss (Tan Woo Thian)
*Keywords: Tan Woo Thian v PricewaterhouseCoopers Advisory Services, bifurcated trial, liability and quantum, proof of loss, damage element of negligence, claim dismissed, consent judgment reserving causation*

**End point:** In negligence the claimant must prove duty, breach and loss. A bifurcated trial does not excuse proof of loss at the liability stage; the claim was dismissed for want of proof of loss (*Tan Woo Thian v PricewaterhouseCoopers Advisory Services* [2021] 1 SLR 1166). After it, insurers' lawyers commonly add a reservation of causation to consent interlocutory judgments.

*Source note: PI4 s8–9.*

### pcp-m-4-30 — Consent interlocutory judgment reserving causation (Salmizan; Crapper)
*Keywords: consent interlocutory judgment, reserve causation, Salmizan bin Abdullah v Crapper Ian Anthony 2023 SGHC 75, Crapper Ian Anthony v Salmizan bin Abdullah 2024 SGCA 21, disputed injuries, assessment of damages, issue-based judgment, duty and breach only, standard wording, percentage, registrar assessing damages*

**End point:**
- **High Court** (*Salmizan v Crapper* [2023] SGHC 75): once interlocutory judgment is entered, causation of disputed injuries cannot be raised at AD, so it must be dealt with at the liability stage.
- **Court of Appeal** (*Crapper v Salmizan* [2024] SGCA 21): parties may enter a consent interlocutory judgment reserving causation. Liability need not be fully established first. A determination of duty and breach alone is still a proper (issue-based) judgment.

State Courts wording: "By consent an interlocutory judgment is entered for the claimant against the defendant at [x]% of the damages to be assessed, leaving the issues of causation, damages, interest and costs reserved to the registrar assessing the damages."

*Source note: PI4 s10–13.*

## 4.IX Post-judgment and other matters: Public Trustee, insurers, work injury

- **Settlement or judgment sum: paid to the lawyer or the claimant? Public Trustee approval of costs (s 18(3) MVA); PT Circular 1/2014 (over $5,000)** → [4-31](#pcp-m-4-31)
- **Insurer has repudiated cover: can it join the action?** → intervene with leave → [4-32](#pcp-m-4-32)
- **Workman injured at work: WICA or sue at common law?** → [4-33](#pcp-m-4-33)

### pcp-m-4-31 — Usual consequential orders and the Public Trustee
*Keywords: usual consequential orders, UCO, Motor Vehicles (Third-Party Risks and Compensation) Act s 18(3), Public Trustee approves solicitor-and-client costs, assessed costs, 3 months to begin assessment, settlement paid directly to claimant, only solicitor and client costs to lawyer, Form 7 Appendix A1 State Courts PD 2021, Public Trustee Circular 1 of 2014, two payments, damages above $5,000*

**End point:** Under s 18(3) MVA, a lawyer acting in such matters may receive only:
- costs agreed with the Public Trustee;
- assessed costs, if there is no agreement; or
- costs the Public Trustee determines, if the lawyer does not begin assessment within 3 months.

The settlement or judgment sum is paid directly to the claimant; only the solicitor-and-client costs go to the lawyer. This prevents absconding and overcharging. The usual consequential order wording is in Form 7, App A1 of the State Courts PD 2021. Under Public Trustee Circular 1 of 2014, the insurer pays two cheques, one to the claimant and one (costs) to the lawyer. The Public Trustee process applies only where the judgment or settlement sum (excluding costs and interest) exceeds the "relevant amount", $5,000 (s 18(5); s 2). Breach of s 18 is an offence, and any other agreement to pay for such work is void (s 18(7)–(8)).

*Source note: PI4 s15–17; MVA ss 2 ("relevant amount"), 18(3), (5), (7), (8) (SSO, current at 10 Nov 2026).*

### pcp-m-4-32 — Intervention by insurers
*Keywords: insurer intervene, leave of court, repudiation, refuses indemnity, financial interest, Rules of Court intervention*

**End point:** Where an insurer refuses to indemnify its policyholder or driver for breach of the policy, its financial interest is still affected. It commonly applies for the court's permission to intervene and take part in the claim, under the Rules of Court provisions on intervention.

*Source note: PI4 s18.*

### pcp-m-4-33 — Work injury: WICA or common law
*Keywords: work injury compensation, WICA, workman injured in course of employment, compensation scheme, right to sue employer at common law, election, State Courts CDR special provisions*

**End point:** A workman injured in the course of employment may claim under the work injury compensation scheme (WICA), or sue the employer at common law in court. In the State Courts, CDR has special provisions for these cases. See also the Employment Subject on WICA ([3-13](#pcp-m-3-13)).

*Source note: PI4 s19.*

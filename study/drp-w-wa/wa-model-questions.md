## 1 · Written Advocacy

### 1.I Approach and writing principles

**Q1-1** · `1.I.1` · PYP 2023 Q5 (planning stage) · 6 minutes · `drp-w-1-1`

*Sub-point 1.I.1: reading the drafting task; mapping limbs and elements to facts.*

You have the 2023 Question 5 paper in front of you (the facts are set out at Q1-19).
Spend 6 minutes on the plan only:

- (a) what exactly must you draft, and what must you not draft;
- (b) the cause(s) of action and their elements, each with the fact and annex that proves it;
- (c) the reliefs;
- (d) the traps in the facts.

**Answer.**

**(a) The task.** A Statement of Claim only: "There is no need to draft the Originating
Claim" (PYP23 p6 ¶2). It must conform to ROC 2021 and SCPD 2021, and plead "only material
facts (and not evidence)" (¶3). It is a draft for the partner, then the client (p8). There
is no page limit, but it must "properly and concisely" set out the claims and reliefs (p8).

**(b) The map.**

| Element | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Parties | LCPL, Singapore company (UEN 2002000321J), 789B Mechanics Lane; repairs and servicing of luxury cars. Ho, Singapore citizen, 333 Bukit Panjang Road #11-11 | email ¶4.1–4.2; Annex A |
| Contract | Employment contract dated 1 July 2020; head mechanic; cl 13.1 confidentiality (customer contact details expressly "information": cl 13.2); survives termination (cl 13.3); Singapore law (cl 18) | Annex B |
| Breach | 7 Aug 2023, 11:33pm: Ho emailed the customer list (names, emails, phone numbers) from his work account to his personal Gmail, without consent | Annex C; email ¶4.6 |
| Termination | Resigned 1 July 2023; notice period to 31 Aug 2023 | email ¶4.4 |
| Continuing risk | Joined competitor Xcellant Repairers (XRPL) as chief mechanic, 1 Sept 2023; still holds the list | email ¶4.5 |
| Loss | Business affected; amount unknown | email ¶4.8 |
| Demand | Letter of demand and offer of amicable resolution, 12 Nov 2023 (Just Hantam LLC), rejected | ¶5; email ¶2 |

Alternative cause: breach of confidence in equity. The list has the quality of confidence
and was taken without consent (*I-Admin* at [61]; drp-w-1-21).

**(c) Reliefs:** damages to be assessed; an injunction restraining use or disclosure;
delivery up or destruction of all copies; interest; costs; further relief (drp-w-1-20).

**(d) Traps:**

- **Evidence:** the IT department's recovery tools; the social media pages; revenue for
  FY2021 and FY2022. These are evidence or quantum, not material facts (drp-w-1-19).
- **No claim against XRPL:** the client said so (¶4.7).
- **Interim injunction:** asked about (¶7), but it's a separate application, not part of
  the SOC.
- **Who signs:** Constance Young, because the CEO is away (¶6).
- **The O 5 offer:** don't mention its terms.

*Cite: PYP23 p6–13; drp-w-1-1, drp-w-1-19 to drp-w-1-21.*

**Q1-2** · `1.I.2` · New · 8 minutes · `drp-w-1-2`

*Sub-point 1.I.2: principles of good and legal writing.*

Rewrite this opening of a letter to the other side's solicitors. Keep the substance; apply
the SN2 rules of style and structure.

> "We refer to the abovementioned matter and to your good self's letter of 3 March 2026 which we have perused with considerable care and in respect of which we have taken our client's instructions. In the circumstances, and having regard to the foregoing, it is the case that our client is of the view that the sum of seven (7) thousand dollars hereto claimed by your client is not owed by our client on account of the fact that the goods were in fact delivered late, being delivered on 20 February 2026 when they ought to have been delivered by 10 February 2026."

**Answer.**

```text
1.  We refer to your letter dated 3 March 2026.
2.  Our client does not owe the S$7,000 your client claims. The goods were
    due by 10 February 2026 but were delivered only on 20 February 2026.
```

Marker's notes:

- **Lead with the point:** the reader's question is "does he pay?" (SN2 ¶37; primacy).
- **Cut words that do no work:** "abovementioned", "perused", "in the circumstances",
  "having regard to the foregoing", "hereto", "it is the case that".
- **Numbers in figures:** S$7,000, not "seven (7) thousand dollars".
- **One idea per paragraph**, in the active voice.
- **Not "your good self's".**

*Cite: SN2 ¶37; SN1 D–F; Cyril-WAN ("Legal writing comments"); drp-w-1-2.*

**Q1-3** · `1.I.3` · New · 6 minutes · `drp-w-1-3`

*Sub-point 1.I.3: logic and reasoning, fallacies to avoid.*

Name the fallacy in each sentence of this draft submission, and say how to fix it.

1. "The Defendant's director has been sued before by another supplier, so his evidence
   should not be believed."
2. "Either the Defendant pays the full sum now, or it is admitting that it never intended
   to pay."
3. "The Defendant has no defence, because nothing it says amounts to a defence."
4. "If the Court allows this late defence, no deadline under the Rules will ever be
   respected again."

**Answer.**

1. **Ad hominem / poisoning the well.** It attacks the person, not the evidence. Fix:
   point to the specific contemporaneous documents his evidence contradicts.
2. **False alternatives.** There are other possibilities, e.g. a genuine dispute on quality.
   Fix: argue why *this* defence is not genuine.
3. **Circular reasoning.** The conclusion is used as the reason. Fix: give the reason, e.g.
   the Defence is a bare non-admission with no facts or particulars (drp-w-1-26).
4. **Slippery slope.** Fix: argue the prejudice on these facts (length of delay, reasons,
   prejudice: drp-w-1-17).

*Cite: SN5 ¶4, B–E; drp-w-1-3.*

**Q1-30** · `1.I.3` · New · 8 minutes · `drp-w-1-3`

*Sub-point 1.I.3: logic and reasoning, the SN5 fallacies Q1-3 does not test.*

The other side's written submissions in a claim for unpaid invoices contain these sentences.
Name the fallacy in each, and write the one-line reply you would put in your own submissions.

1. "The Claimant cannot prove that the Defendant never received the replacement parts, so
   the Defendant must be taken to have received them and set off their value."
2. "The Defendant did not refuse to pay the invoices; it merely deferred payment
   indefinitely."
3. "It is standard practice in the printing industry for customers to pay 90 days late, so
   the Defendant was not in breach."
4. "The Claimant's sales fell in the month after the Defendant's new contract began, so the
   Defendant caused the Claimant's losses."
5. "The Defendant has paid late on the last four invoices, so it is bound to pay this one on
   time and summary judgment is unnecessary."
6. "Each of the 12 deliveries was only a day or two late, so the supply contract as a whole
   was performed on time."
7. "The Claimant is a reputable listed company, so each of its employees' statements in the
   affidavits must be accurate."
8. "If the goods had been defective, the Defendant could have rejected them. The goods were
   not defective. So the Defendant had no right to terminate."

**Answer.**

| # | Fallacy (SN5) | Reply |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Negative proof** (¶21–22) | The Defendant asserts the set-off and bears the onus of proving it; the Claimant's inability to disprove receipt is not proof of receipt |
| 2 | **Distinction without a difference** (¶12–14) | Deferring payment indefinitely after the due date is, in substance, refusing to pay: the invoices remain unpaid |
| 3 | **Ad populum** (¶25–26) | What others in the industry do is irrelevant; the contract's 30-day term governs |
| 4 | **False cause** (¶33–34) | Correlation is not causation; the Defendant points to no act of the Claimant that caused the fall |
| 5 | **Gambler's fallacy** (¶31–32) | Past late payments say nothing about whether this invoice will be paid; it is due now and unpaid |
| 6 | **Composition** (¶37–38) | What is true of each delivery is not true of the whole: 12 late deliveries are 12 breaches of the delivery dates |
| 7 | **Division** (¶39–40) | The company's standing says nothing about the accuracy of any one employee's evidence; test each against the documents |
| 8 | **Denying the antecedent** (¶44–46) | "If A then B; not A; so not B" is invalid: the Defendant may have had other grounds to terminate (e.g. repeated late delivery) |

Marker's notes:

- One mark for the name and one for a reply that answers **this** argument, not a general
  definition.
- Reply 1 uses DOTER's **Onus** (drp-w-1-2): the strongest answer to negative proof.
- Keep the reply courteous: exposing a fallacy is not an attack on counsel (ad hominem;
  PCR r 7(2)).

*Cite: SN5 ¶2–4, ¶12–14, ¶21–22, ¶25–26, ¶31–34, ¶37–40, ¶44–46; drp-w-1-3.*

**Q1-31** · `1.I.2` · New · 8 minutes · `drp-w-1-29`, `drp-w-1-2`

*Sub-point 1.I.2: usage, composition and style (SN1 D–F).*

Correct each sentence from a draft affidavit and statement of claim, and give the SN1 rule.

1. "The Claimant delivered the goods, the Defendant did not pay."
2. "The Defendant's director, Mr. Tan signed the contract on behalf of the company and it's
   board."
3. "Each of the Defendants were aware of the confidentiality clause."
4. "The Defendant blatantly and deliberately ignored our client's very reasonable requests."
5. "The Claimant would say that the Defendant may have been somewhat late in paying."
6. "The Defendant was not very prompt. The goods were analyzed and found to be of poor
   colour."
7. "On 3 March the Defendant emails the Claimant and on 4 March it refused to pay."

**Answer.**

1. "The Claimant delivered the goods; the Defendant did not pay." No comma splice: join
   independent clauses with a semicolon, or a conjunction and a comma (SN1 ¶42–43).
2. "The Defendant's director, Mr Tan, signed the contract on behalf of the company and its
   board." Parenthetic name between two commas (¶33); "its" is the possessive (¶26, ¶28);
   "Mr" with no full stop (¶63).
3. "Each of the Defendants **was** aware …" *Each* takes a singular verb (¶54).
4. "The Defendant did not reply to the Claimant's letters of 1, 2 and 5 September 2025."
   State facts, not opinion: adverbs carry opinion, qualifiers weaken (¶93(a), (f), (g));
   specific and concrete (¶83).
5. "The Defendant paid on [DATE], [N] days after the due date of [DATE]." Use *would, may*
   only to convey uncertainty (¶82); no qualifiers (¶93(g)).
6. "The Defendant paid 30 days late. The goods were analysed and found to be discoloured."
   Positive form, not negative (¶79–80); British spelling, consistently (¶58–65).
7. "On 3 March 2026 the Defendant emailed the Claimant, and on 4 March 2026 it refused to
   pay." One tense in a narrative (¶87–88); comma before the conjunction joining
   independent clauses (¶40); full dates (house style).

Marker's notes:

- Spelling alone is not penalised in the exam (OB 49), but sentences 1–3 change or blur
  meaning, and 4–5 put argument and opinion into a document of fact (drp-w-1-10).

*Cite: SN1 ¶26–28, ¶33, ¶40–43, ¶54, ¶58–65, ¶79–88, ¶93; drp-w-1-29; drp-w-1-2.*

**Q1-32** · `1.I.2` · New (WE facts) · 8 minutes · `drp-w-1-2`

*Sub-point 1.I.2: case analysis, good / bad / neutral facts, case theory (SN2 ¶22–28).*

On the Worked Examples facts (Mr Tang lent Mr Chan S$90,000 by WhysApp on 1 April 2025,
transferred 2 April, repayable "maybe by end August"; Mr Chan replied "Ya", "OK" and "Sorry,
I just need some time" to reminders from 31 August to 5 September 2025), Mr Tang also tells
you: "He's a crook: he did the same to his last business partner. I'm sure he's spent it on
his gambling. There was no written loan agreement."

- (a) Sort the material into fact, opinion, assumption, conjecture and inference.
- (b) Classify the good, bad and neutral facts, and say how you deal with the bad one.
- (c) State the case theory in two sentences for the opening of the summary judgment
  submissions.

**Answer.**

**(a)** Facts: the messages, the transfer, the dates. Opinion: "he's a crook". Conjecture:
"spent it on his gambling"; "did the same to his last business partner" is untested
hearsay. Inference: the requests for time acknowledge the debt (WE [19] ¶9). Assumption:
that "maybe by end August" fixed a due date of 31 August 2025; test it, because it is the
point the Defendant could take.

**(b)** Good: "$90k received, thank you"; repeated requests for time; no denial; no reply to
the letter of demand. Neutral: they were friends; Mr Chan's business. Bad: no signed loan
agreement, and "maybe by end August" is loose. Address it, don't hide it: the WhysApp
exchange is the written agreement (WE [21] ¶15), and the reply "Ya" to the 31 August
reminder accepts that date. Leave out the opinion and conjecture: they are not evidence
and would cost ethos (SN1 ¶93(a); SN2 ¶35).

**(c)** "This is a straightforward claim for a loan of S$90,000 that the Defendant
acknowledged receiving and agreed to repay by 31 August 2025. He has never denied the debt;
he has only asked for more time, and his Defence is a bare non-admission."

Marker's notes:

- The SN2 ¶22(d) distinction is the step most candidates skip.
- A Bad Fact must be met (SN2 ¶24); an opening that ignores the missing written agreement
  invites the reply.

*Cite: SN2 ¶22–28, ¶35; SN1 ¶93; WE Annex 1(1), Annex 2(4) ¶1, ¶9, ¶15; drp-w-1-2.*

**Q1-33** · `1.I.4` · New (WE facts) · 15 minutes · `drp-w-1-30`

*Sub-point 1.I.4: opinion or advice to a client.*

Mr Tang (WE facts, Q1-32) asks for short written advice before he decides whether to sue
Mr Chan for the S$90,000. Your partner wants a letter to Mr Tang, not a memo. Draft it. You
have reviewed the WhysApp messages of 1 April to 5 September 2025 and the OOBC transfer
screenshot of 2 April 2025. Today is 15 September 2025; no letter of demand has been sent.

**Answer.**

```text
PRIVATE AND CONFIDENTIAL
15 September 2025
Mr Tang Lee Ming
74B Hibiscus Lane, Singapore 323074

Dear Mr Tang,

ADVICE ON RECOVERING YOUR S$90,000 LOAN TO MR CHAN LONG CHUENG

A. SUMMARY
1.  You have a strong claim against Mr Chan for S$90,000. He agreed in
    writing to repay by the end of August 2025, confirmed he received the
    money, and has since only asked for more time. We recommend a letter of
    demand and a written offer to settle now, and a claim in the District
    Court if he does not pay within 14 days.

B. WHAT WE HAVE RELIED ON
2.  We have reviewed: (a) your WhysApp messages with Mr Chan from 1 April
    to 5 September 2025; and (b) the OOBC transfer screenshot of
    2 April 2025. This advice is based on those documents and on what you
    told us on [DATE]. It may change if further information or documents
    come to light, so please tell us of any other dealings with Mr Chan
    about this money.

C. THE FACTS
3.  On 1 April 2025 Mr Chan asked to borrow S$90,000 and said he would repay
    "maybe by end August". You agreed. On 2 April 2025 you transferred
    S$90,000 to his PPSB account, and he replied "$90k received, thank you".
4.  Since 31 August 2025 you have reminded him repeatedly. He has not paid.
    He has not denied the debt; he has asked for more time.

D. THE QUESTIONS
5.  You asked: (a) can you recover the S$90,000; and (b) what are the next
    steps?

E. OUR ADVICE
Can you recover the money?
6.  Yes, in our view. The messages are a written record of the loan and
    its terms, and his "thank you" and later requests for time show he
    accepts he owes it. The weak point is the words "maybe by end August",
    which he could argue left the date open. But he answered "Ya" to your
    31 August reminder, which supports that date.
7.  The court can also award you interest (usually at 5.33% a year) and part
    of your legal costs.

What happens next?
8.  The court rules require you to try to settle before suing. So we will
    send (a) a formal letter demanding payment within 14 days, and (b) a
    separate written offer to settle, for example S$87,500 in up to three
    instalments by the end of September if you wish to offer a discount.
9.  If he does not pay, we can start a claim. If his defence only says he
    "does not admit" the claim, we can apply for judgment without a full
    trial (summary judgment).

F. WHAT WE NEED FROM YOU
10. Please confirm (a) whether you wish to offer a discount or instalments,
    and on what terms; and (b) your bank details for payment.

Yours sincerely,
[NAME]
Legal Excellence LLP
```

Marker's notes:

- **Executive summary first** (SN2 ¶14): the answer and the recommendation.
- **Documents relied on, and the caveat** that the advice may change (SN2 ¶11).
- **Facts and issues at the outset**, succinctly (SN2 ¶12); headings throughout (¶13).
- **No legalese** (SN2 ¶9–10): "judgment without a full trial (summary judgment)", not "O 9
  r 17 application for want of a bona fide defence"; no case names for a lay client.
- **The Bad Fact is addressed** ("maybe by end August": SN2 ¶24).
- Facts match the WE (Annex 1(1): the S$87,500, 3 instalments, end September offer).
  Interest at 5.33% under CLA s 12 is in the court's discretion (drp-w-1-20); the O 5
  offer before commencing is drp-w-1-6.
- **Not** included: Mr Tang's views on Mr Chan's character (opinion, conjecture: Q1-32).

*Cite: SN2 ¶9–15, ¶24; WE Annex 1(1); drp-w-1-30, drp-w-1-6, drp-w-1-20, drp-w-1-26.*

### 1.II Correspondence

**Q1-4** · `1.II.1` · PYP 2024 (SQ p49–55) · 30 marks with Q1-7 (marks not printed) · `drp-w-1-4`, `drp-w-1-5`, `drp-w-1-8`

*Sub-point 1.II.1: letter of demand, structure and contents.*

**Facts (condensed from SQ p49–55):**

- You are an associate at Xtra Legal LLC (XL), acting for Yellow Flame Kindergarten Pte Ltd
  (YFK).
- YFK leased 99 Waterlogged Way, Singapore 220099 from Mrs Patience Noh under a tenancy
  agreement dated 1 August 2019 (TA): term 1 October 2019 to 30 September 2024; rent
  S$20,000 a month; security deposit S$70,000, paid 1 August 2019.
- The deposit is refundable within 14 calendar days after the joint inspection (cl 3.2).
  Clause 19: after a joint inspection the tenant delivers the premises, and "the Landlord
  shall not have any claim against the Tenant in respect of any damage to the Premises other
  than for the damage ascertained at the joint inspection".
- The joint inspection took place on 28 September 2024, 10:30am to 12:00 noon, attended by
  YFK's director Mrs Terri Kate Hu and operations manager Ms Rosalind Sim. Mrs Noh raised no
  complaints and took back the keys.
- At 6:53pm that day she sent Ms Sim a WhatsApp: she does not accept the handover, wants
  reinstatement works, and will not return the deposit.
- She has ignored calls for 4 weeks.
- YFK's bank: Assured Reliance Bank, account 001-7777-666, account name Yellow Flame
  Kindergarten Pte Ltd.
- Mrs Noh's address: 82 Hollow Way, Singapore 280082; email nohpatience@pmail.com.

Prepare the letter of demand for the return of the security deposit. (Q1-6 deals with the
client's further wishes; Q1-7 with the Order 5 offer.)

**Answer.**

*Plan:* the contract and the deposit; cl 3.2 (due date) and cl 19 (no claim except damage
found at the inspection); the inspection and handover; the due date passed (12 October
2024); non-payment; the demand with a deadline and bank details; the consequence.

```text
XTRA LEGAL LLC
Our Ref: [ ]          Your Ref: To be advised
[DATE]
By Registered Post & Email (nohpatience@pmail.com)
Mrs Patience Noh
82 Hollow Way
Singapore 280082

Dear Madam,

CLAIM FOR RETURN OF SECURITY DEPOSIT OF S$70,000.00
99 WATERLOGGED WAY, SINGAPORE 220099

1.  We act for Yellow Flame Kindergarten Pte Ltd ("our client").
2.  Our client's instructions are that:
    (a) by a tenancy agreement dated 1 August 2019 (the "TA"), you let the
        premises at 99 Waterlogged Way, Singapore 220099 (the "Premises") to
        our client from 1 October 2019 to 30 September 2024 at a monthly rent
        of S$20,000.00;
    (b) our client paid you a security deposit of S$70,000.00 under the TA;
    (c) on 28 September 2024, from 10:30am to 12:00 noon, our client's
        Mrs Terri Kate Hu and Ms Rosalind Sim conducted a joint inspection
        of the Premises with you. You raised no issue on the condition of the
        Premises; and
    (d) at the end of the joint inspection our client delivered the Premises
        to you by handing over the keys, which you accepted.
3.  Clause 19 of the TA provides that, after the joint inspection and
    delivery, "the Landlord shall not have any claim against the Tenant in
    respect of any damage to the Premises other than for the damage
    ascertained at the joint inspection". No damage was ascertained.
4.  Under clause 3.2 of the TA, you were to return the security deposit
    within 14 calendar days after the joint inspection, that is, by
    12 October 2024. In breach of clause 3.2, you have failed, refused
    and/or neglected to do so.
5.  WE HEREBY DEMAND that you pay the sum of S$70,000.00 to our client
    within 7 calendar days from the date of this letter, to:
    Bank: Assured Reliance Bank; Account No.: 001-7777-666;
    Account Name: Yellow Flame Kindergarten Pte Ltd.
6.  TAKE NOTICE that if you fail to do so, our instructions are to commence
    legal proceedings against you without further reference to you, in
    which event our client will also seek interest and costs.
7.  In the meantime, all our client's rights remain fully reserved.

XTRA LEGAL LLC

```

Marker's notes:

- **Open letter.** The settlement offer goes in a separate WP letter (Q1-7). SILE's guide
  answer does the same (SQ p119–121).
- **Clause 19 is the key term.** Quote it to shut out the landlord's after-the-event
  complaint (the 6:53pm WhatsApp).
- **The demand stops at S$70,000.** No inconvenience damages, no S$400 letter costs, no
  interest now (Q1-6). The guide answer says interest and costs will be sought *if
  proceedings are commenced* (SQ p120 ¶7).
- **Leave out evidence:** the unanswered calls, and the regret at taking no photos.
- **Differs from the guide answer:** the guide uses "Dear Sirs" and "pay to our client or
  to us"; this answer addresses Mrs Noh as an individual. Both are acceptable.
  The guide's 7-day deadline is kept.

*Cite: SQ p49–55, p119–120; drp-w-1-4, drp-w-1-5, drp-w-1-8.*

**Q1-5** · `1.II.1`, `1.II.2` · SN3 ¶12–15 (course exercise) · 25 minutes · `drp-w-1-4`, `drp-w-1-5`

*Sub-point 1.II.1: letter of demand (debt for goods sold).*

**Facts (SN3 ¶12–15, Annexes A1–A2):**

- You are a first-year associate at Lawttery Chambers LLC, acting for Preenters Suppliers
  Pte Ltd (PSPL), which supplies printing inks.
- Publishers Plattfom Pte Ltd (PPPL), 123 Slow Street #01-02 Dilatory Centre, Singapore
  999123, ordered 96 cartons of ink through PSPL's online ordering form on 3 June 2024.

  14 days; payment is due within 14 days; PSPL "ha[s] the right to seek interest" if the

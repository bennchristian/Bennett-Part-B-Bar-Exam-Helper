# PCP-W Templates — drafting tasks

Two documents the PCP Paper 1 papers have asked candidates to draft, or that a question's
answer is built around. Drafting was set only in the separate 2021 and 2022 papers (Statement of
Particulars for a divorce in both; a short brief to a partner on a qualified plea in 2021); the
2023 and 2025 papers set none. These skeletons are reference only: the I-CRUPAC Pre-writes in
`prewrites.md` hold the law. Each block says what each part must contain and the common mistakes.

## 0.IV Plea in mitigation

### pcp-w-0-44 — Written plea in mitigation: skeleton and part notes
*Keywords: plea in mitigation, mitigation plea, written mitigation, skeleton, structure, personal background, antecedents, remorse, testimonials, medical report, psychiatric report, sentencing precedents, sentence sought, do not qualify the plea, PCR r 14(7), brief to partner*

**When used:** after conviction on a plea of guilty, following the Prosecution's address on
sentence (s 228(1)–(3) CPC); also the frame for a "brief to your partner" on what the client
can and cannot say in mitigation (PYP21-CRI Q2.2).

**Skeleton:**

1. **Heading and introduction.** Case number, "Plea in Mitigation on behalf of [Accused]"; the
   charges proceeded with, those taken into consideration, and that the accused pleaded guilty
   and admitted the SOF without qualification.
2. **Personal background.** Age, family, education, employment, health; dependants (elderly
   parents, children); any personal circumstances (bereavement).
3. **Antecedents.** Say nothing inconsistent with the record (PCR r 14(8)); never "first
   offender" if there is any conviction. A clean record is neutral, not mitigating (*Chew Soo
   Chun v PP* [2016] 2 SLR 78 at [30]).
4. **Circumstances of the offence.** Within the four corners of the SOF: no premeditation, low
   value, restitution, brief duration. Nothing that denies an element (s 228(4)).
5. **Mitigating factors.** Early plea and genuine remorse; cooperation with the police;
   restitution; youth; good character (testimonials annexed); mental condition with its
   contributory link (report annexed; if disputed, a Newton hearing, 0-15).
6. **Sentencing principles and precedents.** Which principle dominates (rehabilitation for the
   young or mentally ill; 0-34); precedents distinguished by the absence of aggravating
   factors; for multiple charges, the one-transaction rule, s 307(1) and totality (0-36).
7. **Sentence sought.** A specific sentence and why (e.g. a fine; a short detention order; the
   lowest term; concurrent sentences); requests for backdating or deferment (0-37).
8. **Annexes.** Testimonials, medical or psychiatric reports, proof of restitution.

**Common mistakes:**

- Qualifying the plea: "I forgot to pay", "it was an accident", "I didn't know it was a bottle"
  — the court must reject the plea (s 228(4); *Md Rafiqul Islam Abdul Aziz v PP* [2017] 3 SLR
  619 at [27]) (0-13).
- Asserting facts outside the SOF without evidence; if the Prosecution objects, withdraw, prove
  or call evidence (*PP v Aniza bte Essa* [2009] 3 SLR(R) 327 at [61]).
- Scandalous or vilifying allegations, e.g. blaming the victim (PCR r 14(7)).
- Describing a client with antecedents as of clean record (r 14(8)).
- Asking for a sentence the offence does not allow (check mandatory minimums and caning).

*Source note: CL3 s4, s10–17, s26–27 (s 228(1)–(5); Md Rafiqul [27]; Aniza [61]; r 14(2), (7), (8)); CL6 s9, s14, s19–22 (Chew Soo Chun [30]; principles; s 307; totality); Cyril-Exam §2.3, §7.2 (background details); Nic-Crim 5.7 (mitigation content — tertiary); PYP21-CRI Q2.2 (brief to partner).*

## 1.II Divorce application

### pcp-w-1-42 — Divorce application: brief particulars (Statement of Particulars) — skeleton and part notes
*Keywords: Statement of Particulars, brief particulars, divorce application, Form 2, originating application for divorce, particulars of adultery, particulars of behaviour, particulars of desertion, separation particulars, co-respondent, identity of the other person, dates and incidents, draft the Statement of Particulars*

**When used:** with the originating application for divorce (Form 2 under the FJ(G)R 2024;
formerly the Writ and Statement of Particulars under the FJR 2014), to set out the facts relied
on to prove the s 95A(1) fact. Set as a drafting task in 2021 and 2022 ("content, not
formatting").

**Skeleton** (the course slides give only that the Form 2 application states the s 95A fact,
brief particulars and the ancillary claims (FL9 s8); the layout below is a working layout, not
the prescribed form):

1. **Title.** In the Family Justice Courts; the application number; [Applicant] v [Respondent]
   (and [Co-respondent] where adultery is alleged and the person is identified). "Brief
   Particulars" / "Statement of Particulars".
2. **The fact relied on.** One sentence: "The marriage has irretrievably broken down. The
   Applicant relies on the fact that the Respondent has behaved in such a way that the Applicant
   cannot reasonably be expected to live with the Respondent (s 95A(1)(b) Women's Charter)."
3. **Particulars, numbered, chronological, specific.** Dates (or approximate periods), places,
   what was done or said, effect on the Applicant; most serious and recent first or in date
   order. By fact:
   - *Adultery (s 95A(1)(a)):* that the Respondent had consensual sexual intercourse with
     [named person, if known] on or about [dates] at [place]; how it came to light (e.g. a
     private investigator's report); that the Applicant finds it intolerable to live with the
     Respondent; when the Applicant learnt of it (watch the 6-month rule, s 95A(2)) (1-12).
   - *Behaviour (s 95A(1)(b)):* each incident — violence (date, injury, medical treatment, police
     report, PPO), verbal abuse, financial irresponsibility (withdrawals, gambling, failure to
     maintain), improper association, neglect of the children; cumulative effect; last incident
     date (s 95A(3)) (1-13).
   - *Desertion (s 95A(1)(c)):* date the Respondent left, without consent, intending to end the
     marriage, and the continuous 2-year period (1-14).
   - *Separation (s 95A(1)(d)–(e)):* date of separation; separate households (if under one roof,
     separate rooms, meals, finances); the 3 or 4 years; consent (1-15).
   - *Mutual agreement (s 95A(1)(f)):* the written agreement's reasons, reconciliation efforts
     and arrangements (1-16).
4. **Children and ancillary relief.** Children of the marriage and their ages; the relief
   claimed (custody, care and control, access, maintenance, division) — stated briefly; the
   detail comes in the ancillary affidavit.

**Common mistakes:**

- Pleading fault the client cannot prove, or relying on adultery known for over 6 months of
  cohabitation (s 95A(2)).
- Vague allegations ("he was always abusive") without dates or incidents.
- Inflammatory language — inconsistent with therapeutic justice and the duties in PCR r 15A
  (1-1, 1-2).
- Relying on the Applicant's own conduct (s 95(2)).
- Treating the particulars as a way to win the ancillary matters: the fact relied on does not
  generally affect custody or division (1-12).

*Source note: FL9 s8–9 (Form 2; brief particulars; contested and uncontested tracks); FL3 s15–23 (s 95A facts); FL-CS1 s4 (writ now originating application); Cyril-Exam §3.3.1 (particulars of adultery: consensual intercourse and identity of the other person); PYP21-FAM Q2, PYP22-FAM Q2 (drafting tasks). Layout is a working skeleton, not the prescribed Form 2.*

# CLK-M — Model Questions by Category

Paper `clk-m`. Every CLK practice question in one place, sorted by Subject → Topic →
Sub-point Key (the Keys and Topic headings in `coverage.md`), so you can review one category
at a time. Built 4 Nov 2026.

**What's in it:**
- the 40 official sample questions [SampleQs p89–106, key p130–131];
- Drills 1–4 and mock-1 Part B (PP1–PP20) from `drills.md`, with their existing answer keys;
- new questions, so that every Sub-point with an end-point ID has at least one question.
  Extra new questions target the 4 Nov traps: specific / targeted / general correction
  orders, data intermediaries and who notifies the PDPC, legitimate interests vs consent for
  marketing, s 48O and *Reed v Bellingham*, the CII Code of Practice, the meaning of
  "computer", whether regulations are "law", and code vs law (Lessig).

**How to read a question:** the tag line gives the question number · Sub-point Key · source
(Official sample / Drill n / mock-1 / New) · the end-point block in `cheatsheets.md` that
answers it. The answer follows the options: why it is right, why each other option is
wrong, then the cite. Cites use the source key in `outline.md`. A `Conflict:` sentence notes
where sources disagree; the answer follows the Course Materials (or the official key, where
stated). `TODO: verify` marks a point the sources don't settle.

**How to use it:** cover the answer, answer the question, then check. Log every miss in
`weak-spots.md` under its Key. Don't use this file with any live quiz or assessment
(Handbook §7.11).

Questions from mock-1 Part A (the official samples) and mock-1 Part B are answered here, so
sit mock-1 **before** reviewing those categories.

## 0 · Data Protection & Cyber Regulation

### 0.I PDPA scope and personal data

**Q0-1** · `0.I.1` · Official sample DPCR Q1 · `clk-m-0-1`, `clk-m-0-3`

Which one of the following statements regarding the term "personal data" in the Personal Data Protection Act 2012 is FALSE?

A. Personal data can include information that is false or inaccurate.
B. Personal data of an individual can include the personal data of another individual.
C. Personal data only relates to information about living individuals.
D. Personal data only relates to information about individuals who can be identified from the data (on its own or in combination with other information).

**Answer: C.** Data about a person dead 10 years or less is still partly covered (disclosure provisions and s 24), so personal data is not limited to the living. A: personal data is data "whether true or not". D: it must identify an individual alone or with other information the organisation has or is likely to access. B: not the false statement (official key is C).
*Cite: PDPA p11 (s 2(1)), p14 (s 4(4)); DP-1 p17; SampleQs p89 Q1, key p130*

**Q0-2** · `0.I.2` · New · `clk-m-0-2`

Priya is a procurement manager at a supplier. Her buyer saves her name, job title, office phone number and work email in its contacts system, all supplied so the buyer can deal with her in her business role. Which is MOST ACCURATE?

A. These are personal data, so the buyer needs Priya's consent under Part 4 before it may use them.
B. They are business contact information, but the buyer is still bound by the Protection Obligation in Part 6.
C. They are business contact information not provided solely for personal purposes, so Parts 3 to 6A do not apply to them.
D. Business contact information is outside the PDPA only if it includes a home address.

**Answer: C.** Name, title, business phone, address and email, not provided solely for personal purposes, fall outside Parts 3 to 6A. A: no Part 4 consent is needed for such data. B: Part 6 is within Parts 3 to 6A, so it does not apply either. D: the exclusion turns on business details not given solely for personal purposes, not on a home address.
*Cite: PDPA p8 (s 2(1)), p14 (s 4(5))*

**Q0-3** · `0.I.3` · Drill 1 DP16 · `clk-m-0-3`

Which statement about personal data of deceased individuals is CORRECT?

A. The PDPA never applies to deceased individuals.
B. All PDPA obligations apply for 100 years after death.
C. For a person dead 10 years or less, only the disclosure provisions and the Protection Obligation apply.
D. Only the access and correction rights apply.

**Answer: C.** s 4(4): for someone dead 10 years or less, only the disclosure provisions and s 24 apply; for more than 10 years the Act does not apply. A: "never" is wrong. B: the 100-year figure concerns records, not all obligations. D: wrong set of provisions.
*Cite: PDPA p10 (s 2(1) individual), p14 (s 4(4))*

**Q0-4** · `0.I.4` · Drill 1 DP4 · `clk-m-0-4`, `clk-m-0-5`

Which of the following is NOT excluded from the PDPA's data protection obligations?

A. An individual acting in a personal or domestic capacity.
B. A company incorporated in Hong Kong that collects data from Singapore customers.
C. A public agency.
D. An employee acting in the course of employment.

**Answer: B.** A foreign company is an "organisation" whether or not formed in Singapore, so it is bound. A, D and C are the s 4(1) exclusions.
*Cite: PDPA p11 (s 2(1)), p13 (s 4(1)); Reed p21*

**Q0-5** · `0.I.5` · Official sample DPCR Q7 · `clk-m-0-5`

Which of the following organisations are required to comply with the data protection provisions under the Personal Data Protection Act 2012 relating to data protection?

I. Corporate entities established in Singapore.
II. Unincorporated associations.
III. Individuals acting as a partner in a partnership.
IV. Corporate entities established outside Singapore.

A. I and II.
B. III and IV
C. I, II and IV only.
D. All of the above.

**Answer: D.** An "organisation" is any individual, company, association or body of persons, whether or not formed or resident in Singapore. So all four are bound. A, B and C each leave out a bound category.
*Cite: PDPA p11 (s 2(1)); SampleQs p91 Q7, key p130*

**Q0-6** · `0.I.6` · Drill 1 DP3 · `clk-m-0-6`, `clk-m-0-39`

Which statement about a data intermediary (DI) processing personal data for X under a written contract is CORRECT?

A. The DI is subject only to the protection, retention and certain breach-related obligations; X has the same obligations as if it processed the data itself.
B. The DI is subject to all PDPA data protection obligations.
C. Once X appoints a DI, responsibility for protecting the data passes to the DI.
D. The DI must notify the PDPC directly of any notifiable data breach.

**Answer: A.** A DI owes only ss 24, 25, 26C(3)(a) and 26E; the controller is treated as if it processed the data itself. B: too wide. C: responsibility is not transferred (SingHealth). D: the DI tells the controller, which tells the PDPC.
*Cite: PDPA p13 (s 4(2)-(3)); DP-1 p18; SingH p7, p49. Conflict: DP-1 p18 also lists Part 6B, but s 4(2) excludes it; the statute is followed.*

**Q0-7** · `0.I.6` · New · `clk-m-0-6`

Nova Retail engages PayrollCo under a written contract to process its staff payroll data on Nova's behalf and only for Nova's purposes. Which obligations does PayrollCo, as data intermediary, owe under the PDPA?

A. All of the data protection obligations in Parts 3 to 6B, as if it were the controller.
B. Only ss 24, 25, 26C(3)(a) and 26E: the protection and retention obligations and certain breach-related duties.
C. Only the Protection Obligation in s 24, because retention limitation is the controller's duty alone.
D. None, because the contract makes Nova solely responsible.

**Answer: B.** A data intermediary under a written contract owes only ss 24, 25, 26C(3)(a) and 26E. A: far too wide. C: s 25 retention also applies to it. D: those four provisions bind it directly, whatever the contract says.
*Cite: PDPA p13 (s 4(2)-(3)); DP-1 p18. Conflict: DP-1 p18 also lists Part 6B; s 4(2) excludes it, and the statute is followed.*

**Q0-8** · `0.I.6` · New · `clk-m-0-6`, `clk-m-0-39`

CloudStore Pte Ltd, a cloud vendor, hosts the customer database of Brightmart Pte Ltd under a written processing contract. A weak configuration by CloudStore exposes the data. Brightmart says it is not liable because CloudStore was processing on its behalf. Which is MOST ACCURATE?

A. Brightmart is not relieved: it has the same obligations as if it processed the data itself, while CloudStore separately owes ss 24, 25, 26C(3)(a) and 26E.
B. Brightmart is relieved, because responsibility for protection passes to CloudStore once a data intermediary is appointed.
C. Only CloudStore can be penalised, because the breach occurred in its systems.
D. CloudStore owes no PDPA obligations, because its duties arise only under its contract.

**Answer: A.** The controller keeps the same obligations as if it processed the data itself; in SingHealth the controller was fined $250,000 and its intermediary IHiS $750,000. B and C: using a DI does not delegate the controller's duty away. D: a DI owes ss 24, 25, 26C(3)(a) and 26E directly.
*Cite: PDPA p13 (s 4(2)-(3)); DP-1 p18; SingH p7, p49; Nic-DP p7-8*

**Q0-9** · `0.I.7` · New · `clk-m-0-7`

Which of the following statements about the PDPA's relationship with contracts, other laws and PDPC guidelines is NOT correct?

A. Other written law prevails over the PDPA where the two are inconsistent.
B. PDPC advisory guidelines are not binding.
C. An organisation cannot excuse a breach of the PDPA by pointing to a contractual obligation it owes someone else.
D. An organisation contractually bound to give a business partner personal data may do so even where that breaches the PDPA.

**Answer: D.** A contractual obligation is no excuse for breaching the PDPA, so D is the incorrect statement. A: other written law prevails where inconsistent. B: advisory guidelines are not binding. C: correct, and it is the reverse of D.
*Cite: PDPA p14 (s 4(6)), p78 (s 49); DP-1 p13*

### 0.II Consent and legal bases

**Q0-10** · `0.II.1` · Official sample DPCR Q5 · `clk-m-0-8`

The Personal Data Protection Act 2012 ("PDPA") imposes a number of obligations relating to the collection, use and disclosure of personal data by organisations. In this regard, which of the following statements is FALSE?

A. An individual's consent is not required for the collection, use or disclosure of personal data for marketing purposes.
B. Organisations need not obtain an individual's consent to collect, use or disclose an individual's personal data if they are permitted or required by written law to collect, use or disclose the individual's personal data (as the case may be) without the individual's consent.
C. Organisations may obtain an individual's consent or deemed consent under the PDPA for the collection, use or disclosure of the individual's personal data.
D. An individual's consent is not required for an individual's collection, use or disclosure of personal data that is necessary for any purpose which is clearly in the individual's interests, subject to the requirements and conditions specified in the PDPA.

**Answer: A.** Legitimate interests, business improvement and deemed consent by notification cannot be used for marketing messages, so consent (or another basis) is needed; it is false that marketing never needs consent. B, C and D are true: written-law permission, consent and deemed consent, and the clearly-in-the-individual's-interests exception all exist.
*Cite: PDPA p98 (First Sch Pt 3 para 1(4)), p103-105 (Pt 5); PDPR p15 (reg 13); SampleQs p90 Q5, key p130*

**Q0-11** · `0.II.1` · New · `clk-m-0-8`, `clk-m-0-17`, `clk-m-0-19`, `clk-m-0-12`

Telco Pte Ltd wants to email its existing customers about a new mobile plan. Its compliance team proposes three bases: (I) legitimate interests, after assessing that its interests outweigh any adverse effect; (II) business improvement, because the offers are tailored using purchase history; (III) deemed consent by notification, with an opt-out period. Which is MOST ACCURATE?

A. Only I is available, because the assessment shows no adverse effect.
B. I and II are available, but III is not.
C. II and III are available, but I is not.
D. None of I, II or III can be used to send marketing messages; Telco needs consent (or another basis).

**Answer: D.** Legitimate interests, business improvement and deemed consent by notification are each barred for marketing messages. A: the assessment does not lift the bar for marketing. B and C: each wrongly keeps one of the three barred bases.
*Cite: PDPA p98 (First Sch Pt 3 para 1(4)), p103-105 (Pt 5); PDPR p15 (reg 13)*

**Q0-12** · `0.II.2` · New · `clk-m-0-9`

FitHub, a gym, tells prospective members that they cannot join unless they also tick a box consenting to third-party promotional calls. The calls are not needed to supply gym membership. Which is MOST ACCURATE?

A. The consent is valid, because the member voluntarily ticked the box.
B. The consent is invalid, because it was required beyond what is reasonable to supply the service.
C. The consent is valid if FitHub notifies the purposes within a week after collection.
D. The consent is invalid only if FitHub also gave false or misleading information.

**Answer: B.** Consent is invalid if required beyond what is reasonable to provide the product or service. A: ticking a box does not cure a bundled requirement. C: the s 20 purposes must be notified first, and late notice does not fix the bundling. D: either bundling beyond what is reasonable or deceptive practices suffices.
*Cite: PDPA p20-21 (ss 13-14)*

**Q0-13** · `0.II.3` · New · `clk-m-0-10`

Ravi gives his name and mobile number to a restaurant's host to reserve a table, and the restaurant passes them to the online booking platform it uses to hold the reservation. Which is MOST ACCURATE?

A. Ravi is deemed to consent to the collection, and the deemed consent extends to onward disclosure to the platform for that purpose.
B. Deemed consent covers collection only; the restaurant needs fresh consent to disclose to the platform.
C. Deemed consent arises only if Ravi signs a written form.
D. Deemed consent covers any later purpose the restaurant chooses, because Ravi volunteered the data.

**Answer: A.** Consent is deemed where the individual voluntarily provides data for a purpose and it is reasonable that they would; it extends to onward disclosure for that purpose. B: it covers onward disclosure. C: conduct is enough. D: it is tied to the purpose for which the data was given.
*Cite: PDPA p21 (s 15(1)-(2))*

**Q0-14** · `0.II.4` · New · `clk-m-0-11`

Mei Ling gives her passport details to a travel agent so it can book her a flight. The agent passes them to the airline to complete the booking, and also wants to use them to send her holiday-package offers. Which is MOST ACCURATE?

A. The disclosure to the airline needs Mei Ling's fresh consent, because deemed consent covers only the agent's own use.
B. Both the disclosure to the airline and the holiday-package offers are covered by deemed consent by contractual necessity.
C. Deemed consent covers disclosure to the airline as reasonably necessary to conclude or perform the contract, but not the unrelated holiday-package offers.
D. Deemed consent by contractual necessity covers collection only, not use or disclosure.

**Answer: C.** Data given with a view to a contract is deemed consented to for disclosure and use reasonably necessary to conclude or perform it, including onward to third parties. A: it extends to third parties such as the airline. B: the offers are not reasonably necessary to perform the booking. D: use and disclosure are covered.
*Cite: PDPA p21-23 (s 15(3)-(8))*

**Q0-15** · `0.II.5` · New · `clk-m-0-12`

An organisation wants to rely on deemed consent by notification (s 15A) to use customer data for a new, non-marketing purpose. Which of the following is NOT something it must do?

A. Assess that the use is not likely to have an adverse effect on the individuals.
B. Obtain each individual's express written consent during the opt-out period.
C. Notify the individuals of the purpose and give them a reasonable period to opt out.
D. Keep a record of its assessment.

**Answer: B.** Express consent is the very thing the basis replaces. A, C and D are the required steps: assess no likely adverse effect, notify with a reasonable opt-out period, and keep the assessment.
*Cite: PDPA p24-25 (s 15A); PDPR p15-16 (regs 13-14)*

**Q0-16** · `0.II.5` · New · `clk-m-0-12`, `clk-m-0-8`

Quickmart has assessed that using customers' data would have no likely adverse effect, notified customers of the purpose and given a reasonable opt-out period, and kept the assessment. It now wants to use the data to send promotional SMS messages. Which is MOST ACCURATE?

A. Deemed consent by notification is not available for marketing messages, so Quickmart needs consent (or another basis).
B. It may proceed, because every s 15A step has been completed.
C. It may proceed if it also lodges the assessment with the PDPC.
D. It may proceed only for customers who did not opt out and have bought within the past year.

**Answer: A.** Deemed consent by notification cannot be used for marketing messages, however carefully it is done. B: completing the steps does not lift the bar. C and D: no extra step unlocks it; the basis is simply unavailable for marketing.
*Cite: PDPA p24-25 (s 15A), p98 (First Sch Pt 3 para 1(4)), p103-105 (Pt 5); PDPR p15-16 (regs 13-14)*

**Q0-17** · `0.II.6` · New · `clk-m-0-13`

Aisha withdraws her consent to receive Fashionista's newsletter. Fashionista's terms say consent once given cannot be withdrawn, and it keeps sending the newsletter through its email agency. Which is MOST ACCURATE?

A. The term is binding, so Fashionista may continue.
B. Fashionista must stop, but its email agency may continue because it is a separate party.
C. Fashionista must stop at once and has no duty to explain the consequences of withdrawal.
D. Fashionista cannot prohibit withdrawal; it must explain the likely consequences, then stop and make its intermediaries and agents stop, unless the law requires otherwise.

**Answer: D.** An individual may withdraw on reasonable notice, and the organisation must explain the likely consequences, cannot prohibit withdrawal, and must stop and make its intermediaries and agents stop. A: withdrawal cannot be prohibited. B: agents must also stop. C: the explanation duty exists.
*Cite: PDPA p25-26 (s 16)*

**Q0-18** · `0.II.7` · Official sample DPCR Q8 · `clk-m-0-14`

Which of the following is not a basis (or category of legal bases) for collecting, using and disclosing personal data under the Personal Data Protection Act 2012?

A. Consent of the individual.
B. Notification of purposes.
C. Vital interests of individuals.
D. Legitimate interests of organisations.

**Answer: B.** Notifying purposes (s 20) is a separate obligation, not a legal basis. A, C and D are legal bases: consent, the vital interests exception and the legitimate interests exception.
*Cite: SampleQs p92 Q8, key p130; DP-1 p26; PDPA p95-96 (First Sch Pt 1), p98-100 (Pt 3)*

**Q0-19** · `0.II.8` · New · `clk-m-0-15`

Which of the following is NOT covered by the vital interests exception in Part 1 of the First Schedule?

A. Giving paramedics a collapsed attendee's known allergies where his life is threatened.
B. Contacting an injured traveller's next of kin.
C. Giving an insurer an individual's health data so it can send her a discounted-premium offer she would probably welcome.
D. Processing data that is clearly in the individual's interests where consent cannot be obtained in time, notifying her as soon as practicable.

**Answer: C.** A marketing-style offer is not an emergency, next-of-kin contact or clearly-in-the-interests case, so no vital interests exception applies. A: emergencies threatening life or health. B: next of kin. D: clearly in the individual's interests, with consent not obtainable in time.
*Cite: PDPA p95-96 (First Sch Pt 1)*

**Q0-20** · `0.II.9` · New · `clk-m-0-16`

Which of the following may be collected, used or disclosed without consent under Part 2 of the First Schedule (matters affecting the public)?

I. Personal data that is publicly available.
II. Personal data used by a news organisation solely for its news activity.
III. A retailer's customer purchase history, used to send promotions.

A. I only.
B. III only.
C. I and II only.
D. I, II and III.

**Answer: C.** Publicly available data and news activity by a news organisation are Part 2 exceptions. III is not: a retailer's promotional use is not in Part 2 (and marketing cannot ride on the consent-free bases). A omits II; B and D include III.
*Cite: PDPA p96-97 (First Sch Pt 2)*

**Q0-21** · `0.II.10` · Drill 1 DP5 · `clk-m-0-17`, `clk-m-0-8`

A Singapore bank wants to use customer transaction data to send customers personalised marketing messages, relying on the "legitimate interests" exception. Which is MOST ACCURATE?

C. Marketing needs no consent if the customer is an existing customer.

**Answer: B.** Legitimate interests do not cover sending marketing messages. A: true in general, but carved out for marketing. C: there is no existing-customer marketing exception. D: notification is not a legal basis (official key, SampleQs p92 Q8).

**Q0-22** · `0.II.10` · New · `clk-m-0-17`, `clk-m-0-12`

Orbit Logistics wants to use its drivers' vehicle-tracking data for a new, non-marketing purpose and relies on the legitimate interests exception in Part 3 of the First Schedule. Which is MOST ACCURATE?

A. Before processing, it must assess that its legitimate interests outweigh any adverse effect on the drivers, with mitigation, and give them reasonable access to information about it.
B. It must instead notify the drivers and give a reasonable opt-out period, as under deemed consent by notification.
C. It needs no assessment, because legitimate interests is a category of legal basis and consent is deemed.
D. It must obtain the PDPC's approval before relying on the exception.

**Answer: A.** The organisation's legitimate interests must outweigh any adverse effect, after a prior assessment with mitigation, with reasonable access to information for the individual. B: that is the separate s 15A basis. C: the assessment is required. D: no PDPC approval is part of the test.
*Cite: PDPA p98-100 (First Sch Pt 3); PDPR p16-17 (reg 15)*

**Q0-23** · `0.II.11` · New · `clk-m-0-18`

Alpha Bank is negotiating to acquire Beta Finance, and Beta gives Alpha its customer files to value the business. Which is MOST ACCURATE?

A. No consent is needed, but Alpha should receive only the data necessary for the deal and must destroy or return it if the deal fails.
B. No consent is needed, and Alpha may keep the files indefinitely even if the deal fails.
C. Consent from every customer is needed, because the data is disclosed to another organisation.
D. No consent is needed, but only after the transaction has completed.

**Answer: A.** Data may be processed without consent in a business asset transaction, subject to conditions such as limiting it to necessary data and destroying or returning it if the deal fails. B: the destroy-or-return condition applies. C: no consent is needed. D: the destroy-or-return condition for a failed deal shows the exception operates before completion.

**Q0-24** · `0.II.12` · New · `clk-m-0-19`, `clk-m-0-8`

RetailCo wants to analyse identifiable customer purchase histories to improve its product range. It also wants (a) to share the same analysis with a related corporation for the same improvement purpose, and (b) to use the analysis to send targeted promotional emails. Assume identifiable form is needed and a reasonable person would find the improvement uses appropriate. Which may it do without consent under the business improvement exception?

B. The analysis and the sharing with the related corporation, but not the promotional emails.
C. The analysis and the promotional emails, but not the sharing.

**Q0-25** · `0.II.13` · New · `clk-m-0-20`

A hospital wants to disclose patient data to a university for a research project. Which requirement applies to disclosure for research but NOT to use for research?

### 0.III Other organisational obligations

**Q0-26** · `0.III.1` · Official sample DPCR Q4 · `clk-m-0-21`

All organisations are required by the Personal Data Protection Act 2012 ("PDPA") to implement various measures to ensure proper accountability for personal data in their possession or under their control. Which one of the following is NOT an express requirement of the PDPA?

A. To designate one or more individuals to be responsible for ensuring that the organisation complies with the PDPA.
B. To develop and implement policies and practices that are necessary for the organisation to meet its obligations under the PDPA.
C. To develop a process to receive and respond to complaints that may arise with respect to its application of the PDPA.
D. To develop a data inventory and conduct a data protection impact assessment to assess risks relating to the organisation's collection, use and disclosure of personal data.

**Answer: D.** A data inventory or DPIA is not an express requirement (guidance lists an inventory only as a measure that may be taken). A, B and C are express: designate a DPO, develop policies and practices, and set up a complaints process.
*Cite: PDPA p19-20 (ss 11-12); SampleQs p90 Q4, key p130; DP-1 p28*

**Q0-27** · `0.III.2` · New · `clk-m-0-22`

A fitness app's notice tells users it will use their heart-rate data "for any purpose it sees fit". Which is MOST ACCURATE under the purpose limitation obligation (s 18)?

A. The use is permitted, because users were told.
B. The use is not permitted, because purposes must be ones a reasonable person would consider appropriate and ones the individual has been informed of.
C. The use is not permitted only because the app did not obtain written consent.
D. The use is permitted, because s 18 asks only whether a reasonable person would find the purpose appropriate, not whether individuals were told.

**Answer: B.** Data may be collected, used or disclosed only for purposes a reasonable person would consider appropriate and that the individual has been informed of; an open-ended "any purpose" fails the first limb. A: being told is not enough. C: s 18 turns on appropriateness and notice, not written consent. D: informing the individual is part of the test.
*Cite: PDPA p27 (s 18); DP-1 p26*

**Q0-28** · `0.III.3` · New · `clk-m-0-23`

Which statement about the notification obligation (s 20) is FALSE?

A. Purposes must be notified on or before collection.
B. A new purpose must be notified before the data is used for it.
C. The organisation must give a contact for questions on request.
D. Notification is never required when personal data is processed for employment purposes.

**Answer: D.** Notification is not needed for deemed consent or s 17 exceptions, but it is still needed for employment purposes. A and B: timing rules. C: contact on request.
*Cite: PDPA p27-29 (s 20)*

**Q0-29** · `0.III.4` · mock-1 PP2 · `clk-m-0-24`

When must an organisation make a reasonable effort to ensure personal data is accurate and complete?

A. Always, for all personal data it holds.
B. Only when the individual requests it.
C. When the data is likely to be used to make a decision affecting the individual, or disclosed to another organisation.
D. Only for sensitive personal data.

**Answer: C.** s 23 requires the effort only if the data is likely to be used to make a decision affecting the individual or disclosed to another organisation. A: not for all data held. B: no request is needed. D: there is no sensitive-data limit.
*Cite: PDPA p32-33 (s 23)*

**Q0-30** · `0.III.5` · mock-1 PP7 · `clk-m-0-25`

A company holds personal data whose original purpose is no longer served, but it is still needed to defend a pending lawsuit. Under s 25 PDPA:

A. it must delete the data immediately.
B. it must transfer the data to the PDPC.
C. it must anonymise the data immediately.
D. it may keep the data, because retention is still necessary for legal purposes.

**Answer: D.** Retention must stop (or the data be anonymised) only once both the purpose is no longer served and retention is no longer necessary for legal or business purposes; the lawsuit keeps the second limb open. A and C: both limbs must be met first. B: there is no transfer to the PDPC.
*Cite: PDPA p33 (s 25)*
